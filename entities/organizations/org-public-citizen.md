# Public Citizen

Public Citizen is a Washington, DC-based national consumer advocacy organization [founded in 1971 by Ralph Nader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Citizen), with approximately 500,000 members and a mission to champion democratic accountability in government, consumer rights, and strong health, safety, and environmental protections. Since 2023 it has operated one of the most substantive AI political-accountability programs in the US civil society landscape, producing original investigative research on AI industry lobbying and campaign spending, distributing model state legislation to lawmakers, and anchoring a major coalition campaign against federal preemption of state AI consumer protection laws.

## Founding and congressional watch infrastructure

Public Citizen was established in 1971 as a counterweight to corporate lobbying power in Washington. Its organizational architecture — six specialized divisions including Congress Watch (focused on money-in-politics, trade, and government accountability), the Health Research Group, the Litigation Group, and the Energy Program — reflects Nader's design: durable consumer protection requires legislative advocacy, independent research, and litigation working in coordination. The Congress Watch division, which houses the AI advocacy program, has decades of experience monitoring campaign finance disclosures, lobbying registration filings, and regulatory capture dynamics; it brought that existing surveillance infrastructure directly to AI policy as the industry's political spending scaled rapidly after 2022. The organization is led by co-presidents Robert Weissman and Lisa Gilbert.

## Mapping the AI lobbying machine

The core of Public Citizen's AI program is original investigative research into AI industry lobbying and campaign spending, converting opaque federal disclosure filings into publicly navigable accountability tools. Its [February 2026 "Generative Influence" report](https://www.citizen.org/article/generative-influence/) documented that 3,570 federal lobbyists — comprising 26% of all registered lobbyists in 2025 — reported working on AI issues, with the total AI lobbying population having grown by 168% since 2022 and data-center-specific lobbying by [500% over the same period](https://www.citizen.org/news/one-in-four-federal-lobbyists-now-work-on-ai/). Corporate clients constituted 82% of all AI lobbying activity versus 18% for civil society organizations including unions, universities, and advocacy groups — a five-to-one resource imbalance quantified through systematic analysis of public lobbying registration filings. Eleven large technology companies alone spent more than $105 million on federal lobbying in 2025 with AI issues saturating those engagements.

The companion financial investigation, ["$1.1 Billion in Big Tech Political Spending Fuels Attacks on State AI Laws"](https://www.citizen.org/news/1-1-billion-in-big-tech-political-spending-fuels-attacks-on-state-ai-laws/), documented at least $764.5 million in political spending by tech executives and investors during the 2024 election cycle, combined with over $226 million in corporate federal lobbying through the first three quarters of 2025, connecting this capital flow directly to efforts to preempt state-level AI consumer protection laws. The report found that 74% of this spending favored Republicans and that Elon Musk accounted for 46% of the total from named tech-sector donors. The documented subjects include executives from Meta, OpenAI, Amazon, Google, Palantir, and Andreessen Horowitz.

## Stop AI Preemption campaign

Public Citizen's investigative research anchors an active coalitional mobilization campaign. When Congress began moving legislation that would [block all state and local governments from enforcing any civil law or regulation governing artificial intelligence for 10 years](https://www.citizen.org/article/stop-ai-preemption-protect-the-public-from-corporate-harm/) — eliminating state consumer protections in healthcare, housing, education, law enforcement, and democratic participation — Public Citizen joined with [Americans for Responsible Innovation](../organizations/org-americans-for-responsible-innovation.md) and Accountable Tech to launch a coordinated grassroots campaign. Within its first two weeks the coalition gathered more than 25,000 petitions from members and voters opposing the preemption provision; a bipartisan group of state lawmakers representing states that had already enacted AI consumer legislation publicly condemned the federal effort.

The campaign's central argument links the financial exposure work directly to the preemption push: the same AI industry campaign spending documented in the lobbying reports is funding the political effort to strip states of consumer protection authority. Congress rejected the initial 10-year moratorium language in 2025 after opposition from governors, state attorneys general, and state legislators in both parties; subsequent attempts to attach preemption provisions to defense authorization legislation also failed.

## Model legislation for state lawmakers

The investigation and mobilization work is paired with a legislative tool-provision program. Public Citizen has published [model artificial intelligence legislation for state lawmakers](https://www.citizen.org/article/model-artificial-intelligence-legislation-for-state-lawmakers/) that provides draft bill language in four consumer protection areas: chatbot disclosure requirements making clear when consumers are interacting with an AI system; protection from non-consensual intimate deepfake abuse; fair and medically-reviewed AI healthcare coverage decisions; and transparency requirements governing AI use in election communications. The model bills are designed for direct adoption by state legislators as a movement strategy to maintain state-level consumer protection authority during the period when federal preemption campaigns are most active.
