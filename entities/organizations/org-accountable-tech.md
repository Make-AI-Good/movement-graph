# Accountable Tech

Accountable Tech was a Washington, DC-based nonprofit advocacy organization founded in 2020 to curb the societal harms of Big Tech's business practices — with a focus on surveillance advertising, AI-generated disinformation, and the integrity of democratic processes. The organization was co-founded by Nicole Gill, who served as executive director, and Jesse Lehrich, a former foreign policy spokesman for Hillary Clinton's 2016 presidential campaign whose experience managing responses to Russia's information warfare operation shaped the organization's early framing. The organization announced its [wind-down in August 2025](https://x.com/accountabletech/status/1956006916213141993), pivoting its resources and relationships to its youth-led partner, Design It For Us, a coalition advocating for safer social media and online platforms for young people.

## Core campaigns

### Ban Surveillance Advertising

The organization's flagship program aimed to outlaw what it termed "surveillance advertising" — the practice of tracking user behavior across platforms to micro-target political and commercial messaging. Accountable Tech is credited with [defining surveillance advertising as a phrase now used industry-wide](https://accountabletech.org/our-impact/). In April 2021, in coordination with Apple's launch of App Tracking Transparency, the organization launched the **Everyone Opt Out** campaign, reaching millions of grassroots supporters across its coalition. In September 2021, it submitted a sweeping [rulemaking petition to the FTC](https://accountabletech.org/campaign/ban-surveillance-advertising/) urging the agency to prohibit surveillance advertising as an unfair method of competition. Polling commissioned by the organization showed [81% of Americans support banning the practice](https://accountabletech.org/research/surveillance-advertising/). In January 2022, Representatives Anna Eshoo and Jan Schakowsky and Senator Cory Booker introduced the Banning Surveillance Advertising Act, which Accountable Tech had been organizing behind — a bill the organization celebrated as direct movement on the rulemaking pathway.

### No Deepfakes for Democracy

Ahead of the 2024 U.S. elections, Accountable Tech [led a coalition of 40 civil society organizations](https://accountabletech.org/statements/40-civil-society-orgs-demand-big-tech-take-action-to-prevent-deepfakes-from-harming-democracy/) demanding that social media companies take immediate action to detect, label, and remove political AI-generated deepfakes. The campaign — which it called **No Deepfakes for Democracy** — emerged after more than ten viral AI deepfakes related to the 2024 election reached an estimated 140 million viewers, including an AI-generated audio clip of Vice President Kamala Harris amplified by Elon Musk on X. The coalition's demands centered on mandatory disclosure from creators about the AI tools used to generate political content and platform-level detection and labeling requirements. The [staff-published analysis of the deepfakes threat](https://accountabletech.org/staff-post/the-rising-threat-of-deepfakes-safeguarding-truth-ahead-of-the-2024-elections/) documented the surveillance capacity the organization built to track these incidents in real time.

### AI policy advocacy

When Senate Majority Leader Schumer released an AI Legislative Roadmap that the organization considered to prioritize industry interests over public protections, Accountable Tech mounted a rapid response and [organized a Senate roundtable headlined by Senator Cory Booker](https://accountabletech.org/our-impact/) to elevate civil society voices in the process. This positioned the organization as a consistent presence in U.S. federal AI policy formation alongside its platform-accountability campaigns.

### Instagram Teen Accounts research

The organization [partnered with Design It For Us](https://accountabletech.org/our-impact/) — the youth-led coalition to which it later pivoted its resources — to test and [expose the limits of Instagram's "Teen Accounts" default protections](https://accountabletech.org/staff-post/). The research showed that the settings touted by Meta as protective fell significantly short of their stated safety function.

## Wind-down and legacy

On August 14, 2025, Accountable Tech announced it would cease operations, framing the decision as a deliberate handoff to a new generation: the youth-led Design It For Us coalition, which the organization had incubated and supported throughout its existence. The closure came after five years of campaigns that the organization credited with establishing surveillance advertising as a recognized policy problem, exposing specific AI-adjacent platform harms, and building durable coalitions across civil society.
