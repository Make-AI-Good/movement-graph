# Food & Water Watch

Food & Water Watch is a Washington, D.C.-based nonprofit advocacy organization that mobilizes a grassroots constituency of over two million supporters to challenge corporate control of food, water, and energy systems through research, organizing, and lobbying. Founded in 2005 by Wenonah Hauter, who had directed Public Citizen's Energy and Environment Program since 1997, it built a two-decade record of campaigns against fracking, water privatization, and industrial agriculture before becoming, in 2025–2026, the national anchor of the Stop Data Centers Coalition — the largest coordinated U.S. effort yet to impose a moratorium on hyperscale AI data center construction.

## Founding and structure

Hauter [left Public Citizen in 2005](https://www.foodandwaterwatch.org/20th-anniversary/timeline/) to found an organization willing to pursue bolder demands against the corporations that dominate food and water policy, including direct calls for bans that mainstream environmental advocacy groups typically avoided. The organization's structural choices reflect that posture: Food & Water Watch operates as a 501(c)(3) research and advocacy organization, and does not accept government or corporate donations, which it cites as the basis for its willingness to challenge industries that fund mainstream groups. Its political work runs through Food & Water Action (the 501(c)(4) arm that endorses candidates and runs ballot measure campaigns) and its legal work through Food & Water Justice (its in-house litigation department). By 2026 it had grown to more than 100 staff, with annual revenue of approximately $20.8 million (FY2025). The board is [chaired by Maude Barlow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_%26_Water_Watch), former UN Senior Advisor on Water.

## Core track record

Food & Water Watch's pre-2026 campaigns established the organizational model it brought to data centers: early adoption of a maximalist demand before mainstream policy had caught up, sustained multi-year campaigns at the state level, and use of research and earned media to shift the terms of debate toward the federal level. On fracking, it was the first national organization to call for a complete ban when most environmental groups still supported "safe fracking," eventually achieving statewide bans in New York (2014), Maryland (2017), and California (2024), and leading what it described as the largest anti-fracking march in U.S. history with 10,000+ participants at the 2016 Democratic National Convention. On water, it won the first major campaign against water privatization in Stockton, California, pressured the federal government to secure $50 billion in water infrastructure funding through the 2021 Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act, and worked state utility commissions during the COVID-19 pandemic to stop water shutoffs affecting 247 million Americans. On food safety, it was the first organization to publicly report high salmonella rates in U.S. chicken processing (2006) and pressured Starbucks to eliminate use of rBGH growth hormone in its dairy supply (2007). The through-line is a willingness to frame systemic corporate behavior as requiring prohibition rather than regulation, backed by multi-sector coalition-building to achieve it.

## Stop Data Centers Coalition

In November 2025, Food & Water Watch became the first major national organization to call for a full nationwide moratorium on new hyperscale data center construction, framing the AI-driven buildout as a replication of the extractive fossil-fuel model it had opposed for two decades — "tech colonization" transferring public electricity, water, and land to Big Tech interests without community consent or enforceable safeguards. In March 2026 it published a comprehensive report documenting the scale of the threat: data centers were claiming [97 percent of all new U.S. electricity demand](https://www.foodandwaterwatch.org/2026/03/04/comprehensive-report-lays-out-case-for-nationwide-halt-on-new-data-centers/), contributing to an 833 percent spike in wholesale power costs in some regions, a 43 percent increase in New York residential electricity rates between 2020 and 2025, and water depletion at the scale of entire small-city systems.

On [June 11, 2026](https://www.foodandwaterwatch.org/2026/06/11/500-groups-from-47-states-call-for-nationwide-ai-data-center-moratorium/), Food & Water Watch facilitated delivery of a congressional moratorium letter signed by more than 520 organizations from 48 states — health, faith, environmental, labor, and community groups — demanding passage of the Artificial Intelligence Data Center Moratorium Act introduced by Senator Bernie Sanders and Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez. The following day it launched the [Stop Data Centers Coalition](https://www.foodandwaterwatch.org/2026/06/12/stop-data-centers-coalition/) as the permanent national structure for coordinating grassroots moratorium campaigns at the local, state, and federal levels. The coalition's coordinating committee spans the environmental-justice, racial-justice, consumer, and civic sectors: Citizens Action Coalition, Green Latinos, [MediaJustice](../organizations/org-mediajustice.md), North Carolina Environmental Justice Network, Our Revolution, Pennsylvania Physicians for Social Responsibility, and Third Act.

## State moratorium victories

Food & Water Watch led the state-level campaign that produced the first statewide data center moratorium in the country. Starting in December 2025, it drafted model legislation alongside New York State Senator Liz Krueger. Over six months it [generated more than 12,000 constituent emails and 1,200 phone calls](https://www.foodandwaterwatch.org/2026/07/15/new-york-data-center-moratorium-victory/) to state legislators, bused more than 100 New Yorkers to Albany for rallies and lobby days, organized coalition letters from 150+ organizations and approximately 500 small business owners, and sustained pressure on Governor Hochul within 24 hours of the legislature's June 4, 2026 passage of a one-year moratorium bill. On July 14, 2026, Governor Kathy Hochul signed an executive order pausing construction of data centers 50 megawatts and above statewide — the first such statewide moratorium in the nation. Coalition partners pursued parallel victories: Citizens Action Coalition organized 27 groups in Indiana and stopped roughly a dozen specific proposals; the North Carolina Environmental Justice Network helped pass at least 17 local moratoria; and Pennsylvania advocates drove passage of a state bill providing a three-year pause on data center permitting.
