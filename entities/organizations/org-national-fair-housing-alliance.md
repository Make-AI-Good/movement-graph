# National Fair Housing Alliance

The National Fair Housing Alliance (NFHA) is a Washington, DC-based consortium of more than 220 nonprofit fair housing organizations, state and local civil rights agencies, and advocates across the United States — the only national civil rights organization dedicated solely to eliminating housing discrimination. Founded in 1988, NFHA has become the primary civil society force shaping how algorithmic systems in housing and lending are governed: it litigated to transform Facebook's housing ad platform, built the PPM algorithmic audit framework that federal standards bodies drew on, convenes the annual Responsible AI Symposium for civil rights leaders and technologists, and coordinates member organizations in civil rights enforcement of the Fair Housing Act against AI-enabled discrimination.

## Founding and consortium structure

NFHA was founded in 1988 as a federated consortium. The Washington, DC headquarters organization coordinates and amplifies more than 220 member organizations — nonprofit fair housing centers, state civil rights agencies, and individual advocates — spanning every U.S. region. Member organizations conduct local testing and investigations, provide direct services to housing seekers facing discrimination, and report trends that NFHA synthesizes into its annual Fair Housing Trends Reports. Lisa Rice leads NFHA as President and CEO, serving as its primary public voice before Congress, at federal regulatory agencies, and at international AI governance venues. Dr. Michael Akinwumi, a computer scientist the organization describes as one of the world's premier AI research scientists, directs NFHA's [Responsible AI Lab](https://nationalfairhousing.org/program/responsible-ai/) as Chief AI Officer.

## The Facebook lawsuit and 2019 settlement

In March 2018, NFHA and three member organizations — the Fair Housing Council of Greater San Antonio, the Fair Housing Justice Center of New York, and Housing Opportunities Project for Excellence in Miami — filed suit against Facebook alleging that its ad-targeting algorithms enabled housing advertisers to discriminate in violation of the Fair Housing Act. The complaint established that Facebook's system let advertisers exclude housing ads by race, ethnicity, gender, age, zip code, and other protected characteristics. On March 18, 2019, the parties settled: Facebook created a dedicated housing, employment, and credit advertising portal prohibiting targeting by race, ethnicity, color, national origin, gender, age, religion, family status, disability, or sexual orientation; eliminated zip-code targeting in favor of radius-based geographic limits; and restructured its Lookalike Audience feature to exclude protected-class proxies. The [settlement affected 210 million U.S. Facebook users](https://nationalfairhousing.org/national-fair-housing-alliance-settles-lawsuit-with-facebook-transforms-facebooks-ad-platform-impacting-millions-of-users/) and was described by NFHA as establishing "a new industry standard in Big Data and Tech concerning civil rights laws." It preceded and helped structure the June 2022 Department of Justice consent decree with Meta, which required Meta to develop a Variance Reduction System to address racial and other disparities in its housing-ad delivery algorithm.

## Responsible AI Lab and the PPM Framework

Following the Facebook settlement, NFHA formalized its algorithmic work through the Responsible AI Lab, led by Dr. Akinwumi. The Lab operates across five workstreams — tech equity, privacy, explainability, reliability, and controllability — each addressing a dimension of civil rights risk in housing-adjacent AI systems. Its primary technical contribution is the [Purpose, Process, and Monitoring (PPM) Framework](https://nationalfairhousing.org/the-national-fair-housing-alliance-releases-a-new-framework-for-auditing-algorithmic-systems-purpose-process-and-monitoring-ppm/), a life-cycle audit structure covering pre-development, development, and post-development monitoring phases. The PPM Framework specifies evaluation criteria for fairness, privacy risk, transparency, and accountability at each stage and has been applied in NFHA's partnership with fintech fairness firm FairPlay to test AI methodologies in mortgage lending. The Responsible AI Lab also contributed to two major federal AI standards artifacts: the White House Blueprint for an AI Bill of Rights and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, inserting civil rights requirements into both.

## Responsible AI Symposium

NFHA runs an annual Responsible AI Symposium, with its third edition held March 30–April 1, 2026 in Washington, D.C. under the theme "Zero Gap — Aligning AI with Civil and Human Rights." The Symposium convenes regulators, elected officials, civil rights leaders, technical researchers, and housing and lending professionals, functioning as a convening infrastructure for the algorithmic accountability movement's civil rights wing. Lisa Rice has also keynoted at international venues including the World Summit AI in Doha, extending NFHA's civil rights framing of AI governance to global policy circles.

## Congressional and regulatory engagement

Lisa Rice has testified before the Senate's bipartisan AI Forum and the Senate Banking Subcommittee on Housing, Transportation, and Community Development, [calling for mandatory algorithmic fairness audits and comprehensive responsible AI legislation](https://nationalfairhousing.org/lisa-rice-president-and-ceo-of-the-national-fair-housing-allianceto-call-on-congress-to-enact-comprehensive-responsible-ai-legislation/). NFHA's policy positions center on mandatory AI fairness audits for systems used in housing and lending decisions, federal transparency standards for algorithmic decision-making, and civil rights integration into AI governance at every regulatory and legislative level. The 2025 Fair Housing Trends Report documented that algorithms are creating new threats of digital redlining, steering, and bias in tenant screening and pricing — continuing the evidentiary foundation for NFHA's advocacy posture.
