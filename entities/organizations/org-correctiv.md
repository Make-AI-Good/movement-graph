# CORRECTIV

CORRECTIV is Germany's first nonprofit investigative newsroom, established in January 2014 by journalist David Schraven and [co-founder Jonathan Sachse](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/news/scoop-nonprofit-correctiv-sparked-huge-pro-democracy-protests-all-over-germany-how-did-they) through the charitable entity *Correctiv – Recherchen für die Gesellschaft gGmbH*. Begun with [a three-year €1 million annual grant from the Brost Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correctiv), the organization started publishing in July 2014 and has grown to over 100 staff across Essen and Berlin, a nationwide CORRECTIV.Lokal network of 1,700+ local reporters, and nearly 1 million social media followers. Its work rests on three pillars: investigative journalism exposing structural injustice and threats to democracy; media education through programs including Reporter4You and Reporterfabrik; and technology — encompassing the CrowdNewsroom platform, AI-assisted tools, and community-building software. All editorial content is free and ad-free. [Funding is roughly half from private donations and half from foundations and institutions](https://correctiv.org/en/about-us/faq/), with no single source permitted to exceed 50 percent of revenues — the structural guarantee of editorial independence.

## CrowdNewsroom — participatory investigations

Beginning in 2015, CORRECTIV [built the CrowdNewsroom platform](https://www.niemanlab.org/2017/05/investigative-outlet-correctiv-crowdsourced-data-collection-with-the-help-of-a-local-newsroom/) to enable readers and communities to contribute non-public data to investigations. For each project CORRECTIV partners with a regional newsroom — jointly promoting data collection, verifying submissions, and producing stories from the assembled dataset. Projects have covered the Hamburg rental market (readers uploaded lease agreements; approximately 15,000 apartments were analyzed, surfacing patterns in one of Germany's tightest housing markets), cancelled school classes in North Rhine-Westphalia (over 520 participants logged 3,552 cancellations, [demonstrating that the state government was misreporting the true figure](https://gijn.org/stories/crowdnewsroom-using-communities-to-assemble-non-public-data-sets/)), domestic violence, and doping-related medications in professional football. More than 4,000 people have contributed data across all CrowdNewsroom projects. The platform operationalizes the participatory-journalism model: readers become co-investigators, and the resulting dataset is one that neither the newsroom nor any individual contributor could have assembled alone.

## AI and disinformation work

CORRECTIV operates a dedicated fact-checking division, CORRECTIV.Faktencheck, that identifies and debunks false claims and half-truths circulating in German-language media daily. Its AI-specific work has deepened since 2022 through the **noFake** project — a collaboration with Ruhr University Bochum and Technical University Dortmund — that [combines crowdsourcing and artificial intelligence to identify and refute false reports](https://correctiv.org/en/newsroom-updates/2022/04/13/nofake-an-interdisciplinary-project-combatting-disinformation-with-human-and-artificial-intelligence/). The noFake system automates preliminary categorization and image-authentication analysis while keeping final verdicts with human fact-checkers; citizen participants are trained through workshops and work within the CORRECTIV.Faktenforum platform alongside professional journalists. CORRECTIV also participates in the Vera.AI project, a cross-European initiative enabling AI-powered monitoring of online disinformation landscapes at national and international scale.

CORRECTIV's investigative reporting on AI-generated disinformation has concentrated on electoral integrity. A 2025 investigation documented that a Russian disinformation operation erected approximately 100 fake websites before Germany's federal election, designed to discredit German politicians and suppress democratic participation. A parallel investigation revealed German political parties running paid social media advertisements spreading false claims and hate speech, reaching hundreds of thousands of voters unchecked as Meta simultaneously scaled back its independent fact-checking program in Germany.

## Major investigations

CORRECTIV's most consequential investigation, published on 10 January 2024, was ["Secret Plan Against Germany"](https://correctiv.org/en/latest-stories/2024/01/15/secret-plan-against-germany/) — an undercover account of a 25 November 2023 private meeting near Potsdam at which far-right extremists, high-ranking AfD politicians, and wealthy donors discussed plans for mass "remigration" of immigrants, including German citizens of foreign background. The 6.5-week investigation deployed an 11-person team using hidden cameras, telephoto surveillance from a rented sauna raft on the hotel's adjacent lake, and encrypted internal communications. The published article became the most-read in CORRECTIV's history, with coverage from the New York Times, the Guardian, and major international outlets. [Over 3 million Germans took to the streets across hundreds of cities in the weeks that followed](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/news/scoop-nonprofit-correctiv-sparked-huge-pro-democracy-protests-all-over-germany-how-did-they) — the largest sustained pro-democracy protest wave Germany had seen in years — and the AfD dropped approximately 5 percentage points in approval ratings within 10 weeks. The investigation forced a parliamentary debate on a formal AfD-party-ban process.

Earlier landmark investigations include the CumEx-Files — a cross-border collaboration with international media partners exposing a €55 billion European dividend-tax fraud scheme carried out by banks across multiple jurisdictions — and original reporting on the downing of [flight MH17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correctiv), which independently confirmed Russian military involvement before the Joint Investigation Team's findings were made public.

## Organization and status

CORRECTIV is structured as a *gGmbH* (charitable limited liability company under German law) and recognized as a public-benefit organization. In 2025 the Russian government designated CORRECTIV an "undesirable organization" — a formal legal status under Russian law that criminalizes cooperation by Russian nationals — [widely read as an indicator of the organization's impact on Russian disinformation exposure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correctiv). CORRECTIV is a member of the Global Investigative Journalism Network and has received numerous international journalism awards.
