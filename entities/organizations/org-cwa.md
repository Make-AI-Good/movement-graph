# Communications Workers of America

The Communications Workers of America (CWA) is the [largest communications and media labor union in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communications_Workers_of_America), representing approximately 700,000 members across the United States, Canada, and Puerto Rico. Founded in 1947 from the National Federation of Telephone Workers — itself organized in 1938 by telephone switchboard operators at AT&T and its regional Bell System subsidiaries — CWA has since expanded far beyond its telecommunications origins to cover workers across journalism, broadcasting, tech, airlines, manufacturing, public service, and healthcare. Headquartered at 501 3rd Street NW in Washington, DC, CWA is an AFL-CIO affiliate led by President Claude Cummings Jr. and Secretary-Treasurer Ameenah Salaam, organized into nine geographic districts with approximately 1,200 chartered local unions. Its relevance to the AI-good movement rests on three interlocking tracks: a union-wide AI bargaining and policy program coordinated at the national level; the [Alphabet Workers Union – CWA Local 9009](../organizations/org-alphabet-workers-union.md), its flagship tech-worker minority union organizing inside AI labs; and the [NewsGuild-CWA](../organizations/org-newsguild-cwa.md), its journalism affiliate that has become the field-defining labor force on AI in newsrooms.

## Founding and growth

The National Federation of Telephone Workers was formed in 1938 as an umbrella structure for independent telephone locals that had emerged during the New Deal organizing wave, chiefly at AT&T's Bell System. A losing 1947 AT&T strike catalyzed the federation's transformation into a single national industrial union, which adopted the Communications Workers of America name at its first convention in Miami in June 1947, with 200 delegates representing 162,000 workers. CWA affiliated with the Congress of Industrial Organizations (CIO) in 1949, then with the AFL-CIO following the AFL-CIO merger in 1955. The 1983 AT&T divestiture — the forced breakup of the Bell System into AT&T and seven regional Baby Bells — represented the union's largest single structural shock, reducing its core employer base overnight; CWA's 1983 strike involved approximately 600,000 workers and became a landmark moment in US industrial-relations history. The union's subsequent growth came through diversification: mergers with the International Union of Electrical Workers (IUEW), the National Association of Broadcast Employees and Technicians (NABET), the International Typographical Union, and the Newspaper Guild (which later became the NewsGuild-CWA), expanding CWA's footprint across broadcasting, print journalism, and industrial manufacturing.

## Structure and major divisions

CWA's approximately 700,000 members span both private and public sectors, organized through affiliated divisions and sector-specific unions:

- **Association of Flight Attendants-CWA (AFA-CWA):** over 55,000 flight attendants at 20 US airlines — the largest flight-attendant union in the world
- **[NewsGuild-CWA](../organizations/org-newsguild-cwa.md):** approximately 27,000 journalists, editors, photographers, and news workers across US, Canada, and Puerto Rico — the journalism sector's primary union, running the AI bargaining campaign across 58+ contracts
- **NABET-CWA (National Association of Broadcast Employees and Technicians):** approximately 10,000 broadcasting and cable technicians, primarily at ABC (Disney), NBC (Comcast/NBCUniversal), and cable operators
- **IUE-CWA (International Union of Electrical Workers):** more than 45,000 manufacturing workers in electrical and electronics production
- **Public, Healthcare and Education Workers sector:** exceeding 140,000 public-sector, healthcare, and higher-education workers across state and local governments, universities, and hospital systems

CWA's tech-worker footprint is coordinated through **CODE-CWA** (Campaign to Organize Digital Employees), launched in 2020 as the union's formal infrastructure for organizing technology and video-game workers. CODE-CWA's most prominent affiliate is the [Alphabet Workers Union – CWA Local 9009](../organizations/org-alphabet-workers-union.md), formed in January 2021 to organize Google/Alphabet workers and contractors across the US and Canada around AI ethics, Project Nimbus, and workplace governance. Other CODE-CWA-affiliated units include the United Videogame Workers-CWA (formed March 2025) and bargaining units at ZeniMax (acquired by Microsoft) and Vodeo Games.

## National AI program

CWA established a **National Advisory Committee on Artificial Intelligence** in 2023, composed of frontline CWA members and staff from across the union's diverse sectors — call centers, telecommunications, journalism, airlines, tech, and public service — charged with studying AI deployment in represented workplaces, developing union-wide bargaining principles, and issuing recommendations for empowering members facing AI-driven changes. The committee's findings from member surveys documented a recurring pattern: call center workers reported that [many AI monitoring and workflow tools increase stress and reduce job satisfaction](https://cwa-union.org/workers-rights/artificial-intelligence) while some augmentative tools improve roles; NewsGuild members reported deep concern about automation-driven job displacement and determination to negotiate enforceable protections.

On December 7, 2023, CWA released its [**CWA Principles on Artificial Intelligence**](https://cwa-union.org/news/cwa-shapes-future-ai-work-new-principles-bargaining-policy) — the union's official policy framework directing bargaining committees across all sectors to negotiate protections against: invasive AI surveillance of workers; automated hiring, disciplinary, and pay-setting decisions; work intensification and speed-up through AI-driven performance management; reductions in compensation or benefits tied to automation; and job elimination without transition support. The principles also commit CWA to bargaining for economic benefit-sharing — ensuring workers see rising living standards when employer productivity gains follow from AI adoption. CWA's [AI Resource Hub](https://cwa-union.org/workers-rights/artificial-intelligence) provides member bargaining committees with a practical toolkit: a factsheet on employer AI deployment patterns, template information-request language for contract negotiation, an issue brief cataloging existing CWA contract AI provisions for reuse, guidance for legislative lobbying, and model state and federal legislation language.

## AI contract wins across sectors

CWA's AI bargaining programme has produced enforceable AI provisions across multiple sectors and employer relationships:

**Tech and video games:** CWA-affiliated ZeniMax workers — part of the Zenimax Workers United unit after the Microsoft acquisition — negotiated [a bilateral agreement with Microsoft defining collective principles on AI workplace implementation](https://cwa-union.org/news/its-your-contract-how-cwa-members-are-shaping-ai-through-power-union-contract), requiring Microsoft to provide advance notice to the union when AI deployment affects bargaining-unit work. The agreement is notable as a pioneering tech-sector AI contract provision from a major industry employer, establishing procedural consultation rights before AI is deployed against represented workers.

**Journalism:** [NewsGuild-CWA](../organizations/org-newsguild-cwa.md) has produced [58 collective bargaining agreements with AI-specific language](https://cwa-union.org/news/its-your-contract-how-cwa-members-are-shaping-ai-through-power-union-contract) across its member newsrooms — the widest AI bargaining footprint in the journalism sector. The December 2025 POLITICO arbitration victory, in which an arbitrator ruled that POLITICO violated its collective bargaining agreement by deploying Capitol AI Report-Builder and Live Summaries without the required 60-day notice and good-faith bargaining, is the watershed enforcement precedent for the journalism-worker AI campaign. The ruling led to permanent shutdown of both tools.

**Telecommunications:** Frontier Communications workers in California secured a three-year contract with [a joint labor-management structure for addressing AI implementation](https://cwa-union.org/news/its-your-contract-how-cwa-members-are-shaping-ai-through-power-union-contract) — establishing formal worker voice in decisions about AI tool deployment rather than accepting management's unilateral determinations. This is the telecommunications-sector complement to the tech and journalism AI provisions.

**Broadcasting:** NABET-CWA members at *Snap Judgment* (a public radio program) negotiated intellectual property protections including safeguards against the transfer of creative workers' output to AI training models — an early broadcasting-sector example of consent-based AI-training restrictions bargained at the local level.

## Position in the corpus

CWA sits in the corpus as the parent-union anchor for the corpus's two primary labor-AI bargaining affiliates — [NewsGuild-CWA](../organizations/org-newsguild-cwa.md) and [Alphabet Workers Union](../organizations/org-alphabet-workers-union.md) — and as the national institutional home of the union-wide AI bargaining and policy programme that gives those affiliates their organizational framework. While NewsGuild-CWA and AWU-CWA are the most operationally active AI-organizing units in the corpus, the cross-sectoral AI principles, the National Advisory Committee, the AI Resource Hub, and the sector-spanning AI contract record are CWA's own outputs — the parent-union work that makes the affiliate-level campaigns legible as instances of a coherent national organizing strategy. CWA also anchors the corpus's telecommunications-sector thread, where AI monitoring and automation of call center and network-maintenance work represent one of the highest-density AI-deployment environments in the US labor market but has not yet produced the same public-facing AI organizing as the journalism and tech sectors.
