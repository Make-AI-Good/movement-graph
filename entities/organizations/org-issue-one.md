# Issue One

Issue One is a Washington, DC-based nonpartisan democracy reform organization [formed in October 2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issue_One) through the merger of Americans for Campaign Reform and Fund for the Republic, and led by founder and CEO Nick Penniman. Anchored in campaign finance and money-in-politics reform, the organization has developed two programs central to AI political accountability: a systematic quarterly surveillance of Big Tech federal lobbying and campaign spending — now tracking AI companies including Anthropic, OpenAI, and Nvidia — and the [Council for Responsible Social Media](https://issueone.org/projects/council-for-responsible-social-media/), a crosspartisan body of 60+ former lawmakers, national security officials, pediatricians, and accountability experts that convenes public hearings engaging non-specialist audiences in social media and AI governance.

## Founding and institutional architecture

Issue One was assembled from two predecessor organizations that together span more than two decades of campaign finance advocacy. Americans for Campaign Reform, founded in 2003, focused on public funding of federal elections; the Fund for the Republic, established in 2012, targeted dark money and undisclosed political spending. Their 2014 merger created an organization combining structural reform advocacy with original investigative research on the money flows shaping federal policy. The resulting institutional architecture is built around formal coalitions: the ReFormers Caucus, launched in 2015, brought together more than 200 former members of Congress, Cabinet secretaries, and election officials from both parties as a bipartisan pressure base for political reform. This infrastructure — relationships with former lawmakers across decades of public service — was subsequently mobilized for technology governance as Big Tech's federal lobbying scaled rapidly after 2020.

## Mapping Big Tech's lobbying machine

Issue One's Money in Politics program has evolved into one of the most comprehensive surveillance operations tracking AI industry federal lobbying. Using systematic analysis of federally mandated lobbying disclosure filings, Issue One publishes quarterly and semi-annual reports documenting spending by major technology, social media, and AI companies. Its analysis of the [first three quarters of 2025](https://issueone.org/articles/big-tech-lobbying-2025-q3/) found seven major companies — Alphabet, ByteDance, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, Snap, and X — spent a combined $50 million on federal lobbying, averaging nearly $400,000 for every day Congress was in session; Q3 alone recorded $16 million, the highest single-quarter total these companies had collectively reached. Meta's $19.7 million represented a company record; OpenAI's $2.1 million marked a 68% increase from the same period in 2024; and Nvidia's lobbying registered a 388% year-over-year increase through H1 2025 as it sought a direct stake in AI regulatory outcomes.

The [H1 2026 analysis](https://issueone.org/press/big-tech-spends-millions-to-buy-influence-in-washington-in-first-half-of-2026/) expanded tracking to eleven companies and trade associations and found combined spending of $41 million — averaging more than $226,000 per day — an 8% increase from the same period in 2025. Anthropic spent $1.97 million in Q2 2026 alone, its highest quarterly total on record, joining Alphabet, Meta, Microsoft, Nvidia, and OpenAI in deploying 324 combined lobbyists — approximately one lobbyist for every 1.5 members of Congress. Issue One's Advocacy Vice President Alix Fraser framed the campaign explicitly in terms of AI policy stakes: Congress was simultaneously debating "the future role of artificial intelligence in society, whether we will protect children online, how to protect U.S. national security."

## Council for Responsible Social Media

Alongside the lobbying-tracking program, Issue One operates the [Council for Responsible Social Media](https://issueone.org/projects/council-for-responsible-social-media/), a crosspartisan body launched in October 2022 that functions as Issue One's engagement mechanism for convening non-specialist audiences in AI and technology governance debates. The Council draws together approximately 60 senior figures across five expert domains: former members of Congress from both parties, former Cabinet secretaries and agency heads, former CIA and NSA directors and national security officials, information integrity specialists including Facebook whistleblower Frances Haugen, and tech accountability advocates including Center for Humane Technology co-founder Tristan Harris. The Council is co-chaired by Dick Gephardt, the former Democratic House Majority Leader, and Kerry Healey, a former Republican Lieutenant Governor of Massachusetts.

The Council's distinctive function is its convening of public hearings that bring parents, pediatricians, child psychologists, and bipartisan lawmakers into structured accountability conversations about social media and AI platforms — deliberate engagement of non-specialist constituencies directly affected by algorithmic and AI-driven harm in making the policy case for federal safeguards. The Council endorsed the Kids Online Safety Act, citing polling that found nearly nine in ten US voters support its core provisions. A Council op-ed by former Secretary of Defense Chuck Hagel argued that the Trump administration's executive-order directive banning federal AI funds from states with restrictive AI laws constituted a national security threat, bringing the Council's national-security credibility directly to bear on AI governance debates.

## AI preemption opposition and legislative advocacy

Issue One has aligned with broad civil society opposition to federal legislation that would block state governments from enforcing consumer protection rules governing artificial intelligence. When Congress debated an AI preemption provision that would have imposed a decade-long moratorium on state AI regulation — moving through both the reconciliation process and defense authorization legislation — Issue One's advocacy program opposed the provision through its lobbying-tracking research and the Council's bipartisan public credibility. The preemption provision was excluded from final legislation in each of its legislative vehicles after opposition from state governors, attorneys general, and legislators from both parties documented its effects on existing state consumer protection law. Issue One has additionally endorsed the Youth AI Privacy Act as part of its broader technology reform agenda.
