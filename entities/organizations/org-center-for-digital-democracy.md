# Center for Digital Democracy

The Center for Digital Democracy (CDD) is a Washington, D.C.-based nonprofit research and advocacy organization whose mission is to ensure that digital technologies serve democratic values while safeguarding privacy, civil rights, and consumer protection. Founded in 1991 by Jeff Chester and Dr. Kathryn Montgomery as the Center for Media Education (CME), and relaunched under its current name in 2001, CDD has been the leading U.S. civil-society organization tracking how data-driven commercial practices — and increasingly, AI-powered advertising and behavioral profiling — harm consumers, children, and democratic processes. It operates at the intersection of children's digital rights, consumer protection, and algorithmic accountability, coordinating with over thirty-six civil-society groups and engaging the Federal Trade Commission, Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, and state attorneys general across a sustained regulatory advocacy strategy.

## Founding and history

The organizational lineage begins in 1991, when documentary filmmaker Jeff Chester and American University professor Kathryn Montgomery [co-founded the Center for Media Education](https://democraticmedia.org/about/history) to contest children's television advertising and expand public participation in telecommunications policy. CME's signal achievement was leading the campaign for the [Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA, 1998)](https://democraticmedia.org/about/history) — the U.S. statute requiring parental consent for online data collection from children under thirteen and the foundational federal privacy law protecting minors in the digital environment. CME also secured the E-rate fund supporting school and library internet access, successfully advocated for the FCC's three-hour educational programming rule for commercial television, and negotiated open-access safeguards during the AOL Time Warner merger. In 2001, the Stern Family Fund provided a "public interest pioneer" award that supported CME's reconstitution as the Center for Digital Democracy, repositioning the organization to address the emerging commercial internet marketplace. Today CDD is led by Jeff Chester as Executive Director, Dr. Katharina Kopp as Deputy Director and Director of Policy, and Dr. Kathryn Montgomery as Research Director and Senior Strategist. The board is chaired by Lauren-Glenn Davitian of the CCTV Center for Media and Democracy, with Nicholas Carter (2020 Vision Ventures) and Javier Ruiz Diaz (digital policy consultant) as additional directors.

## AI surveillance and the advertising industry

CDD's contemporary work centers on how artificial intelligence amplifies the surveillance-marketing infrastructure it has tracked since the 1990s. A [2024 report](https://democraticmedia.org/newsroom/press-releases) documented that the connected television and streaming industry conducts "vast surveillance of viewers" using AI-driven ad tactics — generating thousands of hypertargeted ad variations for individual viewers through real-time bidding systems that process behavioral, location, and health-interest data. In August 2026, CDD [published findings](https://democraticmedia.org/newsroom/press-releases) that nine major platforms are deploying generative AI to build an "unaccountable persuasion pipeline" targeting children — a set of targeting capabilities that CDD argues privacy law alone cannot address because the harm is structural to the advertising model, not a compliance gap. CDD's analysis has specifically focused on how AI-powered contextual advertising mimics behavioral targeting's manipulation effects while evading privacy-first regulatory framing, and on how generative AI enables Meta and other platforms to scale personalized commercial persuasion in ways that existing consent frameworks cannot contain.

## Regulatory strategy: FTC, CFPB, and state AGs

CDD's advocacy runs primarily through formal regulatory channels. On behalf of a coalition of eleven health, privacy, consumer protection, and child rights groups, it filed [COPPA modernization comments](https://democraticmedia.org/filings) at the FTC in March 2024 alongside the American Academy of Pediatrics and Fairplay, arguing that the original rule required 21st-century updates to address behavioral profiling and AI-driven targeting of minors. CDD and [EPIC](../organizations/org-epic.md) jointly filed comments with the California Privacy Protection Agency on commercial surveillance practices. CDD has also filed with the CFPB on data broker harms (March 2025) — specifically examining how identity graphs and connected TV surveillance systems expose consumers to financial harm — and submitted merger comments to the DOJ/FTC urging scrutiny of Big Tech data-asset accumulation as an anticompetitive surveillance infrastructure. In October 2025, CDD coordinated a coalition urging the FTC to halt Meta's plan to use AI chatbot conversation data for advertising, warning that approval would normalize surveillance-driven marketing across the industry. The August 2026 Meta settlement with state attorneys general prompted CDD to assess the agreement as reaching only surface features rather than the algorithmic engine driving harmful outcomes — a framing that signals CDD's push for structural remedies rather than consent-decree settlements that leave the underlying business model intact.

## Coalition coordination

CDD coordinates with a documented network of over thirty-six organizations — including [EPIC](../organizations/org-epic.md), Public Citizen, Common Sense Media, Fairplay, U.S. PIRG, ParentsTogether Action, the Consumer Federation of America, and the 5Rights Foundation — on regulatory filings, amicus briefs, and public accountability campaigns. Its regulatory submissions carry consistent coalition co-signatories, and its research feeds directly into legislative advocacy: COPPA 2.0, which [advanced in the U.S. Senate in March 2026](https://democraticmedia.org/newsroom/press-releases), would extend privacy protections to teenagers and limit targeted advertising to under-sixteen users, tracking the scope CDD has advocated for since the late 1990s. CDD's sustained institutional knowledge — decades of documented industry practice, regulatory relationships, and coalition infrastructure — positions it as one of the primary conveners of the U.S. consumer-protection civil-society response to AI-driven commercial surveillance.
