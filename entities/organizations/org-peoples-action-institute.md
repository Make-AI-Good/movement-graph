# People's Action Institute

People's Action Institute is the 501(c)(3) policy and research arm of **People's Action**, a national federation of [40 member-based grassroots power-building organizations across 30 states](https://peoplesactioninstitute.org/about-us/our-history/) with more than a million members. The two entities — People's Action (the 501(c)(4) political arm) and People's Action Institute — share leadership, operate as an integrated unit, and are jointly headquartered in Chicago, Illinois.

## Founding and history

People's Action [was formed in 2016](https://peoplesactioninstitute.org/about-us/our-history/) through the merger of nine state and local grassroots organizing networks, structured around four predecessor formations: National People's Action (NPA), the Alliance for a Just Society, USAction together with the Campaign for America's Future, and the Center for Health, Environment and Justice. The oldest stream traces to NPA, [founded in Chicago in 1972 by Gale Cincotta and Shel Trapp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Action) as a neighborhood advocacy organization. NPA's foundational campaigns drove passage of the Home Mortgage Disclosure Act (1975) and the Community Reinvestment Act (1977); the 2016 merger brought these lineages together under unified brand and infrastructure.

## AI accountability work

People's Action entered the AI-accountability terrain through two overlapping threads: a ratepayer-justice campaign targeting utility cost consequences of AI data center buildout, and co-organizing of the national "Dump Big Tech" protest wave in August 2026.

**United Ratepayers** is People's Action Institute's campaign coordinating 14 member organizations to organize utility customers in states where AI data center expansion is driving electricity rate increases. Actions have [disrupted the annual gathering of energy-industry executives](https://peoplesaction.org/utility-customers-disrupt-summit-of-utility-executives-over-rising-electricity-costs-and-utility-profits/) and packed public utility commission hearings demanding affordable, clean, reliable power — framing data-center-driven rate hikes as a community harm disproportionately falling on residential and low-income customers.

In August 2026, People's Action Institute [co-organized the "Dump Big Tech" campaign](https://www.techbrew.com/stories/dump-big-tech-protest-palantir-google) alongside Make the Road, [Food & Water Watch](../organizations/org-food-and-water-watch.md), and other national and local partners. The multi-city wave of coordinated actions targeted OpenAI, Palantir, and Google offices; data center construction sites in Oregon and West Virginia; and elected officials across at least five states during Congressional recess. Member organization PUSH Buffalo [participated in actions denouncing data center development in Buffalo, New York](https://peoplesaction.org/dump-big-tech-push-buffalo-denounces-data-center-development/). The [campaign](../campaigns/camp-dump-big-tech-2026.md)'s three shared demands were stricter AI regulation, an end to federal Big Tech partnerships — specifically targeting OpenAI's Pentagon surveillance contract and Palantir's federal data systems — and enforceable energy and water usage caps for data centers.

## Structure

The organization operates as a network model: People's Action Institute and People's Action share leadership and staff, with the 501(c)(3) / 501(c)(4) split determining which activities each entity conducts. [Sulma Arias](https://peoplesaction.org/about-us/sulma-arias-long/) serves as executive director of both organizations — the first Latina immigrant to lead them — succeeding an earlier co-executive director structure. Member organizations retain significant autonomy within the federation, including the ability to join sub-campaigns like United Ratepayers and to affiliate with multi-org coalitions like Dump Big Tech independently.

The federation is funded by a mix of foundation grants and member-based fundraising. Documented institutional funders include the [Ford Foundation](https://www.fordfoundation.org/work/our-grants/awarded-grants/grants-database/peoples-action-institute-117058/), the W.K. Kellogg Foundation, the Marguerite Casey Foundation, and the Foundation to Promote Open Society.
