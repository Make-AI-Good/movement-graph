# Coda Story

Coda Story is a New York-based nonprofit investigative newsroom [founded on January 18, 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coda_Media) by Natalia Antelava and Ilan Greenberg, covering the roots of global crises through a distinctive focus on how authoritarian governments and powerful institutions deploy technology against civil society. Its AI-related investigative work spans three original investigations: Chinese AI-enabled surveillance against the Uyghur minority, Pakistan's biometric ID system used to strip citizenship from ethnic and religious minorities, and European Union–funded surveillance technology supplied to North African border authorities. The Uyghur investigation [won the 2020 European Press Prize Distinguished Reporting Award](https://www.europeanpressprize.com/article/the-uyghur-women-fighting-chinas-surveillance-state/), establishing Coda Story as the primary English-language outlet for original investigative reporting on AI-enabled state surveillance outside the United States.

## Founding and structure

Natalia Antelava, formerly BBC's resident correspondent in the Caucasus, Central Asia, the Middle East, Washington DC, and India, co-founded Coda Story with Ilan Greenberg, a former staff reporter at The Wall Street Journal. The organization launched with a pilot project on LGBT rights in the former Soviet Union before expanding to cover authoritarian technology, disinformation, oligarchy, and related global crises. It is incorporated as Coda Media Inc., a US 501(c)(3) private operating foundation, with [offices in New York and Tbilisi, Georgia](https://codastory.com/about/). Antelava serves as CEO and editor-in-chief. The board includes Maria Ressa — co-founder of Rappler and 2021 Nobel Peace Prize laureate — and Nicholas Dawes, a Human Rights Watch veteran. Coda is a member of the Institute for Nonprofit News and the Global Investigative Journalism Network. In February 2019, [Coda launched its Authoritarian Technology channel](https://medium.com/coda-story/coda-story-launches-new-channel-covering-authoritarian-tech-b6d648047485), providing focused global reporting on how governments weaponize AI, facial recognition, and digital tracking against citizens. The [MacArthur Foundation provided $583,400 across four grants between 2023 and 2026](https://www.macfound.org/grantee/coda-media-inc-10115764/) supporting general operations and coverage of authoritarianism and technology; the Open Society Foundations is listed as a major supporter ($5,000+) since 2020.

## Uyghur surveillance investigation

Coda Story's most recognized AI investigation, [published in 2020](https://www.europeanpressprize.com/article/the-uyghur-women-fighting-chinas-surveillance-state/) under the title "The Uyghur Women Fighting China's Surveillance State," documents how China deployed AI-enabled systems against the Uyghur minority in Xinjiang province. Reporter Isobel Cockerell conducted fieldwork in Istanbul's Zeytinburnu neighborhood, interviewing Uyghur women who had escaped Xinjiang, including Miyesser Mijit (who witnessed the rollout of facial recognition), Amina Abduwayit (subjected to biometric collection including DNA, blood, and voice samples), Gulbahar Jalilova (detained fifteen months in internment camps), and Nurjamal Atawula (separated from her family since 2016). The investigation documented the role of iFlytek — a Chinese AI company [claiming a 70 percent share of China's speech recognition industry](https://www.europeanpressprize.com/article/the-uyghur-women-fighting-chinas-surveillance-state/) — in supplying voice recognition and voiceprint technology to Xinjiang police, enabling the Chinese state to build a comprehensive biometric surveillance file on every Uyghur. Facial recognition devices at city gates scanned faces and entered all information into police databases in real time. The European Press Prize jury described the investigation as "forensic and highly informative" in its documentation of AI surveillance as a tool of ethnic persecution, awarding Cockerell its Distinguished Reporting Award.

## Pakistan biometric ID investigation

A November 2021 investigation by Alizeh Kohari — Coda Story's inaugural Bruno Fellow — [documented how Pakistan's National Database and Registration Authority (NADRA)](https://www.codastory.com/authoritarian-tech/pakistan-biometric-identification-nadra/) weaponizes its centralized biometric identification system against ethnic minorities, women, and political dissidents. NADRA blocked approximately 660,000 national identity cards between September 2013 and October 2016; over 150,000 remained suspended as of March 2020. Pashtun people comprised 63 percent of blocked cards despite representing only 15 percent of Pakistan's population. The investigation documented through individual case studies how blocked IDs rendered citizens effectively stateless — excluded from state-subsidized medicines, food rations, and cell phone access — including the Imkaan Gymnastics Team (young athletes barred from competing outside Karachi despite Bengali-speaking families present in Pakistan for generations) and members of ethnic and religious minority communities across Karachi. Former senator Farhatullah Babar, digital rights researcher Nighat Dad, and Haris Gazdar of the Collective for Social Science Research were among the officials and researchers interviewed. NADRA did not respond to questions raised by the investigation. A high court ruling in the Hafiz Hamdullah case subsequently declared NADRA's card-blocking process illegal.

## EU migration surveillance investigation

A May 2023 investigation by Zach Campbell and Lorenzo D'Agostino [used Freedom of Information requests to the European Commission](https://www.codastory.com/authoritarian-tech/icmpd-eu-refugee-policy/) to expose how the International Centre for Migration Policy Development (ICMPD) — a Vienna-based non-governmental agency — received hundreds of millions of euros in EU contracts to supply surveillance technology and training to border authorities in Tunisia, Morocco, and other North African states. The arrangement conditioned the deployment of this technology on cooperation in preventing migration to Europe. The investigation identified a structural accountability gap: by routing surveillance assistance through ICMPD as an intermediary rather than through EU institutions directly, the arrangement bypassed European Parliament oversight of how EU funds and technology were being used. Documented consequences included Tunisian coast guard units deliberately stranding migrant boats at sea and leaving passengers adrift after stealing vessel motors. European Parliament member Özlem Demirel told the investigators that "the EU is breaking its own rules." The investigation extended Coda Story's AI surveillance reporting into the humanitarian context of algorithmically managed border enforcement, where surveillance technology directly determines who reaches safety and who is turned back.

## Role in the movement

Coda Story's Authoritarian Technology journalism treats AI surveillance as a global civil rights issue rather than a domestic tech-policy question. Its geographic remit — Eastern Europe, Central Asia, South Asia, the Middle East, and North Africa — covers the regions where AI surveillance systems are most consequentially deployed against marginalized communities, and least covered by the American-centred algorithmic accountability movement. Each of its three signature AI investigations documents surveillance systems deployed by governments against specific ethnic, religious, or migrant communities: the Uyghur investigation exposed AI biometric tools as instruments of cultural erasure; the Pakistan investigation exposed biometric ID infrastructure disproportionately stripping citizenship from minority populations; the EU border investigation exposed surveillance technology producing documented deaths in the enforcement of migration control. Coda Story extends the investigative journalism model of [ProPublica](../organizations/org-propublica.md), [The Markup](../organizations/org-the-markup.md), and [Rest of World](../organizations/org-rest-of-world.md) into the geographies and communities most at risk from AI-enabled state surveillance, with a sustained thematic focus that treats each investigation as part of a long-running accountability record rather than a discrete story.
