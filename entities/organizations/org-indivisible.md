# Indivisible

Indivisible is a national grassroots organization founded in December 2016 to build local chapter-based resistance to authoritarian governance and defend democratic institutions. Operating through more than 4,600 affiliated local groups across all 50 states, the organization has extended its democratic-accountability mandate into technology and AI — framing Big Tech's political spending, AI data center expansion, and AI-enabled surveillance as threats to democratic self-governance.

## Founding and history

Indivisible emerged from a 23-page handbook published online on [December 14, 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indivisible_(organization)) by former congressional staffers Ezra Levin and Leah Greenberg, titled *Indivisible: A Practical Guide for Resisting the Trump Agenda*. The guide went viral after Levin shared it on social media, amplified by Robert Reich and George Takei; within two months, more than 3,800 local groups had self-organized under the Indivisible name. Levin and Greenberg formally incorporated as a 501(c)(4) nonprofit in February 2017 and have served as co–Executive Directors since. The organization operates through three legal entities: Indivisible Project (501(c)(4)), Indivisible Civics (501(c)(3) charitable arm), and Indivisible Action (a PAC). Leah Greenberg and Ezra Levin were included in *Time* magazine's 100 Most Influential People of 2019.

A second edition of the [guide](https://indivisible.org/resource/guide/) — *Indivisible: A Practical Guide to Democracy on the Brink* — was released in 2025 in response to Trump's second term, centering opposition to Project 2025, leveraging Democratic power in blue states and cities, and protecting elections in swing states through 2026.

## AI and Big Tech accountability work

By 2026, Indivisible's democratic-accountability mandate had expanded into explicit technology accountability, as the national organization and chapters began treating Big Tech's political spending, AI data center construction, and AI-enabled surveillance as direct threats to democratic self-governance.

The national Indivisible platform promoted a [four-part webinar series, "What Is AI? Why Resist It?"](https://indivisible.org/events/what-is-ai-why-resist-it-a-four-part-webinar-series/) (July–August 2026), organized by the Northeast Los Angeles Alliance for Democracy chapter. Sessions engaged Indivisible members — non-AI-insider community members and activists — on AI job displacement, data center environmental harms, AI surveillance and incarceration, and AI disinformation. Workers from the Amazon Labor Union, National Nurses United, and the film industry participated as speakers, grounding AI accountability in labor and community impact.

[Indivisible Orange County co-organized](https://chapelboro.com/news/unc/unc-students-and-activists-protest-ai-focused-g20-event-at-the-carolina-inn) the September 2, 2026 protest at the G20 Innovation Ministerial at UNC Chapel Hill — a two-day forum attended by Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, and OpenAI CEO Sam Altman. The chapter joined primary organizer [Sunrise UNC](../organizations/org-sunrise-movement.md) and the Party for Socialism and Liberation; hundreds marched calling for stronger AI regulation, data center accountability, and democratic governance of AI. Laura Love of Indivisible Orange County stated: "It shows how wide-ranging the dissent is and the fear is about what AI and data centers are doing, not just to our society, but around the world."

Indivisible has explicitly framed AI data centers as community threats, citing air pollution, constant noise, water quality degradation, and higher electricity and water rates as direct harms. The organization situates major AI companies' political spending — including OpenAI's $140 million midterm super PAC and Palantir's AI-enabled mass deportation infrastructure — as drivers of undemocratic consolidation of power, situating AI accountability inside its core anti-authoritarian mandate.

## Structure and scale

Indivisible operates through a decentralized model: the national organization provides organizing tools, strategic guidance, and a national platform, while local groups act as autonomous chapters under the Indivisible brand. As of a 2019 analysis, [approximately 4,600 groups had affiliated](https://www.influencewatch.org/non-profit/the-indivisible-project-indivisible/), with the network continuing to grow through subsequent organizing surges including the June 2025 No Kings mobilization. Chapters participate in shared campaign frameworks while retaining local initiative — a model that enabled chapters in Los Angeles, Orange County, Denver, and central Illinois to enter the data center accountability space independently while the national organization amplified AI-resistance education. The national organization is headquartered in Washington, DC.
