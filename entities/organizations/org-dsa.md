# Democratic Socialists of America

The Democratic Socialists of America (DSA) is the [largest socialist organization in the United States](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Democratic-Socialists-of-America), with [over 120,000 members organized into 220 chapters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Socialists_of_America) across the country as of 2026. A member-led, big-tent organization on the left wing of the political spectrum, DSA pursues its goals through electoral politics, labor organizing, and direct action campaigns on issues spanning healthcare, housing, climate, and labor rights. Its entry into the corpus reflects a distinct AI program that has emerged since 2025 — a national Ecosocialism data center campaign, a platform explicitly naming AI as a worker surveillance and displacement tool, chapter-level organizing that has become central to the municipal data center resistance movement, and a youth wing that has staged direct actions at the offices of major AI companies.

## Founding and structure

DSA was [founded in 1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Socialists_of_America) through the merger of the Democratic Socialist Organizing Committee (DSOC) — established in 1973 by Michael Harrington, the American socialist author whose 1962 book *The Other America* shaped US anti-poverty policy — and the New American Movement, founded in 1971. Harrington served as DSA's first honorary chair. The organization remained relatively small for decades, with around 6,000 members in 2015, before experiencing a surge during and after Bernie Sanders' 2016 presidential campaign that drove membership above 50,000 by 2017 and continued climbing through the pandemic years to the 120,000+ range by 2026. DSA operates as a federated structure of semi-autonomous local chapters and working groups, with national direction provided by an elected National Political Committee; the [Young Democratic Socialists of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Socialists_of_America) (YDSA) is its campus-based youth wing, independently organized but affiliated nationally.

## National AI framing: Workers Deserve More

DSA's [2025–2026 national program "Workers Deserve More"](https://program.dsausa.org/), drafted by a member-elected committee between April and June 2026 and [formally launched on July 14 2026](https://democraticleft.dsausa.org/2026/07/17/dsa-launches-new-program/), is the organization's most explicit national statement on AI as a political question. The platform frames AI as an instrument of class power rather than a regulatory or technical problem: ["We're promised magical technology with AI, but in reality, the bosses lay us off and track our every move"](https://program.dsausa.org/) — a framing that places AI alongside data centers as targets of the same structural critique of corporate ownership and worker displacement. The platform names AI in the context of a broader demand for public ownership of the largest corporations and essential industries, a 32-hour workweek, and the unrestricted right to strike. This is not a policy paper or regulatory proposal but a member-facing political education document, and its AI language — [the first major US socialist platform to name AI explicitly as a corporate surveillance and displacement tool](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/322633/20260801/democratic-socialists-america-names-ai-corporate-surveillance-tool.htm) — functions as both a framing device for member organizing and a signal to aligned organizations about DSA's AI stance entering the 2026 midterm cycle.

## Ecosocialism Transition Committee and the data center campaign

DSA's most operationally significant AI-related program has run through its [Ecosocialism Transition Committee](https://democraticleft.dsausa.org/2026/07/14/how-dsa-organizes-against-data-centers/), which in 2026 convened a national organizing initiative around AI data centers and their ecological and community consequences. The committee [hosted a mass national call on June 21 2026](https://democraticleft.dsausa.org/2026/07/14/how-dsa-organizes-against-data-centers/) at which chapters from New York, Oregon, Arizona, Wisconsin, Michigan, Maryland, Georgia, and multiple other states shared their local campaigns. The shared frame across chapters is that AI data center construction constitutes a dual harm: concentrated utility cost increases passed to ratepayers without consent, and concentrated corporate extraction of local water and power resources in communities with little recourse. Multiple chapters have worked in alignment with the [Stop Data Centers Coalition](../campaigns/camp-stop-data-centers-coalition-2026.md) — the [Food & Water Watch](../organizations/org-food-and-water-watch.md)-led 520-organization coalition demanding a federal moratorium on hyperscale data center construction — and with state legislators on moratorium bills. In New York, [NYC-DSA-aligned State Senator Kristen Gonzalez's](https://socialists.nyc/working-groups/) office worked alongside the NYC-DSA Tech Action Working Group in crafting data center and AI legislation that contributed to New York's June 2026 statewide moratorium bill. [Portland DSA's campaign](https://portland.chapters.dsausa.org/the-data-centrists/), described by organizers as a political "buzzsaw" that made Governor Kotek's advisory-group response necessary, and [Seattle DSA's People's Forum on AI](https://seattledsa.org/2026/04/what-can-we-do-about-data-centers-a-i/) organizing series are the highest-profile chapter-level campaigns in this wave.

## NYC-DSA Tech Action Working Group

The [NYC-DSA Tech Action Working Group](https://techaction.nyc/) is a standing formation within New York City DSA developing what it describes as a socialist perspective on technology and strategies to resist Big Tech's hegemony in politics, economics, and culture. The working group has engaged directly on AI regulation, data center advocacy, and tech labor organizing, and [State Senator Kristen Gonzalez](https://socialists.nyc/working-groups/) — a former organizing committee member — has continued working with the group from the legislature to craft AI and data center regulation. The group's engagement extends to co-organizing on platform labor, joining the QuitGPT boycott advocacy infrastructure, and contributing to DSA's national framing of tech-sector power as a labor question.

## YDSA direct actions

The Young Democratic Socialists of America co-organized the [August 10 2026 occupation of OpenAI's DC lobbying office](../events/event-openai-dc-lobby-protest-2026-08-10.md) alongside the [Sunrise Movement](../organizations/org-sunrise-movement.md), mobilizing [30+ student activists who occupied the building for approximately two hours before 13 arrests](https://www.commondreams.org/news/ai-protest). Activists carried messages including "Stop Stealing Our Future" and "OpenAI Bought My Senator," directing their demands at OpenAI's reported $140+ million in 2026 midterm super PAC spending and its lobbying against AI regulation. The action was part of a broader [Dump Big Tech](https://www.commondreams.org/news/ai-protest) wave during the August 2026 congressional recess that included 20+ state actions targeting OpenAI, Palantir, and Google offices and data center construction sites. YDSA has also participated in [QuitGPT](../campaigns/camp-quitgpt-chatgpt-boycott-2026.md) training and mobilization, running campus-based organizing sessions on how to build coalitions against tech companies.

## Posture in the movement

DSA's role in the make-AI-good movement is distinctive: it is the only entity in the corpus that combines a mass national membership base (120,000+ members, 220 chapters) with an explicit AI-as-class-power framing and a multi-channel AI program spanning national platforms, chapter-level environmental campaigns, legislative engagement, and student direct action. Its Ecosocialism data center work has made it a significant node in the municipal and state-level data center resistance movement, and its YDSA wing's direct actions situate DSA alongside [Sunrise Movement](../organizations/org-sunrise-movement.md) and [Indivisible](../organizations/org-indivisible.md) in the protest and civil-disobedience layer of the movement. Its national platform's AI framing gives the [Tech Workers Coalition](../organizations/org-tech-workers-coalition.md) and [CWA](../organizations/org-cwa.md) a political-organizing counterpart whose mass-membership base can translate AI accountability into electoral and grassroots pressure at the scale those labor organizations cannot. The depth and coordination of DSA's chapter-level data center campaigns — running independently across a dozen states with shared framing — distinguishes it from single-campaign organizations and makes its chapter network a durable organizational substrate for AI accountability work across legislative cycles.
