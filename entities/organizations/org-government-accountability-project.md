# Government Accountability Project

The Government Accountability Project (GAP) is a Washington, DC–based nonprofit founded in 1977 as the United States' oldest and largest dedicated whistleblower protection and advocacy organization. It [promotes accountability](https://whistleblower.org/our-story/) through three channels: direct legal representation for whistleblowers facing retaliation; legislative campaigns to expand statutory protections; and public investigations that convert insider disclosures into accountability pressure. Since its founding GAP has [represented more than 8,000 clients](https://whistleblower.org/our-story/) across government, corporate, and national security sectors. In the AI governance landscape, GAP operates as the legal and statutory anchor of the AI whistleblower protection infrastructure — providing representation for AI-industry insiders and advocating for the statutory shields that make insider disclosure of AI safety concerns legally survivable.

## Founding and leadership

GAP was [established in 1977 by the Institute for Policy Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Accountability_Project) in the political aftermath of Watergate and Daniel Ellsberg's Pentagon Papers release, which demonstrated both the public value of insider disclosure and the severe personal cost of undertaking it without legal protection. The founding mandate was to build the institutional and legal infrastructure that future whistleblowers would require.

Louis Clark has served as Executive Director and CEO since 1978, and Thomas Devine as Legal Director since 1979 — a nearly five-decade partnership. [Devine](https://www.influencewatch.org/non-profit/government-accountability-project/) is a principal architect of the Whistleblower Protection Enhancement Act and the All Circuit Review Extension Act, two of the most significant expansions of federal whistleblower protection of the past two decades. Karen Gray serves as General Counsel; James Harden as CFO. GAP's [2024 revenue was $4.4 million](https://www.influencewatch.org/non-profit/government-accountability-project/) against expenses of $3.5 million, with a staff of 23. Major funders have included the Open Society Foundations, the Rockefeller Family Fund, the CS Fund, and the Democracy Fund.

## Litigation and representation

GAP's litigation program provides direct legal representation to whistleblowers facing retaliation across sectors. Notable cases include Rick Parks, who [exposed safety failures in the Three Mile Island nuclear cleanup to the Nuclear Regulatory Commission in 1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Accountability_Project); Thomas A. Drake, an NSA senior executive who challenged mass surveillance programs and [faced ten felony charges that were eventually dropped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Accountability_Project); Edward Snowden, whom GAP represented until 2015; and Eric Ben-Artzi, a Deutsche Bank employee who received an [$8 million SEC whistleblower award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Accountability_Project) after disclosing securities violations with GAP's support.

In October 2020, GAP launched the [Democracy Protection Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Accountability_Project) to support whistleblowers reporting alleged interference in the 2020 US presidential election, partnering with Public Citizen and Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington.

## Legislative campaigns

GAP's legislative program has shaped the US whistleblower protection landscape across administrations. Devine's advocacy produced the Whistleblower Protection Enhancement Act — closing a critical federal employee protection gap — and the All Circuit Review Extension Act, which expanded the federal courts where employees can challenge retaliation. GAP's [current priorities](https://whistleblower.org/legislative-initiatives/) include opposing Schedule F policies that would strip merit-based civil service protections, the Expanding Whistleblower Protections for Contractors Act covering $759 billion in annual federal contracting, the Whistleblower Protection Improvement Act granting civil service workers jury trial access, and a consolidated uniform whistleblower statute replacing the current patchwork of sector-specific laws.

## AI whistleblower advocacy

GAP's engagement with AI-specific whistleblower protection centers on statutory shield advocacy and coalition participation. On June 2, 2025, GAP was among the [22 organizations co-endorsing the AI Whistleblower Protection Act](https://www.grassley.senate.gov/news/news-releases/support-grows-for-ai-whistleblower-protection-act) introduced by Senators Chris Coons (D-Del.) and Chuck Grassley (R-Iowa) — a bill that counters the chilling effect of restrictive NDAs and non-disparagement clauses used by AI companies, extending explicit employment protections to AI employees who disclose violations of law or dangers to public safety, health, or national security. Co-endorsers included the National Whistleblower Center, Center for Democracy & Technology, Americans for Responsible Innovation, Center for Humane Technology, Demand Progress, The Tech Oversight Project, and [The Signals Network](https://www.coons.senate.gov/news/press-releases/senators-coons-grassley-introduce-ai-whistleblower-protection-act).

On July 30, 2025 — National Whistleblower Day — GAP co-signed [The AI Whistleblower Initiative](../organizations/org-ai-whistleblower-initiative.md)'s "Publish Your Policies" campaign, joining [more than 30 organizations and academic signatories](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/RPrkxFGmDvA5nPJdQ/call-on-ai-companies-publish-your-whistleblowing-policies) — including Transparency International, the Future of Life Institute, Stuart Russell (UC Berkeley), and Lawrence Lessig (Harvard Law School) — in calling on frontier AI companies to publish their internal whistleblowing policies. At launch, OpenAI was the sole frontier AI company with a published policy; Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Meta AI, xAI, and Mistral had not published one.

AIWI also lists GAP as a referral partner within its AI whistleblower [support network](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/the-ai-whistleblower-initiative-supporting-agi-insiders-when-it-matters-most-w-founder-karl-koch/) — routing AI insiders who complete AIWI's anonymous Third Opinion consultation to attorneys with specialized whistleblower law expertise. Devine's four decades of experience negotiating NDA carve-outs, equity-vesting retaliation claims, and statutory shield applications directly addresses the legal deterrent stack that AI-industry insiders face.
