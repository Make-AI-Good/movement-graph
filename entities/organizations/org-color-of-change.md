# Color of Change

Color of Change is the nation's largest online racial justice organization, a 501(c)(4) advocacy group [founded in 2005](https://colorofchange.org/blog/internal_press/color-of-change-nations-largest-online-racial-justice-organization-celebrates-15-years-of-groundbreaking-advocacy-reimagines-possibilities-for-the-world-ahead-of-november-elections/) by James Rucker and Van Jones in the wake of Hurricane Katrina to strengthen the political voice of Black Americans through digital organizing. With [over 7 million members](https://colorofchange.org/about/), the organization mobilizes its base against injustice in corporations, government, and media — applying sustained pressure through petition campaigns, advertiser accountability actions, and public policy advocacy. Its technology accountability program is among the most consequential in the grassroots AI-accountability movement: Color of Change co-led the Stop Hate for Profit campaign that pulled more than 1,200 advertisers from Facebook in 2020, forced a two-year civil rights audit of the platform's algorithmic systems, and produced two concrete structural commitments from Facebook on algorithmic racial bias.

## Founding and organizational structure

Rucker, a former co-founder of MoveOn.org, and Van Jones — later a CNN commentator and Obama administration adviser — built Color of Change around the email-list organizing model, targeting Black Internet users and community members as the primary constituency. The 501(c)(4) structure enables full political advocacy, with the affiliated Color of Change Education Fund (a 501(c)(3)) receiving foundation support for research and civic education work, including technology accountability programming funded by the [MacArthur Foundation](../funders/fund-macarthur-foundation.md), the [Ford Foundation](../funders/fund-ford-foundation.md), and the [Open Society Foundations](../funders/fund-open-society-foundations.md).

Rashad Robinson served as president from 2011 through 2024, during which the organization scaled its advertiser accountability and technology campaigns into national efforts with measurable corporate outcomes, including the Stop Hate for Profit campaign, a Black Tech Agenda for federal AI policy, and campaigns against facial recognition deployment in policing and public housing. [Nadine Smith](https://colorofchange.org/blog/internal_press/nationally-renowned-civil-rights-leader-joins-color-of-change-as-new-president-and-ceo/), founding executive director of Equality Florida — one of the longest-running LGBTQ civil rights organizations in the South — became president and CEO effective January 12, 2026, following a national search. Arisha Hatch serves as Vice President and Chief of Campaigns; board chair is Heather McGhee, author and democracy advocate.

## Stop Hate for Profit and the advertiser accountability campaign

[Stop Hate for Profit](https://www.cnbc.com/2020/07/01/facebook-frustrates-advertisers-as-boycott-over-hate-speech-kicks-off.html), launched in June 2020, was co-organized by Color of Change, [the Anti-Defamation League](org-anti-defamation-league.md), the NAACP, [Free Press](org-free-press.md), Common Sense Media, and Sleeping Giants following the death of George Floyd and a documented wave of algorithmic amplification of hate speech, voter suppression content, and militia organizing on Facebook's platform. The campaign asked advertisers to withdraw paid advertising from Facebook and its owned platforms for one month. More than 1,200 advertisers — including Unilever, Adidas, Ford, and Volkswagen — paused their spend, directly pressuring the platform's primary revenue source: advertising constituted approximately 98.5% of Facebook's 2019 revenue.

The campaign's demands were structured around concrete, brand-safety-shaped defects that advertisers could operationalize as withdrawal justifications: algorithmic amplification of specified harm categories, content adjacency failures (ads served against hate speech or militia content), and the absence of a civil rights accountability structure inside the platform. Stop Hate for Profit is the reference case named in the movement-strategy corpus for the advertiser-boycott approach: civil-society organisations mobilizing the advertiser stack — brands, agencies, media-buying desks — into coordinated spend withdrawal from platforms whose AI deployment or content-moderation decisions the coalition names as unacceptable. The Stop Hate for Profit action met the model's key requirement: it converted a movement-side complaint about algorithmic amplification into a procurement decision the platform's ad-sales team could not ignore.

Campaign outcomes included Facebook's public announcement of two structural commitments that had been standing demands of the civil rights coalition: the creation of a senior civil rights oversight role inside the company, and the establishment of a dedicated team to study algorithmic racial bias. Both were long-standing demands of Color of Change and coalition partners that Facebook had previously declined to act on.

## Facebook civil rights audit

Separately from Stop Hate for Profit, Color of Change was a steering committee member of the Change the Terms coalition alongside Muslim Advocates and MediaJustice, which had demanded an independent civil rights audit of Facebook beginning in 2018. Facebook agreed to commission the audit, conducted by former ACLU deputy director Laura Murphy. The [Civil Rights Audit Final Report](https://about.fb.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Civil-Rights-Audit-Final-Report.pdf), released July 8, 2020, delivered a comprehensive indictment of Facebook's algorithmic and policy decisions across voter suppression and voting information integrity, algorithmic amplification of hate speech and white nationalist content, advertising targeting and discrimination, and content moderation enforcement. Auditors characterized Facebook's policy choices as a "tremendous setback" that opened the door for political abuse, specifically criticizing the decision to leave content by President Trump untouched in multiple documented cases. The audit's findings on algorithmic amplification — the platform's recommendation and amplification systems as active vectors for hate and extremism, not merely passive distribution channels — grounded the Stop Hate for Profit demands in a document Facebook itself had commissioned.

## Black Tech Agenda and AI governance policy

Color of Change launched the Black Tech Agenda in 2022 as a comprehensive policy framework for making technology safe for Black communities and a roadmap for racial equity in tech governance. The agenda's six pillars span antitrust enforcement against Big Tech consolidation, privacy safeguards and data protection, algorithmic accountability in high-risk automated decision-making, universal broadband access, net neutrality, and addressing the disinformation and misinformation crisis. On [algorithmic accountability](https://colorofchange.org/resources/black-tech-agenda/), the framework demands mandatory transparency requirements when automated systems influence hiring, housing, lending, and criminal justice decisions — modeled on the Algorithmic Accountability Act — and calls for greater inclusion of underrepresented communities in AI regulatory processes.

In 2021, Color of Change submitted formal [comments to the National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://www.nist.gov/document/ai-rmf-rfi-comments-color-change) on the AI Risk Management Framework, urging developers to evaluate bias risks across the AI development cycle and citing documented facial recognition error-rate disparities — higher false-positive rates for darker-skinned individuals due to unrepresentative training data — as a model harm requiring mandatory pre-deployment assessment. That same year, Rashad Robinson testified before the House Energy and Commerce Committee's Subcommittee on Communications and Technology on algorithmic discrimination legislation, pressing for anti-discrimination safeguards as a precondition for AI deployment in domains already marked by racial bias: criminal justice, housing, and healthcare. Color of Change issued a public statement on the Biden administration's October 2023 AI Executive Order pressing for standards that explicitly address racial bias in high-stakes automated systems deployed in criminal justice and public benefits administration.

## Surveillance and facial recognition campaigns

Color of Change has mounted sustained opposition to facial recognition AI in policing and public housing. The organization [demanded a moratorium on facial recognition in federally subsidized public housing](https://colorofchange.org/press_release/color-of-change-ban-on-facial-recognition-surveillance-in-public-housing-necessary-to-prevent-jim-crow-era-discrimination/), framing the technology as a contemporary instrument of discriminatory surveillance — a Jim Crow-era policing tool applied through technically sophisticated means — given that facial recognition systems have documented substantially higher error rates for darker-skinned individuals, concentrating misidentification risk in the communities most reliant on public housing.

The organization led a [petition campaign](https://colorofchange.org/newmode-campaigns/join-color-of-change-in-demanding-ags-hold-clearview-accountable/) demanding that state Attorneys General hold Clearview AI accountable for scraping more than 50 billion images from social media and websites without consent to build a biometric surveillance database sold to law enforcement agencies nationwide. Clearview's model — bulk scraping public internet data to build training corpora and operational databases without the knowledge or consent of those in the data — was framed as a specific harm class requiring regulatory response: the construction of an AI-powered state surveillance infrastructure that disproportionately subjects Black communities to misidentification, false arrest risk, and unaccountable algorithmic policing. The campaign asked for coordinated multi-state enforcement through the state AG channel, a pressure avenue Color of Change has developed alongside its direct corporate advertiser accountability work.
