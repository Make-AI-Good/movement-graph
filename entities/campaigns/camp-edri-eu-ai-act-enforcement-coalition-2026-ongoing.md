# EDRi-coordinated civil-society coalition for EU AI Act enforcement (2025–ongoing)

Following the EU AI Act's entry into force on 1 August 2024, [European Digital Rights (EDRi)](../organizations/org-edri.md) coordinated the successor civil-society coalition to the 2021–2024 legislative campaign — this time oriented toward enforcement and defending against a deregulation agenda that emerged within months of adoption.

## Enforcement strand

GPAI obligations [applied from 2 August 2025](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/enforcement-of-chapter-v-under-the-ai-act/); the AI Office's full supervisory and enforcement powers (investigations, documentation requests, fines, recall orders) apply from 2 August 2026. On 9 July 2025, a coalition of [52 civil-society organisations, experts, and academics](https://edri.org/our-work/open-letter-european-commission-must-champion-the-ai-act-amidst-simplification-pressure/) — co-drafted by CDT Europe, BEUC, EDRi, and ECNL — wrote to the European Commission demanding that it implement and enforce the AI Act rather than reopen it under the forthcoming Digital Simplification package. On 23 September 2025, EDRi and [31 civil-society organisations](https://edri.org/our-work/open-letter-european-commission-member-states-keep-ai-act-national-implementation-on-track/) called on the Commission and Member States to keep national implementation on track, documenting that near-zero Member States had met the deadline to designate national competent authorities.

## AI Omnibus opposition

On 19 November 2025, the European Commission formally proposed the AI Omnibus as part of a broader Digital Omnibus package — framed as technical simplification of the AI Act but [assessed by civil society](https://edri.org/our-work/forthcoming-digital-omnibus-would-mark-point-of-no-return/) as the largest rollback of digital fundamental rights in EU history. Over 133 civil-society organisations and trade unions, including in a [letter co-signed with noyb and the Irish Council for Civil Liberties](https://noyb.eu/en/open-letter-digital-omnibus-brings-deregulation-not-simplification), urged the Commission to halt the proposals. On 15 April 2026, [41 organisations and experts](https://edri.org/our-work/open-letter-eu-lawmakers-must-safeguard-the-ai-act/) wrote to Members of the European Parliament calling for the AI Omnibus to be rejected on procedural and substantive grounds.

The AI Omnibus trilogue deal was reached on 7 May 2026. EDRi, [Access Now](../organizations/org-access-now.md), ECNL, and [Amnesty International](../organizations/org-amnesty-international.md) [called for Parliament and Council to reject it](https://edri.org/our-work/ai-omnibus-deal-eu-lawmakers-should-reject-a-rollback-of-ai-safeguards/), citing its delay of high-risk AI safeguards until December 2027 (and August 2028 for certain systems), reduction of public-database transparency obligations, and shift of machinery AI systems from the horizontal AI Act to weaker sectoral rules.
