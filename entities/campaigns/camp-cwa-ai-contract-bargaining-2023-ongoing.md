# CWA AI collective bargaining campaign (2023–ongoing)

The [Communications Workers of America](../organizations/org-cwa.md) launched its cross-sectoral AI collective bargaining campaign in 2023 through a member-led National Advisory Committee on Artificial Intelligence, charged with studying AI deployment across CWA's represented workplaces — telecommunications, journalism, broadcasting, tech, gaming, and public service — and producing the union's first AI-specific bargaining framework. On December 7, 2023, CWA released the **CWA Principles on Artificial Intelligence**, [directing bargaining committees across all sectors](https://cwa-union.org/news/cwa-shapes-future-ai-work-new-principles-bargaining-policy) to negotiate protections against invasive AI surveillance, automated hiring and discipline, AI-driven work intensification, and job elimination without transition support, while pressing for economic benefit-sharing when employers gain productivity through AI adoption.

## First contract wins (December 2023)

In the same week as the AI Principles release, ZeniMax Workers United-CWA [reached a bilateral agreement with Microsoft](https://cwa-union.org/news/releases/zenimax-workers-united-cwa-collective-bargaining-yields-first-its-kind-tentative) over AI in the workplace — the first such agreement at a US tech major and the first in the video game industry. The agreement committed ZeniMax to human-centered AI implementation through six guiding principles and required Microsoft to provide advance notice to the union and bargain impacts when AI deployment affected bargaining-unit work. The agreement covered 376 members of ZeniMax Workers United-CWA.

Across [NewsGuild-CWA](../organizations/org-newsguild-cwa.md), the campaign produced 58 collective bargaining agreements with AI-specific language by the time of the union's AI contract wins report — the widest AI bargaining footprint in US journalism. Frontier Communications workers in California [secured a joint labor-management AI implementation committee](https://cwa-union.org/news/its-your-contract-how-cwa-members-are-shaping-ai-through-power-union-contract) establishing formal worker voice in AI tool deployment decisions. NABET-CWA members at *Snap Judgment* negotiated IP protections barring the use of their creative output for AI training.

## POLITICO arbitration victory (2025)

The campaign's most significant enforcement outcome arrived in November 2025, when an arbitrator [ruled that POLITICO violated its collective bargaining agreement](https://newsguild.org/politico-journalists-win-landmark-arbitration-on-ai-protections/) with the PEN Guild (a [NewsGuild-CWA](../organizations/org-newsguild-cwa.md) affiliate) by deploying Capitol AI Report-Builder — a branded policy-report tool for POLITICO Pro subscribers generating glaring factual errors without editorial review — and AI Live Summaries during the 2024 Democratic National Convention and Vice Presidential Debate, both without the required 60-day advance notice and good-faith bargaining. POLITICO permanently shut down both tools. The ruling is the first major US labor-arbitration outcome on AI's impact on journalists' work and established an enforcement precedent for the AI contract language the newsroom sector had been negotiating since 2022.

## Ongoing arc

By Spring 2026, CWA had [positioned union contract bargaining](https://cwa-union.org/news/future-ai-starts-us) as "the most effective way workers can set rules for the future of AI at work" — framing the campaign not as a defensive reaction to AI deployment but as the structural mechanism by which workers can proactively shape its terms across sectors. The AI Resource Hub continues to provide bargaining committees with updated toolkit materials, template information-request language, existing CWA AI contract provisions for reuse, and model state and federal legislative language.
