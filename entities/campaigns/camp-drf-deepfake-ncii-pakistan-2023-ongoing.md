# DRF Deepfake and NCII Campaign (Pakistan, 2023–ongoing)

Since 2023 the [Digital Rights Foundation](../organizations/org-digital-rights-foundation.md) has run a distinct research-and-advocacy campaign targeting AI-generated deepfake and non-consensual intimate imagery (NCII) harm in Pakistan — a deepfake-specific strand that operates alongside but separate from the broader [DRF Cyber Harassment Helpline](camp-drf-cyber-harassment-helpline-pakistan.md), which provides victim-support services across online harassment more broadly. The campaign is grounded in the helpline's 2023 caseload — [the first year in which AI-generated deepfake harassment of women journalists and public figures was named as a distinct complaint pattern](https://thefridaytimes.com/05-Apr-2024/ai-among-technologies-increasingly-used-to-harass-women-online-in-pakistan) — and extends into dedicated research publications, election monitoring, platform-accountability advocacy, and a push for explicit Pakistani legislative protection against synthetic intimate images.

## Origins: the 2023 inflection point

DRF's helpline data established the campaign's empirical anchor. The 2023 annual caseload — 2,473 complaints, with non-consensual image use representing 334 cases — was the first year in which Executive Director [Nighat Dad](../persons/person-nighat-dad.md) publicly named generative-AI-mediated deepfakes as a distinct, emergent harm category among the helpline's complaint patterns. In November 2023, ahead of Pakistan's February 2024 elections, DRF's Digital 50.50 feminist e-magazine published ["Election Illusions: Deepfakes and Disinformation in Pakistan"](https://digitalrightsfoundation.pk/election-illusions-deepfakes-disinformation-in-pakistan/) — the organisation's first dedicated analysis of AI-generated deepfakes as a structural electoral threat. The article documented the Chat2024.com platform offering digital clones of Pakistani political leaders, a fabricated video of PTI politician Muhammad Basharat Raja announcing an elections boycott, a sexually explicit AI-generated video of Punjab Information Minister Azma Bukhari circulated across Pakistani social media, and the case of Shehzadi Rai — Pakistan's first elected transgender municipal councilor — who testified to AI-fabricated intimate images shared via WhatsApp used to harass and discredit her.

## The 2024 Election Desk

For Pakistan's February 2024 general elections DRF operated a dedicated Election Desk as an observatory body. The Desk collected real-time data on AI-generated gendered disinformation, technology-facilitated gender-based violence, and platform responsiveness, and documented internet shutdowns and human-rights abuses around the election period. The monitoring produced research including "Gendered Online Hate in Pakistan" and "Gendered Disinformation in South Asia Case Study — Pakistan," grounding DRF's election-specific advocacy in documented real-time evidence.

## Research and platform-accountability advocacy

DRF's 2024 caseload data — [3,171 TFGBV cases](https://www.dawn.com/news/1959376), a rise from 2,473 in 2023 — anchored the platform-accountability strand. Dad stated publicly: ["AI has lowered the barrier for abuse. With just a photo and a free app, anyone can manufacture a scandal."](https://www.dawn.com/news/1959376) The campaign presses platforms for mandatory synthetic-image takedown mechanisms and safeguards against AI nudifier apps — a position grounded in the documented gap between the Federal Investigation Agency receiving approximately 1,200 deepfake-related complaints in 2023 and investigating only twelve of them. In 2025, DRF published "Digital Battlegrounds: Gendered Disinformation, TFGBV, and Hate Speech in the Indo-Pak Escalations," documenting AI-synthesised content weaponised during the May 2025 India-Pakistan military crisis.

## Legislative advocacy and the PECA gap

Pakistan's Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act 2016 addresses cyberstalking and defamation but contains no provision specifically criminalising AI-generated NCII or deepfakes. The PECA Amendment Act 2025's Section 26A — criminalising "intentional dissemination of false and fake information" — does not name synthetic media. DRF advocates for explicit deepfake criminalization, victim-friendly reporting mechanisms, and sensitization of law enforcement and judicial officers to AI-generated harms. The campaign also encompasses DRF's review of Pakistan's draft National AI Policy, in which DRF submitted recommendations on transparency, accountability, and mandatory opt-out provisions from AI-based decision-making.
