# AI Whistleblower Protection Act campaign

[National Whistleblower Center](../organizations/org-national-whistleblower-center.md)'s campaign to enact the first federal statutory shield for AI-sector insiders who disclose safety violations or AI security vulnerabilities. Rooted in the 2024 OpenAI exit-paperwork controversy and the [June 2024 "Right to Warn" open letter](https://www.axios.com/2024/06/04/openai-whistleblowers-open-letter) signed by 13 current and former OpenAI and Google DeepMind employees, the campaign centers on the AI Whistleblower Protection Act (AIWPA, S. 1792 / H.R. 3460) — bipartisan legislation targeting the contractual deterrent stack (NDAs, non-disparagement clauses, equity-vesting retaliation) that currently makes lawful disclosure uniquely costly for AI-sector insiders.

## Background and legislative gap

The immediate trigger was the May 2024 disclosure that departing OpenAI employees faced a choice: sign sweeping non-disclosure and non-disparagement clauses or forfeit vested equity worth millions of dollars. The [June 2024 "Right to Warn" letter](https://www.axios.com/2024/06/04/openai-whistleblowers-open-letter) — signed by 13 insiders and endorsed by Yoshua Bengio, Geoffrey Hinton, and Stuart Russell — stated explicitly that existing US whistleblower law was insufficient because it protects against *illegal* activity only, while the gravest AI risks may involve lawful-but-dangerous conduct not yet regulated. NWC, whose legislative track record addresses exactly this pattern (corporate NDA regimes that deter accountability disclosure), identified the AIWPA as the targeted statutory fix.

## The legislation

[Sen. Chuck Grassley](https://www.grassley.senate.gov/news/news-releases/grassley-introduces-ai-whistleblower-protection-act) (R-IA) introduced S. 1792 on May 15, 2025 with bipartisan co-sponsors Sen. Chris Coons (D-DE), Rep. Jay Obernolte (R-CA), and Rep. Ted Lieu (D-CA). Obernolte introduced the House companion H.R. 3460 in parallel. The bill covers disclosures of AI security vulnerabilities and violations of federal law during AI development or deployment. Its core provisions: bar any NDA or non-disparagement agreement that would prevent disclosure to a federal agency; prohibit employer retaliation; route complaints through the Department of Labor; and provide remedies including reinstatement, doubled back-pay, compensatory damages, attorney fee recovery, and jury-trial access with a three-year statute of limitations. In June 2025, Grassley and Coons introduced the AIWPA as an amendment to the National Defense Authorization Act — a must-pass vehicle — [opening a floor-vote path](https://www.coons.senate.gov/news/press-releases/senators-coons-grassley-introduce-ai-whistleblower-protection-act).

## Coalition and public pressure

On June 10, 2025, [NWC coordinated a coalition letter](https://www.whistleblowers.org/news/tech-safety-groups-urge-congress-to-pass-the-ai-whistleblower-bill/) signed by more than 20 tech safety advocacy organizations urging Congress to pass the AIWPA, including [Americans for Responsible Innovation](../organizations/org-americans-for-responsible-innovation.md), [Center for Humane Technology](../organizations/org-center-for-humane-technology.md), [Government Accountability Project](../organizations/org-government-accountability-project.md), [Encode Justice](../organizations/org-encode-justice.md), and [Tech Oversight Project](../organizations/org-tech-oversight-project.md). NWC simultaneously ran constituent action-alert campaigns directing the public to contact their congressional representatives, framing the AI regulatory gap as analogous to Congress's inaction in the early social media era.

On July 30, 2025 — National Whistleblower Appreciation Day — NWC hosted a [Capitol Hill event](https://www.whistleblowers.org/news/national-whistleblower-day-urges-protections-for-federal-and-ai-whistleblowers/) featuring departing OpenAI researcher Daniel Kokotajlo alongside Sen. Grassley, grounding the legislative campaign in documented cases of insider suppression at frontier AI labs.

As of September 2026, [S. 1792 remains in committee](https://www.govtrack.us/congress/bills/119/s1792); no enacted outcome yet.
