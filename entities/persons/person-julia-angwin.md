# Julia Angwin

Investigative journalist widely credited with establishing empirical algorithmic-accountability reporting as a journalism discipline. [At The Wall Street Journal (2000–2013)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Angwin) she led a privacy investigative team; was part of the [2003 Pulitzer Prize–winning team for corporate corruption coverage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Angwin); and won the [2010 Gerald Loeb Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Angwin) for privacy reporting.

At [ProPublica](../organizations/org-propublica.md) (2014–2018) she led the [Machine Bias](https://www.propublica.org/series/machine-bias) series — including the 2016 COMPAS investigation documenting racial bias in criminal sentencing algorithms — and the Facebook housing-ads discrimination investigations whose findings prompted federal enforcement actions and Fair Housing Act litigation. She was a Pulitzer Prize finalist for Explanatory Reporting in 2017 and won the 2018 Gerald Loeb Award for the work.

Co-founded [The Markup](../organizations/org-the-markup.md) with Jeff Larson and Sue Gardner in September 2018; departed February 2023. Founded [Proof News](https://proofnews.org/) in March 2024, a nonprofit journalism studio extending the algorithmic-accountability model to climate and corporate accountability reporting. Appointed founding director of the independent media initiative at the [Shorenstein Center on Media, Politics and Public Policy](https://shorensteincenter.org/article/independent-media-julia-angwin/), Harvard Kennedy School (2026). Author of *Dragnet Nation: A Quest for Privacy, Security, and Freedom in a World of Relentless Surveillance* (Times Books, 2014 — New York Times bestseller; Financial Times shortlist for Best Business Book) and *On Courage: How to be a Dissident in an Age of Fear* (HarperCollins, 2026); New York Times contributing Opinion writer.
