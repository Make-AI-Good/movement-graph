# Stop Hate for Profit campaign launch (17 June 2020)

On [17 June 2020](https://www.adl.org/resources/press-release/adl-naacp-sleeping-giants-common-sense-free-press-and-color-change-call), six civil-rights and advocacy organizations — the [Anti-Defamation League](../organizations/org-anti-defamation-league.md), the NAACP, [Color of Change](../organizations/org-color-of-change.md), Common Sense Media, [Free Press](../organizations/org-free-press.md), and Sleeping Giants — publicly launched [Stop Hate for Profit](../campaigns/camp-stop-hate-for-profit.md) through a full-page advertisement in the Los Angeles Times headlined ["What would you do with $70 billion?"](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/adl-naacp-sleeping-giants-common-sense-free-press--color-of-change-call-for-global-corporations-to-pause-advertising-on-facebook-to-stop-hate-online-301078408.html) — Facebook's approximate annual advertising revenue — and called on major corporate advertisers to pause all paid advertising on Facebook and Instagram for July 2020. The launch was a media and press-release event, not a physical gathering; its vehicle was the LA Times placement, the accompanying press release distributed via PR Newswire, and a campaign website at stophateforprofit.org.

## Context and trigger

The immediate trigger was Facebook's June 2020 decision not to moderate posts by Donald Trump calling for police and military force against protesters during the George Floyd demonstrations — content the founding coalition argued violated Facebook's own policies and exemplified a documented pattern of algorithmic amplification of hate speech, racism, antisemitism, and disinformation. The coalition's civil-rights grievances pre-dated the Trump post; the George Floyd moment provided an opening to translate longstanding documented failures into a demand with corporate economic leverage.

## Launch demands

The [founding press release](https://www.adl.org/resources/press-release/adl-naacp-sleeping-giants-common-sense-free-press-and-color-change-call) named three categories of Facebook failure: tolerance for posts inciting violence against peaceful protesters; labeling of outlets like Breitbart News and The Daily Caller as "trusted sources"; and failure to act on documented voter suppression efforts. The campaign's initial demands called on Facebook to stop generating ad revenue from hate-inciting content, commission civil-rights audits of its moderation algorithms, provide structured support for users targeted on the basis of identity, and hire a C-suite civil-rights executive with product and policy authority. The coalition subsequently expanded these to a nine-point demand list as the campaign scaled through June and July 2020.

## Early advertiser response

The North Face, REI, and Patagonia [joined the boycott on 22 June 2020](https://techcrunch.com/2020/06/22/facebook-instagram-rei-patagonia-north-face-brands-join-stophateforprofit-boycott) — five days after launch — as the first major outdoor and lifestyle brands to commit publicly. Their commitments accelerated corporate uptake: by July 2020 over 1,200 businesses and nonprofits had joined, including Unilever, Coca-Cola, Verizon, Starbucks, Target, and Ford. The campaign and its outcomes are documented in [camp-stop-hate-for-profit](../campaigns/camp-stop-hate-for-profit.md).
