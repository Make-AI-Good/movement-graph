# Civil society policy track at the Paris AI Action Summit (February 2025)

The [Paris AI Action Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_Action_Summit_2025) — co-chaired by French President Emmanuel Macron and Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi at the Grand Palais on 10–11 February 2025 — incorporated a civil society track running through five pre-summit consultation meetings attended by more than 500 organisations, a one-third civil society share of the summit's 1,000+ attendees, and a dedicated civil society side event on 11 February. That side event, ["Global AI Governance: Empowering Civil Society"](https://www.renaissancenumerique.org/en/events/global-ai-governance-empowering-civil-society/), was organised by Renaissance Numérique, The Future Society, Wikimedia France, Avaaz Foundation, Connected by Data, and the European Center for Non-Profit Law, and ran 9:00–12:00 at Racine Avocats in central Paris; it gathered civil society actors to identify structural obstacles to meaningful participation in global AI governance and to draft proposals for future summits, building on the previous day's summit sessions and on The Future Society's pre-summit consultation of [11,000+ citizens and 200+ expert organisations](https://thefuturesociety.org/towards-progress-in-paris-our-engagement-with-the-ai-action-summit-and-side-events/).

## Civil society statements and advocacy

On 5 February, days before the summit, [more than 100 civil society organisations](https://tacd.org/days-before-the-ai-action-summit-100-civil-society-organisations-urge-policymakers-and-leaders-to-acknowledge-ais-environmental-harms/) — coordinated by the Green Screen Coalition for Digital Rights and Climate Justice — signed a joint environmental statement demanding that policymakers phase out fossil fuels from the AI supply chain, adopt computing-within-limits frameworks, enforce responsible supply chains, ensure equitable community participation in AI deployment decisions, and mandate transparency on environmental impacts. Signatories included [EDRi](../organizations/org-edri.md), [Amnesty International](../organizations/org-amnesty-international.md), [APC](../organizations/org-apc.md), [La Quadrature du Net](../organizations/org-la-quadrature-du-net.md), and [AI Now Institute](../organizations/org-ai-now-institute.md). The coalition presented these demands at the Forum on Sustainable AI within the official summit programme on 11 February at 9:35am CET.

[Access Now](../organizations/org-access-now.md) and EU-based partners attended the main summit and [characterised it as a missed opportunity](https://www.accessnow.org/press-release/ai-action-summit-a-missed-opportunity-for-human-rights-centered-ai-governance/) for human-rights-centred governance, criticising the summit for prioritising tech-company representation and rapid-innovation framing over binding human rights safeguards and lamenting that only a few civil society organisations — especially those representing the global majority — were invited to the official event. [EDRi](../organizations/org-edri.md) attended to advocate for people-centric and sustainable AI regulation, [observing EU leadership ceding ground on human rights and environmental protections](https://edri.org/our-work/edri-gram-19-february-2025/) to US and industry pressure. In France, [La Quadrature du Net](../organizations/org-la-quadrature-du-net.md) launched Hiatus — a coalition of French civil society organisations denouncing the subjugation of public policy to tech industry interests — as a domestic counterpart to the summit week's international civil society advocacy.

## Outcomes

The summit closed with a statement on Inclusive and Sustainable Artificial Intelligence for People and the Planet, signed by 58 countries; the United States and United Kingdom declined to sign. Civil society's collective assessment was critical: the summit had not produced binding governance commitments, had allocated insufficient programme space to human rights and environmental protections, and had underrepresented civil society and voices from the Global South. The side event produced a civil society wish-list of structural improvements for future AI summit processes.

This event sits alongside the concurrent [PauseAI international protest wave](../events/event-pauseai-paris-ai-action-summit-protests-2025-02.md), which targeted the summit from the streets across roughly twenty cities.
