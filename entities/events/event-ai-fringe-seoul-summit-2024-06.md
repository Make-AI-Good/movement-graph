# AI Fringe: Seoul Summit (June 2024)

The AI Fringe: Seoul Summit was a half-day civil society forum held on 5 June 2024 at the Knowledge Centre of the British Library in London, convened by Milltown Partners under the AI Fringe banner. The event was designed to complement the [AI Seoul Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_Seoul_Summit_2024) — co-hosted by South Korea and the United Kingdom in Seoul on 21–22 May 2024 — by widening the discussion to civil society, academia, and industry voices beyond the closed government-level proceedings.

The programme ran from 8:00 AM to 12:30 PM and comprised keynote addresses, fireside conversations, and three panel discussions with eleven speakers. Representatives from the United Kingdom, South Korea, and France were featured to reflect the summit arc's host-nation and successor-nation structure. The two headline sessions addressed AI safety — reviewing progress since the Bletchley Park AI Safety Summit of November 2023 — and the challenges for responsible and trustworthy AI. The [Ada Lovelace Institute](../organizations/org-ada-lovelace-institute.md) was among the civil society partners; the Open Data Institute's Global Head of Policy [spoke at the event](https://theodi.org/news-and-events/blog/reflections-on-the-ai-fringe/) and advocated for independent funding for civil society organisations, inclusion of marginalised community voices in AI policy development, data protection standards sufficient to anchor trustworthy AI, Parliamentary upskilling on AI literacy, and international consensus on dataset-quality standards at future summits. The event closed with a forward look toward France's AI Action Summit, which followed in February 2025.

The AI Fringe was established in 2023 to complement the UK AI Safety Summit at Bletchley Park. It [organized events](https://aifringe.org/) alongside each major government AI summit in the Bletchley–Seoul–Paris arc, with 40+ partner organisations across civil society, academia, and industry. Recordings of the 2024 event were publicly released afterward.
