# The Markup Citizen Browser launch (January 2021)

On [5 January 2021](https://themarkup.org/citizen-browser/2021/01/05/how-we-built-a-facebook-inspector), [The Markup](../organizations/org-the-markup.md) launched Citizen Browser — a custom browser extension and paid-panelist system built to observe directly what platform algorithms show ordinary users. [A nationally representative panel of approximately 1,200 paid Americans](https://themarkup.org/citizen-browser/2021/01/05/how-we-built-a-facebook-inspector) installed the extension, which captured and transmitted real Facebook and YouTube feeds to The Markup's servers, producing statistically valid samples stratified by age, race, gender, geography, and political affiliation. The panel operated in partnership with The New York Times.

The [first major investigation from the panel](https://themarkup.org/citizen-browser/2021/06/24/after-repeatedly-promising-not-to-facebook-keeps-recommending-political-groups-to-its-users), published in June 2021, found that Facebook continued recommending partisan political groups to its users despite multiple public pledges to stop — twelve of the top 100 recommended groups in the panel sample were political, and political groups were recommended most frequently to Trump-voting panelists. Senator Ed Markey wrote to Mark Zuckerberg demanding an explanation; Facebook subsequently attributed the continued behavior to "technical issues" and stated the problem was resolved.

Citizen Browser was the first participatory research infrastructure of its kind in investigative journalism: a paid, demographically stratified panelist methodology translating the opacity of social-platform algorithmic recommendation into statistically grounded, publicly verifiable findings.
