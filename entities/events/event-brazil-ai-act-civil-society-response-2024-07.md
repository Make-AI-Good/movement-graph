# CDR open letter and civil society campaign on Brazil AI Act PL 2338/2023 — July 2024

On 8 July 2024, the Coalizão Direitos na Rede (CDR) [published an open letter](https://direitosnarede.org.br/2024/07/08/open-letter-advocating-for-brazilian-ai-regulation-that-protects-human-rights/) advocating for Brazilian AI regulation that protects human rights, signed by 63 organizations and 82 individuals spanning digital-rights civil society, academic institutions, Indigenous rights advocates, and labour groups across Brazil. The letter was timed to rapporteur Senator Eduardo Gomes (PL-TO) updating his substitute report on PL 2338/2023 for a Senate floor vote that industry lobbying — led by the National Confederation of Industries (CNI) and major technology platforms — had [postponed at least five times by mid-July 2024](https://direitosnarede.org.br/2024/07/13/cdrnamidia-sbt-news-senado-adia-regulacao-da-inteligencia-artificial-e-entidades-acusam-pressao-para-impedir-votacao/), with CDR member organizations publicly accusing industry of coordinating to block the bill.

The letter's seven specific demands on the rapporteur's draft were:

1. **Remove the intent requirement** from prohibited AI uses, so bans apply regardless of whether harm was deliberate
2. **Eliminate causality links** in subliminal technique and vulnerability-exploitation prohibitions
3. **Ban — or impose a moratorium on — facial recognition** for public security and criminal prosecution, citing documented wrongful arrests disproportionately affecting Black Brazilians and women, and an average cost to public resources of 440,000 reais per arrest
4. **Restore credit scoring** to the high-risk classification (as it appeared in an earlier rapporteur draft)
5. **Reclassify predictive policing, emotion recognition, and crime analysis** as categorically unacceptable risks rather than high-risk uses
6. **Guarantee effective civil society participation** in governance structures of the proposed National AI System (SIA) with ANPD as lead coordinator
7. **Restore transparency and explainability provisions** from the June 18 version of the rapporteur's text

The letter explicitly rejected industry arguments that protective rules impede AI development, positioning the coalition as a counterweight to the CNI and Big Tech lobbying campaign. [Intervozes](../organizations/org-intervozes.md), [Coding Rights](../organizations/org-coding-rights.md), [ARTICLE 19](../organizations/org-article-19.md) Brasil, and [Access Now](../organizations/org-access-now.md) — CDR core members whose engagement with PL 2338/2023 dated to the [2023 Senate CTIA hearing phase](event-brazil-ai-bill-civil-society-senate-hearing-2023.md) — were among the signatories. The July 2024 campaign marks the escalation point of Brazilian civil society's longitudinal advocacy arc: from the 2023 hearing-phase technical notes through the July 2024 mobilization for a protective final text, to the December 2024 Senate floor approval that transmitted the bill to the Chamber of Deputies.
