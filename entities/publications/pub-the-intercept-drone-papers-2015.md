# The Drone Papers

*The Drone Papers* is a nine-part investigative series published by [The Intercept](../organizations/org-the-intercept.md) on October 15, 2015, led by Jeremy Scahill and a team including Cora Currier, Ryan Devereaux, Peter Maass, Ryan Gallagher, and Josh Begley. The series is based on classified military documents [leaked by an anonymous whistleblower](https://theintercept.com/drone-papers/the-assassination-complex/) — later identified as Daniel Hale, a former Air Force intelligence analyst — and exposes the inner workings of the United States military's drone assassination program across Afghanistan, Yemen, and Somalia. *The Drone Papers* established, from the program's own classified primary sources, that the kill chain driving American drone strikes rested on signals intelligence of known poor quality, and that the resulting targeting errors produced mass unintended casualties the program systematically concealed through default labeling.

## The algorithmic kill chain

The core methodological finding of *The Drone Papers* is that [signals intelligence (SIGINT) from phone and computer metadata accounted for more than half of targeting intelligence in Yemen and Somalia](https://theintercept.com/drone-papers/the-assassination-complex/) — intelligence the program's own classified documents characterized as "poor" and "limited." The targeting infrastructure relied on a [geolocation-watchlist system linking unique codes associated with cellphone SIM cards and handsets to specific individuals](https://theintercept.com/drone-papers/the-assassination-complex/): if a tracked SIM card was detected at a location, a strike was considered valid. The operational problem was that SIM cards are exchangeable and subject to counter-surveillance discipline; a target's device moving to a different person was indistinguishable, in the SIGINT record, from the target themselves. The program's own source admitted: [*"It isn't a surefire method. You're relying on machines capable of collecting extraordinary amounts of data."*](https://theintercept.com/drone-papers/the-assassination-complex/) The series made visible, through classified documentation rather than advocacy claim, the gap between the algorithmic certainty the drone program projected publicly and the intelligence quality it actually operated on.

## Operation Haymaker and the 90 percent finding

The most cited statistic in the series comes from [Operation Haymaker](https://theintercept.com/drone-papers/the-assassination-complex/), a special operations campaign in northeastern Afghanistan from January 2012 to February 2013. During that period, U.S. special operations airstrikes killed more than 200 people, of whom only 35 were the intended targets. [During one five-month window within the operation, nearly 90 percent of those killed were not the intended targets.](https://theintercept.com/drone-papers/the-assassination-complex/) The documents also revealed the mechanism that made this statistic invisible inside the program: all casualties — intended target or not — were [initially classified as "EKIA" (Enemy Killed in Action)](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/whistleblower-comes-forward-with-documents-that-offer-glimpse-into-u-s-militarys-drone-program) unless and until later proven otherwise. The default label transformed a structural error rate into apparent success in the program's own data, and the resulting statistics on "enemy" kills informed subsequent targeting cycles.

## The whistleblower: Daniel Hale

The source behind *The Drone Papers* was [Daniel Hale](https://theintercept.com/2021/07/27/daniel-hale-drone-leak-sentencing/), a former Air Force intelligence analyst who worked at the National Security Agency and at the Joint Special Operations Task Force at Bagram Air Base in Afghanistan, giving him direct access to the drone program's targeting infrastructure. He disclosed 11 classified documents — 8 secret, 3 top secret — to The Intercept. His [stated reasoning](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/whistleblower-comes-forward-with-documents-that-offer-glimpse-into-u-s-militarys-drone-program) was that the program represented a "death sentence without notice, on a worldwide battlefield" and that the public was entitled to know how targeting decisions were made. Hale was indicted in May 2019 and [pleaded guilty to one count under the Espionage Act in March 2021](https://theintercept.com/2021/07/27/daniel-hale-drone-leak-sentencing/), receiving a 45-month sentence on July 27, 2021 — at the time the second-longest sentence ever handed down for whistleblowing by a civilian court in the United States. His prosecution became a focal case for press freedom and whistleblower-protection advocacy, with Reprieve, Amnesty International, and other movement organizations citing his imprisonment as evidence of the legal exposure facing those who expose AI-mediated harms in classified national security contexts.

## Posture within the corpus

*The Drone Papers* is the corpus's foundational documentation that algorithmic kill-chain reasoning — machine-processed metadata producing targeted killing decisions — was operational in US drone warfare by 2012, was internally known to carry poor intelligence quality, and was producing mass unintended casualty rates the program's own labeling practices concealed. This evidentiary basis — drawn from the program's own classified documents, not external critique — distinguishes the series from contemporaneous advocacy reporting. The 90-percent Haymaker statistic became the most cited single data point in the movement's case against automated targeting systems and the principle of meaningful human control, anchoring [Stop Killer Robots](../organizations/org-stop-killer-robots.md)' argument that the gap between algorithmic certainty and operational reality in lethal systems is historically documented, not theoretical. The series preceded the formal Lethal Autonomous Weapons Systems (LAWS) debate at the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons (CCW) by several years, and remained the canonical empirical reference when those debates intensified after 2017.
