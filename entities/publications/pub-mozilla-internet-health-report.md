# Mozilla Internet Health Report

The *Mozilla Internet Health Report* is an annual civil-society report series published by the [Mozilla Foundation](../organizations/org-mozilla-foundation.md) tracking the conditions for an open, safe, and beneficial internet. Published across five editions from 2017 through 2022, it was one of the few sustained civil-society-anchored publications documenting internet and AI governance trends for a general audience — drawing on [Mozilla Foundation's research networks, fellows, and grantees](https://wiki.mozilla.org/Internet_Health_Report) to produce a credible, broadly accessible account of how technology shapes society.

## Editions and trajectory

The series launched in 2017 with a pilot "0.1" edition, followed by full editions in 2018, 2019, 2020, and a final edition in 2022. Early editions organized their analysis around five domains: Decentralization, Openness, Privacy & Security, Web Literacy, and Digital Inclusion — a framework Mozilla built to describe what a healthy internet requires across its technical, political, and social dimensions. [The series was coordinated by Solana Larsen as editor, under the Mozilla Foundation's Insights team](https://wiki.mozilla.org/Internet_Health_Report), with J. Bob Alotta (VP) and Kasia Odrozek (director) providing institutional leadership across editions.

By 2022, Mozilla's programmatic focus had narrowed to trustworthy AI, and the report pivoted with it. The final edition abandoned the five-domain framework to concentrate entirely on artificial intelligence. The series was discontinued after 2022, as Mozilla Foundation's public research output moved toward more targeted AI-accountability work.

## The 2022 AI edition

The 2022 edition, [published July 18, 2022](https://www.mozillafoundation.org/en/blog/mozillas-2022-internet-health-report-names-power-disparity-between-who-benefits-from-ai-and-who-is-harmed-by-ai-as-top-challenge-to-health-of-internet/), identified "the growing power disparity between who benefits from AI and who is harmed by AI" as the primary challenge to internet health — framing AI governance not as a technical problem but as a structural power problem. Powerful entities were tightening their grip on AI development while bearing little accountability for its harms. The edition posed two organizing questions: "Who has power over AI?" and "Who is shifting that power?"

In format, the 2022 edition [was presented as a five-episode IRL podcast](https://www.mozillafoundation.org/en/blog/2022-internet-health-report-podcast-spotlights-people-reclaiming-power-over-ai/) hosted by social change strategist Bridget Todd — a deliberate shift from text report to narrative audio designed to reach communities outside tech-policy networks. The five episodes examined: AI-driven military surveillance and weaponization; labor rights in gig economy platforms; representational gaps in geospatial data; election disinformation and hate speech amplification; and AI bias in healthcare datasets. The edition won a Webby Award for the podcast format.

The 2022 edition's solution orientation distinguished it from prior editions: rather than cataloguing harms, it [featured twenty contributors building inclusive, human-centered AI alternatives](https://www.mozillafoundation.org/en/blog/mozillas-2022-internet-health-report-names-power-disparity-between-who-benefits-from-ai-and-who-is-harmed-by-ai-as-top-challenge-to-health-of-internet/) — showcasing people redistributing control over AI rather than only documenting the imbalance. Editor Solana Larsen summarized: "The centralization of influence and control over AI doesn't work to the advantage of the majority of people."

## Movement significance

The *Internet Health Report* was a flagship civil-society publication offering a recurring, institutionally-backed framework for assessing internet governance accessible to non-specialist audiences. Its five-domain structure gave advocates and policymakers a durable vocabulary for naming what an internet serving democratic values requires; its recurring format established internet health metrics as a trackable category across the movement, not just a snapshot critique. The 2022 AI power-disparity framing — centering whose interests AI serves and whose it harms — entered the movement's vocabulary alongside contemporaneous work from the [Algorithmic Justice League](../organizations/org-algorithmic-justice-league.md) and the [AI Now Institute](../organizations/org-ai-now-institute.md), making the *Internet Health Report* one of the coordinated civil-society voices naming the structural character of AI's inequality problem in the early-2020s formation period of the make-AI-good movement.
