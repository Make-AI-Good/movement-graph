# Generative Influence: More than one quarter of all federal lobbyists are now lobbying on AI issues

*Generative Influence: More than one quarter of all federal lobbyists are now lobbying on AI issues* is a research report published by [Public Citizen](../organizations/org-public-citizen.md)'s Congress Watch division on February 24, 2026, [documenting the scale and composition of AI industry lobbying in Washington through systematic analysis of five years of House lobbying disclosure filings](https://www.citizen.org/article/generative-influence/). Authored by Mike Tanglis and Eileen O'Grady, the report is the most comprehensive public accounting of AI industry influence in the federal legislative process through 2025 — quantifying who is lobbying on AI, for whom, and at what scale relative to civil society voices.

## Scale and growth

The report's central finding: [3,570 federal lobbyists — 26% of all registered federal lobbyists in 2025 — reported working on artificial intelligence issues](https://www.citizen.org/article/generative-influence/) in their House lobbying disclosure filings. This represents a 168% increase from 2022, when AI-focused lobbyists made up roughly 11% of the total registered lobbying population. The expansion in the breadth of lobbying relationships was steeper still: when measured by unique lobbyist-client pairs rather than individual lobbyists, AI lobbying activity grew 265% over the same three-year period, capturing the proliferation of corporate clients retaining multiple firms to work the same issues simultaneously.

The fastest-growing subset is data center lobbying. [Lobbyists explicitly citing data center issues in their filings reached over 400 in 2025](https://www.citizen.org/news/one-in-four-federal-lobbyists-now-work-on-ai/), a surge of approximately 449% from 68 in 2022, driven by AI model-training energy demands, federal permitting and siting legislation, and procurement contracts for AI infrastructure.

## Corporate dominance of the AI lobbying field

The structural finding that grounds the report's advocacy significance: [corporate entities constituted 82% of all AI lobbying activity, against 18% for non-corporate groups](https://www.citizen.org/article/generative-influence/) — a five-to-one resource imbalance in favor of AI industry interests over civil society organizations, unions, universities, and public interest advocates. All top 30 entities by AI lobbying volume, and 91 of the top 100, are corporations or corporate trade associations. The U.S. Chamber of Commerce led all clients with 91 AI lobbyists in 2025, followed by Microsoft (63), Meta (55), Intuit (51), and Amazon (48). Eleven large technology companies collectively spent more than $105 million on federal lobbying in 2025 with AI issues saturating those engagements.

By sector, Software & Services dominated with 1,448 AI lobbyists (30% of the total) representing 200 corporate clients. Media & Entertainment contributed 434, Financial Services 382, and Defense-related companies 259 — a breadth illustrating AI lobbying as a cross-industry mobilization rather than a tech-sector-only phenomenon.

## Methodology

The report analyzed five years of House lobbying disclosures from 2020 through 2025, searching registration filings for mentions of "artificial intelligence," "autonomous vehicles," and "data centers." The data source is the official House Office of the Clerk lobbying disclosure database, which requires all registered lobbyists to identify issue areas for each client engagement. The methodology counts unique individual lobbyists per year and unique lobbyist-client relationships, capturing the headcount and reach of the deployed influence machine rather than its cost and enabling comparison across disclosure cycles.

## Movement significance

*Generative Influence* is the empirical foundation [Public Citizen](../organizations/org-public-citizen.md) deploys in its Stop AI Preemption campaign and model state legislation program, providing the documented structural explanation for why, despite years of polling showing public support for AI regulation, [Congress has failed to regulate AI in any meaningful way](https://www.citizen.org/article/generative-influence/): the lobbying apparatus is deployed at a five-to-one resource advantage over civil society. The report makes this connection explicit — lobbying scale is not a background fact but the operative mechanism by which industry shapes legislative inaction.

The report pairs with Public Citizen's companion financial investigation, ["$1.1 Billion in Big Tech Political Spending Fuels Attacks on State AI Laws"](https://www.citizen.org/news/1-1-billion-in-big-tech-political-spending-fuels-attacks-on-state-ai-laws/) (November 2025), which documented at least $764.5 million in political spending by tech executives and investors in the 2024 election cycle and connected that capital flow directly to efforts to preempt state-level AI consumer protection laws. Together the two reports constitute the foundational Public Citizen documentation of AI industry political capture: *Generative Influence* maps the registered lobbying machine; the companion report traces the campaign money that coordinates with it.
