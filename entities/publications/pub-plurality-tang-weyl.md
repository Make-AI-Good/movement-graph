# ⿻ 數位 Plurality: The Future of Collaborative Technology and Democracy

*⿻ 數位 Plurality: The Future of Collaborative Technology and Democracy* is a book by [Audrey Tang](../persons/person-audrey-tang.md), E. Glen Weyl, and the ⿻ Community, [published by RadicalxChange on May 20, 2024](https://www.radicalxchange.org/updates/announcements/plurality-launch/) — the foundational text for the participatory-AI-governance and digital-democracy strand of the make-AI-good movement, and the most substantial single published account of what technology in service of democratic governance should build toward rather than only resist.

## Open-source authorship model

The book embodies its own argument in its production: [written openly on GitHub](https://github.com/pluralitybook/plurality), translated by community volunteers into more than a dozen languages, and [released into the public domain under CC0](https://plurality.net/) — freely available at [plurality.net](https://plurality.net/) as HTML, PDF, and ePub, and as a physical book (ISBN 9798869327116 paperback; 9798869327222 hardcover). Authorship credit goes to E. Glen Weyl, Audrey Tang, and "the ⿻ Community" — acknowledging the distributed global contributors who wrote, translated, and edited across languages and disciplines. This production structure is not incidental: the book's collaborative authorship is itself a demonstration of the recursive-public model it describes, and contributing to the book on GitHub was an instance of participation in the Plurality model before the book's formal release.

## Core framework

[Plurality](https://plurality.net/) proposes a third path between two framings it treats as inadequate: techno-libertarian autonomy, which atomises individuals into market participants with no infrastructure for cooperation, and technocratic AI centralism, which aggregates preferences into uniform policies that erase the differences that make cooperation valuable. The third path — "⿻ Plurality" — recognises and productively harnesses human diversity and cooperative potential: digital technology can **"strengthen democracy, bridge divides, and unlock the potential of human cooperation"** rather than erode or replace deliberation. The primary empirical grounding is Taiwan's two-decade experiment in civic-tech and digital democracy — the [g0v](../organizations/org-g0v.md) community, vTaiwan, Polis-based broad listening, the COVID-era humour-over-rumour pandemic-communications model — presented as demonstration that this path is already deployed at national scale and is not speculative. [Audrey Tang](../persons/person-audrey-tang.md)'s decade-plus as a civic hacker, g0v co-initiator, and Digital Minister is the book's primary proof-of-concept.

## Movement adoption

The framework was in active movement use before the book's formal 2024 publication, having circulated in open draft on GitHub from 2022. The GETTING-Plurality research network, which [co-ran the March 2024 Alignment Assemblies of AI](https://rebootdemocracy.ai/blog/audrey-tang-ai-democracy/) with the [Collective Intelligence Project](../organizations/org-collective-intelligence-project.md) and Taiwan's Ministry of Digital Affairs, took its name from the framework while the book was still in draft. The CC0 release and online availability at [plurality.net](https://plurality.net/) removed access barriers, and the community-translation infrastructure extended the framework into movement communities outside English-language AI-governance networks. The book has won the Randa and Sherif Nasr Book Prize on Religion & the World and the Australian Book Industry Small Publishers' Adult Book of the Year.
