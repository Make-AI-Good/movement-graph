# Eric Schmidt Cozies Up to China's AI Industry While Warning U.S. of its Dangers

*Eric Schmidt Cozies Up to China's AI Industry While Warning U.S. of its Dangers* is an [investigative report](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/eric-schmidt-cozies-up-to-chinas-ai-industry-while) published by the [Tech Transparency Project](../organizations/org-tech-transparency-project.md) on April 11, 2024. Based on Freedom of Information Act documents, tax filings, SEC records, and flight records, the investigation documents that Eric Schmidt — while chairing the National Security Commission on Artificial Intelligence from 2018 to 2021 and publicly warning Congress that China's AI capabilities constituted a strategic threat — simultaneously maintained substantial private financial investments in Chinese AI companies through Hillhouse Capital, and that FOIA-obtained emails suggest he directed NSCAI government staff to assist with his personal China business contacts during that period, potentially in violation of the ethics rules governing Special Government Employees.

## The NSCAI chairmanship and Schmidt's public position

The National Security Commission on Artificial Intelligence was established by the 2018 National Defense Authorization Act to advise Congress and the executive branch on AI applications to national security and defense. Schmidt served as chair from the commission's founding through its dissolution in October 2021, with full access to classified national security information and the status of a Special Government Employee — a category subject to federal conflict-of-interest statutes and ethics rules administered by the Office of Government Ethics.

Schmidt's public framing of China's AI development throughout the NSCAI period was consistently adversarial. The [November 2019 NSCAI interim report](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/eric-schmidt-cozies-up-to-chinas-ai-industry-while) declared that "developments in AI cannot be separated from emerging strategic competition with China." The commission's [2021 final report](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/eric-schmidt-cozies-up-to-chinas-ai-industry-while) concluded that "China's plans, resources, and progress should concern all Americans." In his [May 2023 congressional testimony](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/eric-schmidt-cozies-up-to-chinas-ai-industry-while), Schmidt described the AI competition as central to "the future of free societies, open markets, democratic governments," and [opposed a 2023 six-month generative AI pause](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/eric-schmidt-cozies-up-to-chinas-ai-industry-while) by AI researchers on the grounds it would "simply benefit China." In the [NSCAI's 2021 final report](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/eric-schmidt-cozies-up-to-chinas-ai-industry-while), Schmidt described Hillhouse Capital — the same Chinese private equity group into whose fund his foundation had invested — as a "$60 billion behemoth" exemplifying China's investment capabilities, without disclosing his foundation's financial stake in that entity.

## The Gaoling Feeder investment: $17 million in Chinese AI

The investigation found that in 2019, while Schmidt chaired the NSCAI, his Eric and Wendy Schmidt Fund for Strategic Innovation [invested approximately $17 million in Gaoling Feeder Ltd](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/eric-schmidt-cozies-up-to-chinas-ai-industry-while), a feeder fund channelling capital into the master fund managed by Hillhouse Capital Group, a Chinese private equity firm with an extensive AI company portfolio. As of TTP's most recent tax filing, holdings in Gaoling Feeder [exceeded $16 million](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/eric-schmidt-cozies-up-to-chinas-ai-industry-while). Bloomberg News separately reported that another Schmidt foundation held shares in Tencent Holdings and Alibaba Group.

Hillhouse Capital's portfolio includes companies whose activities the NSCAI framed as national security threats. The portfolio includes [Alibaba, which developed facial-recognition technology reportedly used to detect Uyghur minorities](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/eric-schmidt-cozies-up-to-chinas-ai-industry-while); [Yitu Technology, a surveillance company supplying AI tools to China's Ministry of Public Security](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/eric-schmidt-cozies-up-to-chinas-ai-industry-while); [Baidu, whose chatbot has been reported as used by China's military](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/eric-schmidt-cozies-up-to-chinas-ai-industry-while); and [Tuya, an IoT company U.S. senators sought to sanction](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/eric-schmidt-cozies-up-to-chinas-ai-industry-while). In 2016, Hillhouse [partnered with the state-run Chinese Academy of Sciences on a $150 million AI fund](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/eric-schmidt-cozies-up-to-chinas-ai-industry-while). The investigation documented that Schmidt's private foundation was channelling capital into this portfolio while the NSCAI under his chair was producing official assessments of those same Chinese AI capabilities as threats to American national security.

## FOIA documents and the ethics allegation

The investigation's documentary anchor is a set of FOIA-obtained emails from September 2019 showing [a Schmidt Futures staffer requesting NSCAI government staff assistance with "possible engagements" for Schmidt's planned personal trip to China](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/eric-schmidt-cozies-up-to-chinas-ai-industry-while), copied to NSCAI executive director Ylli Bajraktari. The emails request help identifying meeting contacts, preparing company briefings, and discussing topics. Under U.S. Department of Justice rules governing Special Government Employees, such employees are prohibited from directing subordinates to use "official time" for non-official purposes and from using "public office for private gain." The FOIA emails document government staff resources being directed toward private travel arrangements — a potential violation of both prohibitions. Neither Schmidt's Special Competitive Studies Project nor Schmidt Futures responded to [TTP's requests for comment](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/eric-schmidt-cozies-up-to-chinas-ai-industry-while).

## The November 2019 China trip and its contacts

FOIA-obtained flight records confirmed Schmidt's [November 19–24, 2019 trip to China](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/eric-schmidt-cozies-up-to-chinas-ai-industry-while) — a two-day extension beyond originally stated travel dates. The investigation documented multiple industry meetings. [On November 19, Schmidt met with Kai-Fu Lee](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/eric-schmidt-cozies-up-to-chinas-ai-industry-while), founder of Sinovation Ventures and former head of Google China, a prominent figure in China's AI investment landscape. He also met with [Fu Ying](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/eric-schmidt-cozies-up-to-chinas-ai-industry-while), China's former deputy minister of foreign affairs. A "[fireside chat](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/eric-schmidt-cozies-up-to-chinas-ai-industry-while)" with Neil Shen — founding partner of Sequoia Capital China, later rebranded as HongShan — was also on the trip record; Shen's fund was subsequently probed by the House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party for investments in companies contributing to human rights abuses and Chinese military modernization.

TTP's methodology here — FOIA litigation combined with tax-filing, SEC-records, and flight-records analysis — applies the accountability-journalism on-ramp directly to AI governance capture: the investigation is original reporting on AI harms in the political-money channel, with the "harm" being the undisclosed private capture of U.S. AI national security policy by a principal with overlapping and potentially conflicting financial interests.
