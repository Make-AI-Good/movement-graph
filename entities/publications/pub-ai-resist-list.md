# The AI Resist List

The AI Resist List is a crowdsourced, living database documenting acts of resistance to the AI industry from across the world, [launched on 20 May 2026](https://www.business-humanrights.org/en/latest-news/global-ai-resist-list-collates-collective-actions-against-the-tech-incl-demands-for-transparency-participation-supply-chain-responsibility-governance/) at airesistlist.org. Created by journalist Karen Hao — author of [*Empire of AI: Dreams and Nightmares in Sam Altman's OpenAI*](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/743569/empire-of-ai-by-karen-hao/) and host of the BBC's *The Interface* podcast — and Petra Molnar, Associate Director of the Refugee Law Lab at York University and author of *The Walls Have Eyes: Surviving Migration in the Age of Artificial Intelligence*, the project was [built by a collaborative team of researchers, journalists, and critical scholars working across seven time zones](https://www.techpolicy.press/the-world-is-already-resisting-ai-now-there-is-a-list-to-prove-it/), supported by the [Distributed AI Research Institute (DAIR)](../organizations/org-dair-institute.md), [We and AI](../organizations/org-we-and-ai.md), and the Refugee Law Lab at York University. The initial scoping was led by two Migration and Technology Monitor fellows: [Wael Qarssifi](https://www.techpolicy.press/the-world-is-already-resisting-ai-now-there-is-a-list-to-prove-it/), a Syrian journalist covering surveillance's impact on displaced communities, and [Verónica Martínez](https://www.techpolicy.press/the-world-is-already-resisting-ai-now-there-is-a-list-to-prove-it/), a reporter and photojournalist documenting US–Mexico border militarization — a deliberate centering of communities with lived experience of surveillance and AI-enabled border enforcement.

## Purpose and animating premise

The project's central argument is that AI development is not inevitable — that the rhetoric of inevitability functions as a political choice that concentrates corporate power and insulates AI companies from accountability. Hao describes the list as [meant to "center hope" and to surface "all the different creative ways that anyone can actually participate in applying pressure to the AI industry"](https://www.democracynow.org/2026/5/22/data_centers_and_ai). Molnar frames the documented resistance as [work that is "creative, consequential, and still almost entirely invisible in mainstream coverage of the technology"](https://www.techpolicy.press/the-world-is-already-resisting-ai-now-there-is-a-list-to-prove-it/). Both framings are explicitly counter to the Silicon Valley narrative of AI as a technological force beyond democratic contestation.

The database spans legal challenges, worker organizing, community campaigns, artistic interventions, and technical tools — forms of resistance that together demonstrate AI development is [neither an unstoppable force nor beyond accountability](https://www.techpolicy.press/the-world-is-already-resisting-ai-now-there-is-a-list-to-prove-it/).

## The Countering AI Inevitability Framework

Entries are organized under the Countering AI Inevitability Framework, [four overlapping modes of resistance](https://www.techpolicy.press/the-world-is-already-resisting-ai-now-there-is-a-list-to-prove-it/): **Resisting** (direct opposition through legal action and public mobilization), **Refusing** (worker and community rejection of AI deployment), **Reclaiming** (restoring community control over data, infrastructure, and decision-making), and **Reimagining** (envisioning alternative technical and governance approaches). The framework was [adapted from We and AI's resistance taxonomy](https://montrealethics.ai/tech-futures-introducing-the-resist-list/), designed to expand what counts as resistance beyond the protest-and-litigation forms that dominate coverage.

[Nine "Pillars of Support"](https://montrealethics.ai/tech-futures-introducing-the-resist-list/) structure the *targets* of documented resistance — the interlocking structures that uphold dominant AI companies: **Narratives, Funding, Data, Resource extraction, Data centers, Labour, Adoption, Surveillance**, and **Policy**. Each documented entry is mapped to the pillar(s) it presses against, making the database a strategic map of leverage points in addition to a historical archive. This pillar structure was adapted from [Choose Democracy's Resist List](https://montrealethics.ai/tech-futures-introducing-the-resist-list/), a framework originally developed to organize citizen resistance to authoritarianism by identifying the institutional supports that sustain authoritarian power — here transposed to the AI industry.

## Geographic coverage

At launch, [nearly six in ten region-tagged entries originated from the Global Majority](https://www.techpolicy.press/the-world-is-already-resisting-ai-now-there-is-a-list-to-prove-it/), spanning Germany, Japan, Kenya, Chile, the Philippines, the United Kingdom, Uruguay, the Democratic Republic of Congo, and the United States. The deliberate centering of majority-world resistance reflects the project's framing that AI deployment "occurs with fewer regulatory safeguards" in many Global Majority contexts, where affected communities bear a disproportionate share of AI's harms — from data center energy and water extraction to data labeling labor — while receiving fewer of the supposed benefits.

## Entry examples

The database at launch contained approximately 30 documented examples across multiple sectors and resistance forms:

**Legal action.** The New Mexico Environmental Law Center [challenging OpenAI's "Project Jupiter" data center](https://www.techpolicy.press/the-world-is-already-resisting-ai-now-there-is-a-list-to-prove-it/) on environmental grounds; JMITU in Japan [forcing IBM to disclose AI-driven wage-setting algorithms](https://www.techpolicy.press/the-world-is-already-resisting-ai-now-there-is-a-list-to-prove-it/) — one of the first documented cases of a union successfully compelling AI-wage-algorithm transparency through legal process.

**Worker organizing.** Kaiser Permanente mental health workers' hunger strike over AI workload intensification; the Philippine Coalition of Digital Employees–AI (CODE AI); and the Data Labelers Association in Nairobi, Kenya — connecting the Global Majority labor layer of AI supply chains to the resistance archive.

**Community campaigns.** Uruguayan community mobilization [demanding transparency](https://www.techpolicy.press/the-world-is-already-resisting-ai-now-there-is-a-list-to-prove-it/) from Google data center construction; Friends of the Congo addressing cobalt mining harms in the AI supply chain. Data center resistance appears across the database as a recurring locus of action: community mobilization [successfully stalled over $100 billion worth of hyperscale data center projects](https://www.democracynow.org/2026/5/22/data_centers_and_ai) in 2025 alone, spanning multiple continents and cutting across political affiliations.

**Technical alternatives and artistic interventions.** [Te Hiku Media](../organizations/org-te-hiku-media.md)'s [Te Reo Māori speech-recognition tool](https://montrealethics.ai/tech-futures-introducing-the-resist-list/) — a community-controlled AI model for an indigenous language, emblematic of the Reclaiming mode; Chile's [Quili.ai](https://www.techpolicy.press/the-world-is-already-resisting-ai-now-there-is-a-list-to-prove-it/), a human chatbot interface that routes AI-directed queries to human experts; Sam Lavigne's Slow LLM browser plugin; the "Microslop" viral campaign targeting Microsoft's AI output; Better Images of AI, a curated library of alternative AI imagery offering visual counter-framings to the dominant robot-and-brain iconography.

## Four cross-cutting policy demands

Across the documented resistance, [four policy demands](https://www.business-humanrights.org/en/latest-news/global-ai-resist-list-collates-collective-actions-against-the-tech-incl-demands-for-transparency-participation-supply-chain-responsibility-governance/) emerge: **transparency** requirements throughout AI supply chains; **participation** mechanisms for affected communities in AI governance decisions; **supply chain accountability** extending to mining communities, content moderation workers, and data laborers; and **public control** of AI infrastructure including land, water, and energy resources. The database's collective finding is that these demands [are ones "no existing regulatory framework fully addresses"](https://www.business-humanrights.org/en/latest-news/global-ai-resist-list-collates-collective-actions-against-the-tech-incl-demands-for-transparency-participation-supply-chain-responsibility-governance/) — making it simultaneously an action archive and a governance gap map.

## Relationship to *Empire of AI* and the notinevitable.ai forum

The AI Resist List launched on [the first anniversary of *Empire of AI*'s publication](https://digg.com/ai/0elzuwmq), the timing deliberate: where *Empire of AI* documents OpenAI's internal trajectory and the political economy of the AI industry's power concentration, the AI Resist List surfaces the globally distributed counter-pressure that exists alongside but largely outside that narrative. Both projects share the animating frame that AI's supposed inevitability is a choice, not a fact. An associated editorial forum, [notinevitable.ai](https://montrealethics.ai/tech-futures-introducing-the-resist-list/), extends the database's archival function into ongoing writing and discourse about AI resistance and alternatives.
