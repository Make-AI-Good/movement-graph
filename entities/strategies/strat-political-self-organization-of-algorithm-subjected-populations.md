# Political self-organisation of algorithm-subjected populations

A population subject to an algorithmic decision system — welfare and unemployment recipients under eligibility scoring, tenants under rental-screening algorithms, criminal defendants under pretrial risk assessment, borrowers under credit-scoring adverse actions, migrants under biometric border tech and asylum-decision AI, incarcerated people under parole risk scoring, benefits claimants under adverse-action automation — organises *itself* as a standing political constituency demanding voice in the design, deployment, and remediation of the systems that adjudicate their lives. The vehicle is a membership organisation whose base is the currently-subjected class, with its own leadership drawn from that base, running its own campaigns rather than supplying testimony to campaigns run for it — the "nothing about us without us" principle from the disability-rights and HIV/AIDS activist traditions, transposed to algorithmic adjudication.

An actor chooses this because the accountability infrastructure the movement has built — audit organisations, litigation vehicles, policy advocacy — largely represents *for* impacted people rather than *by* them, and the moral and political legitimacy of the movement's demands is compromised when the population most affected is a source of testimony rather than the movement's principal voice. A subjected-class organisation converts a diffuse harm surface into a bloc a legislator, a vendor, or an agency has to negotiate with — one that can withhold participation, refuse the system publicly, sit at the design table, and speak for its members with an authority the professional-advocate register cannot borrow. It also produces a lasting political constituency that outlasts any single campaign, ruling, or piece of legislation, and it develops leadership from within the affected class rather than importing it from outside.

It trades reach for base-building difficulty. Algorithm-subjected populations are often precarious, stigmatised, criminalised, or dispersed; the organising labour to build durable membership infrastructure among them is enormous, and turnover in subjected status (a benefits claim closes, a tenant moves, a case resolves, an asylum decision resolves either way) undermines the durability the strategy needs. The strategy also depends on the subjected class *identifying itself as one* — welfare recipients and criminal defendants are subjected to related algorithmic architectures but rarely see themselves as a common political class — and cross-population coalition among the differently-subjected populations is a live organising problem, not a solved one. And industry and state counter-strategies — offering individual settlements, threatening benefits withdrawal for organised participation, criminalising the organising itself in border or carceral contexts — bear disproportionately on this base.

## Ecology

Distinct from [AI-harm survivor and affected-family constituency organising](strat-ai-harm-affected-family-constituency-organizing.md). That strategy is anchored on bereaved and post-harm families whose moral authority is the harm itself and whose political demand is remedy for what has already happened. This strategy is anchored on the *currently subjected* class whose demand is voice over the ongoing system — often before an identifiable "harm event" has crystallised at all, because algorithmic adjudication produces its harm in continuous small denials rather than discrete headline incidents.

Distinct from [organise AI supply-chain workers](strat-organize-ai-supply-chain-workers.md). That strategy organises workers *as workers* against algorithmic management in the workplace or in the production of AI systems (content moderation, data annotation, gig dispatch). This strategy organises the population on the *receiving end* of algorithmic decisions in domains that are not the labour relation at all — housing, welfare, incarceration, immigration, benefits, credit.

Distinct from [survivor-led testimony as evidence](strat-survivor-led-testimony-as-evidence.md). Testimony is a *tactic* the subjected population supplies at particular moments; this strategy is the *institution* that produces and sustains that population's ongoing political voice across campaigns and legislative cycles. A movement that runs the testimony tactic without building the self-organisation institution is a movement whose subjected class becomes a rotating cast of witnesses recruited by professional advocates for each new campaign.

Feeds [strategic litigation against algorithmic state decisions](strat-strategic-litigation-against-algorithmic-state-decisions.md) as its natural client base and its political-legitimacy anchor — a lawsuit filed on behalf of welfare-recipients whose leadership is welfare-recipients themselves is politically legible in a way that a lawsuit filed by a public-interest firm for anonymous plaintiffs is not. Feeds [cross-movement coalition bridging](strat-cross-movement-coalition-bridging.md) into housing-justice, criminal-justice-reform, migrant-rights, and public-benefits movements the AI-good movement cannot enter on the strength of AI-specific framing alone — the self-organisation of the subjected class is the bridging tissue.

The strategy's structural bet is that a subjected class organising itself produces a moral and political authority that no representation-*for* register can borrow. Its structural exposure is the base-building slowness against which the algorithmic decision architecture rebuilds itself continuously — every twelve to eighteen months the vendor ships a new model, the agency issues a new rule, the eligibility algorithm re-thresholds, and the coalition's members either re-enter the fight or age out of subjected status altogether.
