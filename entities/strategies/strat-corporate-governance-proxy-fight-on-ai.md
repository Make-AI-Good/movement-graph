# Corporate governance proxy fight on AI vendors and deployers

Nominating dissident director candidates and running a full proxy contest at an AI vendor or AI-deploying public company — as opposed to submitting a shareholder resolution the board can reject or dilute. A proxy fight converts a governance grievance into a live contested board election. Reference lineages include Engine No. 1's 2021 ExxonMobil proxy fight (three seats won on climate-transition grounds), the Interfaith Center on Corporate Responsibility's decades of governance-anchored engagement, Trian Fund Management's activist campaigns, and — from the socially-responsible-investing tradition — CalPERS and ISS's decades of governance-track shareholder advocacy.

An actor chooses this strategy because AI vendors have concentrated their most consequential decisions — training-data sourcing, deployment scope, safety-review governance, executive compensation tied to deployment velocity — at the board level, where individual-officer accountability and formal board-committee structures determine the field's trajectory. A proxy fight, even a losing one, produces a shareholder-vote record every proxy-advisory firm has to weigh in subsequent years, forces incumbent directors to publicly defend the practices the challenger names, and can convert institutional investors' quiet unease into a governance-track intervention they can vote on. It draws heavily on movement-side and SRI institutional investors — union pension funds, faith-based investors, university endowments — whose fiduciary duty gives them cover to support governance-track interventions.

It trades accessibility for reach. A serious proxy fight requires SEC-filing sophistication, seven-figure legal and advisory budgets, credible director-nominee slates, and institutional-investor support the coalition may not command; it targets only public companies with reachable governance layers (which excludes most privately-held AI startups); and it converts a movement demand into a governance question about corporate structure that can obscure the underlying social harm. A proxy fight fought and lost can also entrench the target's current directors with a fresh electoral mandate.

## Ecology

**Escalation from [shareholder resolutions on AI vendors](strat-shareholder-resolutions-on-ai-vendors.md).** A resolution is a *request* the board can honour or ignore; a proxy contest is a *challenge* for the board seats themselves. The two are natural sequential moves — resolutions build the record and the coalition; a proxy fight cashes the record into a live contest.

**Fed by [endowment and pension divestment from AI](strat-endowment-and-pension-divestment-from-ai.md)** and [impact-investing / VC screening criteria on AI](strat-impact-investing-and-vc-screening-criteria-on-ai.md) — the institutional-investor infrastructure those strategies build is the shareholder base a proxy fight votes.

**Lever inside [sustained corporate campaign against a single anchor AI target](strat-sustained-corporate-campaign-single-anchor-target.md).** A boss campaign running every lever against one target treats a proxy fight as one of its escalations; the strategy names the governance-track lever specifically so it can be evaluated on its own.
