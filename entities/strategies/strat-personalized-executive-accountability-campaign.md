# Personalized executive accountability campaign

The movement selects a named AI executive — a lab CEO, a founder, a board member, a senior product officer — and organises a sustained campaign in which that individual is the target: protests at the corporate HQ addressed to them by name, protests at the venues they speak at, open letters demanding they answer specific questions, media pressure that makes them the public face of the harm, and (in the tactic's more disruptive variants) presence at their public appearances, alma maters, and residences. The output the movement pursues is not the individual's resignation as such — it is to make the *personal* cost of the executive's decision-making a variable in the executive's calculation. The strategic ancestry is deep: ACT UP's targeting of Reagan-administration health officials on AIDS; Alinsky's rules for "picking the target, freezing it, personalising it, polarising it"; the environmental movement's targeting of oil-company CEOs; the anti-apartheid movement's targeting of Reagan and Thatcher personally; the tobacco movement's targeting of the seven CEOs who testified before Congress.

An actor chooses this strategy because AI decisions are institutionally attributed to companies but *made* by a small number of identifiable individuals whose personal reputational and social exposure can be operationalised — and because the framing of a corporation as a diffuse impersonal actor is one of the corporate defense's strongest instruments against movement pressure. Personalising the target strips that defense: a specific human made a specific decision, is answerable for it in specific public settings, and can be asked about it by name. The strategy also works asymmetrically against the concentration of power at frontier AI labs: with a handful of individuals in effective control of decisions affecting billions of downstream users, personalising the target aligns the pressure with where the decision actually sits.

It trades broad-coalition legitimacy for tactical directness. Personalisation attracts vigorous internal debate inside the movement — protesters at the target's home, disruptions at their family's events, unmasking of personal information are contested tactics whose defenders and critics both hold serious positions inside social-movement practice. It also carries reputational risk to the movement: an executive who becomes a sympathetic target on account of the tactics used against them can be leveraged into the *opposition's* narrative, and adjacent coalition partners are frequently reluctant to sign onto a campaign that personalises the fight. The strategy also depends on the movement's ability to sustain focus on a single target long enough for the campaign to compound — a target whose salience the movement drops after a news cycle loses the pressure the campaign built.

## Ecology

**Distinct from [mass protest for AI moratorium](strat-mass-protest-for-ai-moratorium.md).** Mass protest addresses the industry or the government in aggregate. This strategy addresses a named individual as its target of pressure. The two often converge — a mass protest at OpenAI HQ carries signage naming Sam Altman personally — but the strategic logic differs: the mass-protest strategy's success metric is the demonstration of scale, the personalised-executive strategy's success metric is the specific individual's altered behaviour, altered public statements, or altered decision.

**Fed by [insider whistleblowing from frontier labs](strat-insider-whistleblowing-from-frontier-labs.md).** The personalised campaign becomes far more targetable when insider disclosures name specific decisions attributable to the specific executive — a named CEO's role in a named safety decision, a specific text message from the executive documenting the trade-off. The disclosures convert a generalised leadership critique into a specific answerable question the executive cannot dismiss as an outsider's misunderstanding of internal deliberation.

**In tension with [counter-narrative framing](strat-counter-narrative-framing.md) when framing depends on abstraction.** A framing strategy that positions "the AI industry" as the target of the movement's narrative loses coherence when a subset of the movement personalises a single executive — the personalised target's defence can be to distance themselves from "the industry" as a whole. Coalition-level decisions about whether to personalise are made against this tension.

**Amplifies [survivor-led testimony as evidence](strat-survivor-led-testimony-as-evidence.md).** A survivor addressing a named executive by name at a public appearance is a different act than a survivor speaking to a legislative body; the former forces the individual answerability the strategy targets, and the movement's decision to personalise the target opens the surface on which survivor testimony can land as direct address.
