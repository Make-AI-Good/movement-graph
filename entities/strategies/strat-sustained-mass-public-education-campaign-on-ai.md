# Sustained mass public education campaign on AI

A decade-long, mass-audience, professionally-produced public awareness infrastructure — funded and staffed as a standing organisation — whose primary output is sustained popular education about AI's specific harms and the movement's positive vision, targeting the constituency the movement needs but does not yet have. Reference lineages include the Truth Initiative's twenty-five-year anti-smoking campaign (nine-figure annual budget, a documented sustained decline in youth smoking rates), the Ad Council's Smokey Bear, Crash Test Dummies, and Friends Don't Let Friends Drive Drunk campaigns, the Partnership for a Drug-Free America (later Partnership to End Addiction), and the AIDS-education infrastructure of the 1980s and 1990s.

An actor chooses this strategy because AI's most consequential political horizon — the general public's baseline mental model of what AI is and does — is currently being shaped by the industry's own marketing budget (multi-billion annually), and no movement-side counter-infrastructure of comparable scale exists. A sustained multi-year campaign, professionally produced, converts diffuse individual concern into a public constituency with shared vocabulary and shared reference points, seeds movement framings into everyday discourse before any specific policy fight requires them, and gives elected officials a public they know is paying attention. It is the strategy that produces the audience every other strategy assumes.

It trades focus for reach. Mass public education is expensive at the scale where it moves opinion (the Truth Initiative operates on roughly $60M/year), requires philanthropic funders willing to commit multi-year unrestricted support to an operation whose output is diffuse public understanding rather than specific wins, and its measurable impact is slow and probabilistic — the shift in public opinion the strategy pursues is real but attributable only in aggregate. The strategy is also exposed to the risk that the campaign's own framings get captured by adjacent movements that want them tuned differently, or land in the political culture as a partisan artefact if the executing organisation is coded as one side.

## Ecology

**Distinct from [counter-narrative framing](strat-counter-narrative-framing.md).** That strategy is a *tactic* — the crafting and supply of framings for allied campaigns to use in their own communications. This strategy is a *standing operation* whose sole product is sustained mass public education at scale, running independently of any particular allied campaign.

**Adjacent to [popular culture shaping AI imagination](strat-popular-culture-shaping-ai-imagination.md).** Both target the pre-political layer of shared public imagination. Popular-culture engagement works through creative industry channels; this strategy works through direct campaign production (paid media, owned media, sustained outreach programmes).

**Feeds every downstream strategy that assumes a mobilisable public.** [Mass participation in agency rulemaking comments](strat-mass-participation-in-agency-rulemaking-comments.md), [mass protest for AI moratorium](strat-mass-protest-for-ai-moratorium.md), [ballot initiative and referendum on AI](strat-ballot-initiative-and-referendum-on-ai.md), and [electoral endorsement and candidate questionnaire on AI](strat-electoral-endorsement-and-candidate-questionnaire-on-ai.md) all assume a public that already knows enough to be mobilised — this strategy is the one that produces it.
