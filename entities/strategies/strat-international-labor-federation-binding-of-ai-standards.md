# International labour federation binding of AI standards

Working through international trade-union federations — the International Trade Union Confederation, UNI Global Union, IndustriALL, the International Labour Organisation, sectoral global unions — to bind AI-and-work standards into cross-border instruments: ILO recommendations and conventions, Global Framework Agreements with multinationals, ITUC resolutions, sectoral-federation policy adopted across national affiliates. Reference lineages include the ILO Declaration on Fundamental Principles and Rights at Work, the Global Framework Agreements IndustriALL has negotiated with two-dozen multinational employers on labour standards, the UNI Global Union's alliance agreements binding platform workers across multiple countries, and the ILO's post-2019 sectoral work on the future of work.

An actor chooses this strategy because AI's harms are cross-border by construction — the same content-moderation labour gets outsourced to whichever country has the weakest labour law; the same algorithmic-management platform operates identically across every country its firm serves — and unilateral national labour law produces predictable arbitrage. The international-federation channel is one of the few durable instruments that binds multinational firms to *sectoral* standards regardless of jurisdiction, and it converts fragmented national worker organising into a coherent cross-border demand a firm cannot escape by relocating a business function. It draws on the long institutional history of transnational labour coordination, and it makes AI-and-work a first-class item on the international-labour policy agenda rather than a peripheral concern.

It trades speed and specificity for durability. International-federation processes run on multi-year timeframes; the standards they produce are compromises across national affiliates with widely divergent capacity; and enforcement typically depends on the affiliate unions' own ability to hold employers to the international agreement. Weak or captured national affiliates hollow out the strategy from below, and the strategy is exposed to the historical pattern of firms signing international standards while contesting them jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction in the courts and legislatures where actual enforcement happens.

## Ecology

**Encompasses [organising AI supply-chain workers](strat-organize-ai-supply-chain-workers.md)** as one of its constituencies: content moderators, data labellers, hardware assemblers whose national organising the international-federation instruments can bind into cross-border floors.

**Distinct from [formal unionisation of AI industry workers](strat-formal-unionization-of-ai-industry-workers.md).** That strategy is the *jurisdictional* base — organising a workforce under a specific country's labour law. This strategy is the *supra-jurisdictional* superstructure that lifts jurisdictional bases into cross-border standards a multinational firm cannot arbitrage away.

**Feeds [cross-professional refusal of AI integration](strat-cross-professional-refusal-of-ai-integration.md).** International sectoral federations of nurses, teachers, journalists, and creative workers can lift national professional-refusal positions into internationally-binding sectoral positions.
