# Insurance-underwriter pressure on AI deployment

Movement-side campaigns organising pressure on insurers, reinsurers, and Lloyd's syndicates to refuse to underwrite defined classes of AI deployment — errors-and-omissions coverage for generative AI products, professional-liability coverage for AI-assisted medical or legal work, product-liability for autonomous systems, cyber coverage for AI-adjacent breach exposures. The strategy targets the underwriting decision itself: not the eventual liability payout, but the insurer's willingness to price and issue coverage at the moment a deployer needs it. When enough underwriters exit a coverage line or price it prohibitively, deployers face a binary choice — proceed uninsured and carry the exposure on the balance sheet, or exit the product line.

An actor chooses this strategy because the insurance stack is a structural brake the state has not yet chosen to apply and the market is already twitching toward on its own. Major carriers (AIG, Great American, WR Berkley) have inserted errors-and-omissions exclusions for generative AI products through 2024–2025 as unilateral risk-management moves; the underlying calculation — that AI-hallucination, autonomous-system harm, and algorithmic-decision discrimination expose insurers to unbounded tails — is one an organised civil-society campaign can amplify from a quiet actuarial retreat into a public accountability posture. The strategy is *below* legislation and litigation in the political stack — an insurer can refuse coverage tomorrow without a statute, and a well-organised campaign can move underwriting policy faster than any legislative body — and it targets a decision node (the underwriting committee) that industry public-affairs machinery is not built to defend.

It trades legibility for indirection. The public reads a legislative fight; the public does not read an underwriting exclusion, and the strategy's wins are footnoted in reinsurance-industry trade press rather than in mass media. The pressure runs through actuarial rather than moral registers, which means the coalitions available to run it look different from the coalitions running the movement's public-facing campaigns — insurance-industry insiders, reinsurance analysts, actuarial-society reformers, and financial-regulator staff are the actors the strategy needs, and few of them read as "movement" in the ordinary sense. The strategy also depends on maintaining pressure across the entire underwriting stack: a single Lloyd's syndicate walking a class does nothing if two others price the residual demand at a slight premium. And the strategy is exposed to industry captive-insurance countermoves — AI companies large enough to self-insure through wholly-owned captives (Meta, Alphabet, Microsoft) can route around any external underwriting refusal.

## Ecology

**The AI-movement adopter is not yet identified.** This strategy is currently the [`insurance and liability-based pressure on AI vendors`](../../intelligence/movement-gaps.md) `confirmed` movement gap — the Synthesizer's 2026-06-10 web sweep found no Insure Our Future analog for AI, and the strategy exists in the corpus as a form-attested but adopter-absent instance. Naming the strategy independently of its adopters is what the Strategy type is built to allow: the strategy is real by its form regardless of who is running it.

**The reference model is [Insure Our Future](https://insureourfuture.co).** Founded 2017, now a 35+ organisation coalition across continents (Rainforest Action Network, Public Citizen, Reclaim Finance, Urgewald, Ceres). The coalition has produced documented coal-underwriter retreat and a running scorecard of insurance-sector fossil-fuel exposure. The tobacco lineage's insurance-side work (asbestos liability, cigarette-manufacturer exposure) is the pre-climate precedent. The AI-movement transposition would require a founding coalition, a scorecard, sustained public-facing engagement with named insurers, and coordination with the underlying legislative-liability track ([`americans for responsible innovation`](../organizations/org-americans-for-responsible-innovation.md), [`Transparency Coalition`](https://www.transparencycoalition.ai/)) that supplies the liability framework the underwriting decision hinges on.

**Complements the corpus's capital-leverage family.** [Shareholder resolutions on AI vendors](strat-shareholder-resolutions-on-ai-vendors.md) operates at the ownership layer; [endowment and pension divestment from AI](strat-endowment-and-pension-divestment-from-ai.md) operates at the ownership-exit layer; this strategy operates at the underwriting layer. The climate movement runs all three arms simultaneously on the same target-set of fossil-fuel firms; the AI movement runs two of the three and has left the underwriting arm on the table. The three arms are structurally complementary — a firm facing coordinated shareholder pressure, divestment, and underwriter refusal is a firm being pressured through every capital-stack channel civil society can reach.

**Fed by [empirical audit and expose](strat-empirical-audit-and-expose.md).** The scorecard the strategy runs on — which insurer covers which AI product for which counterparty at what price — is an audit output before it is an organising output. Existing AI-audit infrastructure (Ranking Digital Rights vendor scorecards, MediaJustice data-centre tracking, Ada Lovelace regulator engagement) is available to feed the underwriting scorecard the strategy has not yet built.
