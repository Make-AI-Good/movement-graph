# Preemption defense — protecting subnational AI authority

Organised civil-society defence of state and local authority to regulate AI against federal (or, in federal systems generally, super-jurisdictional) preemption. The strategy runs on the floor of Congress and inside must-pass legislative vehicles — reconciliation packages, defence authorisations, appropriations bills — where the AI industry attempts to insert language freezing state AI laws for a term of years or striking specific state statutes outright. The counter-mobilisation combines coalition lobbying, bipartisan state-legislator letters, grassroots petitions, model-bill infrastructure that keeps the state pipeline visibly productive, and litigation-readiness against executive-branch preemption vehicles (task forces directed to sue states, agency preemption assertions).

An actor chooses this strategy because preemption is the single legislative move that can zero out a decade of state-level AI-accountability work in one vote, and because the state-and-local layer is where the movement has its clearest wins to protect — consumer-protection statutes, deepfake laws, algorithmic-discrimination requirements, procurement rules — that a preemption clause reaches through the Supremacy Clause without touching any of the substantive fights. Defending the state floor also keeps the multi-jurisdictional laboratory open: fifty possible sites of legislative innovation the industry cannot lobby simultaneously, and a pipeline of experimentation the eventual federal regime will be forced to draw from.

It trades offense for defense on a single legislative cycle, and it depends on a broad coalition holding across ideological lines long enough for the preemption vehicle to move — a defensive coalition splinters faster than an offensive one because there is no shared affirmative demand holding it together. The strategy is also structurally reactive: it runs on the industry's calendar, not the movement's, and each victory is followed by the next attempt in the next must-pass vehicle. The counter-tactic is not a single vote but the industry's willingness to insert preemption language in every plausible legislative host until one succeeds.

## Ecology

**Complements [movement-drafted model legislation on AI](strat-movement-drafted-model-legislation-on-ai.md).** The state pipeline the model-bill machinery feeds is exactly what preemption seeks to freeze; the two strategies form the offensive and defensive halves of a single subnational posture. A visible model-bill pipeline is itself a preemption-defence asset — legislators can point to concrete state activity as the alternative preemption would foreclose.

**Feeds [coalition lobbying of binding regional regulation](strat-coalition-lobbying-of-binding-regional-regulation.md)** at the federal register: the coalition assembled to defeat preemption is often the same coalition available to lobby affirmative federal legislation when the political window opens. Defensive victories are also membership-building for the offensive round.

**Adjacent to [AI industry lobbying transparency exposure](strat-ai-industry-lobbying-transparency-exposure.md).** Preemption attempts are near-invariably industry-drafted; exposing the specific industry actors, funders, and revolving-door channels behind each preemption vehicle changes the political cost of sponsoring one.

**Historical precedent, cross-movement.** Tobacco control fought decades of local-preemption fights (state statutes preempting municipal smoke-free ordinances) as its central defensive lane. Gun-safety lost most of its preemption fights in the 1990s and 2000s and now works from a floor where roughly forty-five states preempt local firearms regulation — the negative worked example. Climate has fought fossil-fuel preemption of state renewable-portfolio standards, methane rules, and rooftop-solar policy. The lineage is deep enough that AI is running a well-attested tactical form rather than an experimental one; the question is whether the AI movement will hold ground the tobacco movement did or lose it the way the gun-safety movement did.
