# Sanctuary-jurisdiction declaration against AI surveillance

Cities, counties, university campuses, faith congregations, and library systems declare themselves — via resolution, ordinance, or governing-body statement — a *sanctuary* against AI-driven surveillance: a general commitment refusing municipal deployment, refusing cooperation with vendor requests, refusing to share existing municipal data into AI-surveillance stacks, and framing the jurisdiction publicly as an AI-surveillance-free zone. Distinct from a specific-use-class ordinance ban (that is [municipal affirmative ban on a class of AI use](strat-municipal-affirmative-ban-on-a-class-of-ai-use.md)): the sanctuary declaration is *general and symbolic* first, and it is designed to be a **mobilisation vehicle** — a governing-body vote that recruits residents into a public identity commitment, produces a national mediagenic story, and marks the jurisdiction as movement-aligned across every subsequent contest that lands there. The reference models are the sanctuary-city ordinances on immigration cooperation, the nuclear-free-zone declarations of the 1980s (roughly 200 US local governments declared themselves nuclear-free during the freeze movement's peak), and the more recent "surveillance-free" university-campus declarations against IMSI catchers and predictive-policing tooling.

An actor chooses this strategy because a sanctuary declaration accomplishes what an ordinance ban does not: it converts residents into public *identity-holders* of an anti-AI-surveillance commitment their governing body has made in their name, and it produces a **coalition-recruitment moment** the movement can mobilise across every subsequent AI question that reaches the jurisdiction — a vendor contract, a school-district procurement, a campus-security bid, a police-department pilot. Each subsequent contest lands on ground the sanctuary declaration has already politically prepared. The declaration also produces contagion pressure between jurisdictions in a way single-use-class ordinances do not: nuclear-free-zone declarations spread council-to-council because the *frame* was transferable, and a well-worded AI-surveillance sanctuary resolution circulates in the same shape.

It trades enforceability for framing. A declaration without a matching ordinance and enforcement mechanism binds nothing legally — a subsequent mayor can quietly ignore it, a state legislature can preempt it, a well-funded vendor can litigate its way past it. Sanctuary-city ordinances taught this lesson: the frame does the political work but the specific-use-class ordinances (immigration cooperation limits, data-sharing prohibitions) do the enforcement work, and jurisdictions that ran the declaration without following through on the ordinance work saw the frame drained of substance within a mayoral cycle. The strategy is also structurally exposed to state-preemption counter-attack in US states with hostile legislatures (as sanctuary-city ordinances have been), and its symbolic register makes it easier to dismiss as gesture than a technical ordinance the vendor bar has to argue against.

## Ecology

**Paired with [municipal affirmative ban on a class of AI use](strat-municipal-affirmative-ban-on-a-class-of-ai-use.md).** The declaration is the political framing that recruits the constituency and produces the coalition; the specific-use-class ordinance is the enforcement instrument the coalition subsequently passes. A movement that runs only the declaration is left with symbolic wins whose substance evaporates in a single election cycle; a movement that runs only the ordinance forgoes the framing and coalition-building the declaration produces. The two together are the mature shape of local AI-surveillance contestation — declaration first as the frame, ordinance second as the mechanism, and continuous mobilisation across both as the coalition-holding infrastructure.

**Contagion-vector kin to [local rapid-response against a single deployment](strat-local-rapid-response-against-single-deployment.md) and [cross-movement coalition bridging](strat-cross-movement-coalition-bridging.md).** The sanctuary form travels *between* jurisdictions in a way single-deployment rapid-response and single-contract ordinance work does not — the frame is designed to be lifted and reused, and the movement's leverage compounds each time a new council adopts it, because contagion is the strategy's *primary* output rather than a byproduct.
