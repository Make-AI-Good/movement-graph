# Advertiser boycott of AI-deploying platforms

Civil-society organisations organise the advertisers who supply a platform's revenue — the brands, agencies, and media-buying desks — into a coordinated withdrawal of ad spend from platforms whose AI deployment the coalition names as unacceptable: recommender systems amplifying named harms, generative-AI content flooding advertiser-facing inventory, moderation systems that gate what appears alongside a brand's message. The organising vehicle is a public letter from a coalition of civil-society groups to named advertisers, a brand-safety scorecard tracking each advertiser's continued spend against defined harms, and a media cycle that converts an individual advertiser's continued presence into a reputational cost. The reference model is Stop Hate for Profit (2020) — Free Press, ADL, NAACP, Color of Change and Common Sense Media pulling more than a thousand brands off Facebook advertising for a month over algorithmic amplification of hate content.

An actor chooses this strategy because platforms' business model runs on advertiser spend more than on user payment, and the advertiser stack is a *shorter* pressure channel than the user base: fewer decision-makers, decisions reviewed at brand-safety and communications levels rather than at product, and each decision publicly consequential enough that a single-brand withdrawal is a news story. The strategy also converts a fragmented "consumer-side" complaint into an institutional-side procurement decision the platform's ad-sales team cannot ignore, and it does so through brand-safety machinery advertisers already run for reasons unrelated to the movement (GARM, the Brand Safety Institute, media-buyer-side content-adjacency screening). Movement pressure that speaks the brand-safety register hooks into infrastructure the target platforms have already legitimised.

It trades depth of pressure for legibility of demand. An advertiser boycott lands only when the movement can specify a concrete, brand-safety-shaped defect the advertiser can point to when justifying withdrawal — algorithmic amplification of a specific harm, generative-AI inventory adjacent to a brand's ad, moderation failure in a category the brand cares about — and when adjacent platforms exist for the ad spend to migrate to. Categorical demands the advertiser cannot operationalise as brand-safety criteria typically fail to move spend. The strategy is also brittle to substitution: an advertiser that pauses on one platform may migrate to another that is equally or more implicated, and platform lock-in from the advertiser's own retargeting-data investment can outweigh the reputational cost of continued spend.

## Ecology

**Distinct from [organized consumer boycott of AI products](strat-organized-consumer-boycott-of-ai-products.md).** The consumer boycott withholds usage or subscription payment from the platform on the user side. This strategy withholds advertising revenue from the platform on the buyer side. The two work in parallel on the same target but through different stakeholder pipelines — consumer boycott mobilises the movement's own supporter base as pledgers; advertiser boycott mobilises brand-safety and communications professionals inside advertiser corporations. A campaign that runs both amplifies through each pressure into the other: user-side movement dropping the platform hardens the brand-safety case for the advertiser to leave, and advertiser withdrawal hardens the movement-side case that the platform is losing legitimacy.

**Fed by [empirical audit and expose](strat-empirical-audit-and-expose.md).** The advertiser-side pressure needs a concrete adjacency story — an audit that documents brand ads placed against harmful AI-generated or AI-amplified content, or a study that quantifies revenue flowing to the platform from named advertisers. The audit output becomes the exhibit the movement's letter to the advertiser cites; without it, the advertiser has no basis to declare a brand-safety defect and no cover to withdraw.

**Distinct from [counter-narrative framing](strat-counter-narrative-framing.md).** Framing moves the public discourse in which advertisers make brand-safety judgments; this strategy moves the specific advertiser decision. A framing campaign might make an advertiser worry about being associated with an AI-deploying platform; an advertiser boycott campaign is the vehicle that turns that worry into a specific withdrawn contract on a specific date.
