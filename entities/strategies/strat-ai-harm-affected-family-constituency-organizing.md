# AI-harm survivor and affected-family constituency organising

Building a sustained membership organisation of individuals and families directly harmed by AI — parents of teenagers who died after chatbot interactions, tenants denied housing by algorithmic screening, gig workers deactivated by opaque systems, subjects of non-consensual deepfake abuse, defendants sentenced on algorithmic risk scores — as a *standing political constituency* rather than a source of individual testimony. The organisation operates a members' newsletter, an active chapter structure, an annual convention, a legislative-liaison staff, and a media presence built around named members over years. Reference lineages include Mothers Against Drunk Driving (bereaved-family constituency that produced a decade of legislative wins), Everytown for Gun Safety (gun-violence-affected families organised as a permanent bloc), and the opioid-affected families whose organised presence anchored the political legitimacy of multi-state settlement negotiations.

An actor chooses this strategy because AI-harm survivor testimony is currently a movement *tactic* — individual survivors called on by disparate organisations for individual moments — rather than an *institution*. No standing infrastructure represents their long-term interests, sustains their political engagement, or converts the accumulating population of harmed people into a legislator-facing bloc. A membership constituency creates continuity across the news-cycle attention individual harm cases receive, gives elected officials a name and a face to lose in each district, and produces the moral authority a policy-professional-driven coalition cannot borrow — the demand that people whose kin were killed by this be centered in the response.

It trades ideological breadth for moral weight. A survivor-anchored organisation foregrounds the specific harms its members have experienced, which shapes policy priorities toward remedies for those harms (deepfake criminalisation, chatbot age verification, algorithmic-decision liability) and away from the movement's broader systemic reforms (compute governance, structural competition, open science). The strategy also imposes a serious care burden — sustaining traumatised members in political engagement is difficult work, and burnout is chronic — and the organisation is exposed to industry counter-strategies of offering financial settlements to individual members contingent on political withdrawal.

## Ecology

**Distinct from [survivor-led testimony as evidence](strat-survivor-led-testimony-as-evidence.md).** That strategy is the *tactic* of using survivor voice at particular moments (hearings, campaign launches, media). This strategy is the *institution* that produces and sustains a constituency capable of doing that at scale over decades.

**Feeds [personalised executive accountability campaign](strat-personalized-executive-accountability-campaign.md)** — affected families are the moral centre-of-gravity personalised-accountability campaigns run on.

**Overlaps [child-safety mobilization against generative-AI companions](strat-child-safety-mobilization-against-generative-ai-companions.md).** Bereaved parents of teenagers harmed by chatbots are the specific sub-constituency that mobilisation runs on; the broader survivor-family strategy would encompass and extend it.
