# AI-generated image-abuse response infrastructure

Build sustained movement-side infrastructure specifically for the harm of AI-generated non-consensual intimate imagery (NCII) — sexualised, harassing, or defamatory AI-synthesised images of a named individual without their consent. The infrastructure is multi-layered: a survivor-facing helpline and take-down assistance service; a hash-based image-fingerprinting registry that hosting platforms can consume to block re-uploads; a legal-support pipeline for civil claims and criminal referrals; a lobbying arm pushing platform-liability and criminal-code amendments naming AI synthesis explicitly; and press strategy that centres survivor voices without further exposing them. The strategy treats the harm as a sustained infrastructural burden requiring specialised operators rather than a moment of legislative-fix work that resolves and closes.

An actor chooses this strategy because AI image-synthesis has, since 2023, produced a class of harm — mass-scale, low-cost, targeted image-based sexual abuse — that pre-existing anti-image-abuse infrastructure (built for revenge-porn, cyberstalking, and school-yard bullying registers) was structurally unequipped for: the images are indistinguishable from photographs to a casual viewer, generation cost approaches zero, and the harm has no pre-existing artefact (the target never posed) for a hash-registry or take-down claim to anchor on. Only a purpose-built infrastructure — hashing at generation, take-down coordination at platform scale, statute updates that name synthesis explicitly, survivor services calibrated to the specific isolation and shame register the synthetic-image harm produces — can respond at the horizon at which the harm is being produced. The strategy also builds durable operating capacity around a specific constituency (survivors, primarily women and adolescents) whose organising voice has been under-carried in the broader AI-critical movement.

It trades general-movement leverage for specialised infrastructural burden. The infrastructure is expensive to build and maintain — a helpline requires 24/7 staffing, a hash-registry requires ongoing platform-partnership coordination, a survivor-services layer requires trauma-informed professionals — and the operators run permanently against a synthesis technology that improves faster than defensive tools can keep pace. The strategy is politically vulnerable to being annexed by criminal-code-expansionist projects orthogonal to the AI-good movement (broad platform-liability regimes that spill into speech restriction; law-enforcement expansions that hurt marginalised communities). And the survivor-centring register the strategy depends on is uniquely susceptible to being extracted for its narrative power by adjacent political programmes that do not carry the corresponding service commitment.

## Ecology

Distinct from [survivor-led testimony as evidence](strat-survivor-led-testimony-as-evidence.md) by scope: survivor testimony is a *tactic* used to advance a legislative or judicial claim; this strategy is a sustained multi-year *infrastructure* to serve survivors and coordinate response, of which supporting survivor testimony is one output among several. Distinct from [AI-specific criminal-code amendments](strat-ai-specific-criminal-code-amendments.md) by breadth: statute updates are one output of this infrastructure, not the strategy's whole shape — the infrastructure exists whether or not a given jurisdiction passes an NCII-synthesis amendment, and much of the infrastructure's work is downstream service and take-down operation the statute does not itself provide. Distinct from [distribute user-side technical countermeasures](strat-distribute-user-side-technical-countermeasures.md) by side of the transaction: user-side countermeasures defend the *input* (an artist's work) against unconsented ingestion into training; this strategy responds to the *output* (a synthesised harmful image) after it exists — the two operate at opposite ends of the AI production pipeline against different harm-classes.
