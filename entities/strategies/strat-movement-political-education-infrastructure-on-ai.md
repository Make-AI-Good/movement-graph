# Movement political education infrastructure for AI organising

An organisation runs a sustained programme that develops the movement's own analysis of AI-and-power, trains new organisers, and grows the bench of AI-fluent movement leaders. The vehicle is not a public curriculum or a mass-audience literacy campaign but a *cadre-forming* infrastructure: an eight-month organiser fellowship with paid stipends and post-programme placement, a dedicated Political Education programme running convenings with member organisations, a members-only political education directorate authoring internal analysis year over year, an annual movement school running residentials on tech-and-power, a member-facing political programme naming AI as a class-power question and circulated as internal-education document. Reference lineages: the Highlander Folk School's role in labour and civil-rights organiser formation, the Chicago Freedom School's youth organiser training, Public Interest Tech University Network's fellowship-plus-alumni-network model, the socialist-tradition member education committees.

An actor chooses this because a movement of the scale AI-good requires cannot be composed of professional advocates alone — it needs a constantly renewed base of organisers who can adapt tactics to a shifting technology landscape, and that base cannot be recruited from outside; it has to be built inside. Political education is also the load-bearing internal glue: it holds the movement's shared analysis together against ideological drift, industry co-optation of language, and the professional pull toward vendor-friendly "responsible AI" postures. Without it, the movement runs on a small stable of senior organisers who cannot be replaced when they retire, burn out, or are hired away — and the strategy every other adopter runs depends on there being a next generation ready to run it.

It trades outcome-legibility for durability. Political education produces no headline win: no legislative passage, no ruling, no vendor withdrawal is directly attributable to the fellowship cohort a coalition trained in year three of its programme. Funders who prefer output-per-dollar metrics find it hard to sustain, and the strategy is chronically under-invested relative to campaign work — a Highlander alumnus's contribution to the movement's capacity may not surface for a decade, which makes the strategy exposed to funding cycles measured in three-year grants. The strategy is also *slow*: its returns compound but the compounding runs on the pace of a generation, not the pace of a campaign.

## Ecology

Distinct from [AI literacy curriculum organising in schools and libraries](strat-ai-literacy-curriculum-organizing-in-schools-and-libraries.md) (institutional education for the general public through the public-education apparatus) and from [sustained mass public education campaign on AI](strat-sustained-mass-public-education-campaign-on-ai.md) (broadcast to a mass audience). This strategy's audience is *the movement's own base and bench* — organisers, cadre, aligned member organisations — not the public or a school system. Curriculum authorship, teaching register, and the definition of a "successful outcome" all differ accordingly: the win is a graduating cohort that stays in movement work, not a documented shift in public opinion or classroom uptake.

Sibling to [public-interest technologist pipeline building](strat-public-interest-technologist-pipeline-building.md), which produces AI-fluent technologists for placement in government, agency, and civil-society technical roles. Both strategies build people-power for the AI-good movement; the pipeline strategy trains *technologists* and directs them into institutional placements, this strategy trains *organisers* and directs them into the coalition's own campaign work. A movement running both arms produces AI-fluent staff on the campaign side and AI-fluent placements on the institutional side; a movement running only the pipeline arm has trained technologists with no organising base to hand them to.

Feeds [counter-narrative framing](strat-counter-narrative-framing.md) and [cross-movement coalition bridging](strat-cross-movement-coalition-bridging.md) as their internal substrate: a coalition cannot coin a frame it does not first collectively hold, and it cannot bridge to climate or labour organising without organisers on each side who know each other's political vocabulary. Political education is what produces the organisers who can carry a shared frame from one movement into another without diluting it. Its structural upstreamness is the same shape as counter-narrative framing has *externally* — political education holds the analysis inside the movement while framing carries it outside.
