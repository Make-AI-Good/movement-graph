# Consumer product recall of AI products

Civil-society organisations file defect petitions and organised complaint dossiers with consumer-product safety regulators — the US Consumer Product Safety Commission under the Consumer Product Safety Act, EU market-surveillance authorities under the General Product Safety Regulation, national analogs — to trigger mandatory recall proceedings against consumer AI products causing physical or psychological harm. The strategy targets a specific and largely unused legal mechanism: consumer product safety statutes grant regulators authority to order manufacturers to withdraw products from the market, notify buyers, refund purchasers, and destroy inventory — powers that operate at a shorter time horizon than litigation, apply mandatorily rather than by voluntary compliance, and reach the product distribution channel itself rather than only the vendor's balance sheet. When a companion chatbot markets itself to minors and its output pattern harms them, when an autonomous consumer vehicle displays a demonstrated defect in obstacle detection, when an AI-powered children's toy exhibits an unsafe output distribution — a movement-side petition converting these harm patterns into a formal recall proceeding is a shorter path to product-market removal than any legislative or litigation route.

An actor chooses this strategy because the mechanism is *fast*, *mandatory*, and *unglamorous* — three properties that put it below the industry's public-affairs radar. Product-safety regulators operate through defect determinations, corrective-action plans, and mandatory recalls under statutes drafted for lead paint and crib collapse, and the political machinery AI companies have built to defend against congressional hearings and FTC actions is not the machinery that responds to a CPSC-style defect-determination proceeding. The strategy also *forces the vendor's hand* in a way voluntary compliance regimes do not: a recall order the vendor refuses converts into an injunction, a seizure, and criminal exposure for named executives. And consumer product safety regimes maintain a *public product-defect database* — the CPSC's SaferProducts.gov, the EU's Safety Gate — that generates continuing reputational cost the moment a defect is entered, before any recall issues. The movement's job is to convert its documented AI-harm patterns into formal defect submissions those databases and proceedings run on.

It trades reach for specificity — the mechanism applies only to consumer-market products, does not reach enterprise AI deployed in workplaces or government, and requires that a specific product-defect theory be articulable rather than a generalised harm class. Some of the most consequential AI-harm patterns (algorithmic discrimination in credit and hiring, mass-surveillance infrastructure, autonomous-weapons systems) sit outside the consumer-product statute entirely, and running consumer-product recall on those cases confuses the movement's own theory of the case. The strategy also depends on a defect-determination burden that is technically demanding — the movement needs product engineers and safety analysts able to articulate the defect in the language the regulator's proceedings require — and product-safety regulators are chronically under-resourced, so a petition may sit for months before the agency takes it up. Aggressive product-liability litigation ([`strat-class-action-litigation-against-private-ai`](strat-class-action-litigation-against-private-ai.md)) may win faster relief against a specific product than a recall proceeding would.

## Ecology

**A specific case of [sectoral regulator activation on AI](strat-sectoral-regulator-activation-on-ai.md).** The consumer-product safety regulator is one of the sectoral agencies the general activation strategy names as an available target. Naming it as a distinct strategy is warranted because the *recall mechanism* is a specific and unusual regulatory tool — market withdrawal, corrective-action orders, public defect databases — with a shape unlike the enforcement pathways of other sectoral agencies, and because the coalitions organising it are typically the consumer-safety coalitions (child-safety, product-safety, home-safety) rather than the general AI-accountability coalitions.

**Feeds [child safety mobilization against generative AI companions](strat-child-safety-mobilization-against-generative-ai-companions.md).** Documented harm from AI-companion products to minors is a canonical consumer-product-safety case: a product marketed to a defined consumer class exhibiting a defect pattern causing documented harm. The child-safety mobilization strategy already runs the parent-coalition organising; the recall strategy is the specific regulatory endpoint that mobilization can drive toward. The Character.AI, Replika, and equivalent-vendor cases surfacing in 2024–2026 are the shape a recall petition would form around.

**Complements [class-action litigation against private-sector AI harms](strat-class-action-litigation-against-private-ai.md) via a parallel track.** A class action pursues damages and injunctive relief through courts; a recall petition pursues market withdrawal through regulators. Same harm evidence, two different endpoints — coalitions running both simultaneously against the same product force the vendor into a defensive posture on both fronts.

**Downstream of [consumer-facing AI use disclosure mandate](strat-consumer-facing-ai-use-disclosure-mandate.md).** Disclosure requirements are prophylactic; recall is remedial. The two are moves at different points on the consumer-protection lifecycle — disclosure prevents non-consenting exposure to AI in the first place, recall removes the product when exposure has already produced harm.

**The AI-movement adopter is largely absent.** No US or EU civil-society organisation is running consumer product recall as a named organising strategy against AI products at the surfacing date. The consumer-safety coalitions (Consumer Federation of America, Consumers Union, Fairplay, Common Sense Media on the child-safety edge) do organise CPSC-adjacent product-safety work but have not yet built AI-focused defect-petition infrastructure. This is a candidate scope-steering item.
