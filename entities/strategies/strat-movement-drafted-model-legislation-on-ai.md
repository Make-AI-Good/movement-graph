# Movement-drafted model legislation on AI

Coalitional drafting of ready-to-adopt bill text — a "model act" — and organised push for its adoption across many jurisdictions in parallel, so each individual jurisdiction picks up a movement-produced product rather than starting from scratch or accepting industry-drafted legislation as the default. Reference lineages include the American Legislative Exchange Council (right-wing model-bill machinery running for decades), the Uniform Law Commission (professional-uniform-law drafting adopted piecewise across US states), the NAACP Legal Defence Fund's civil-rights model ordinances, the Center for Media Justice's model surveillance-ordinance work, and the ACLU-anchored Community Control Over Police Surveillance (CCOPS) family that has been adopted by more than twenty US municipalities.

An actor chooses this strategy because legislators facing complex technical policy without existing statutory ancestors will adopt whichever text lands on their desk first with adequate coalition backing, and industry-drafted legislation has the incumbent advantage of well-funded model-bill production. Producing a movement-side model act converts drafting labour into a one-time expense recovered across many jurisdictions, gives every state, provincial, and municipal legislator a coalition-endorsed bill to file, and produces the standardisation that makes cross-jurisdictional advocacy coherent — each new adoption reinforces the interpretive foundation earlier ones built. The output is legislative uniformity as a movement asset rather than an industry one.

It trades responsiveness to local political context for uniformity, and it imposes coalitional discipline many organisations find costly. A model bill written to unify twenty jurisdictions may fit no jurisdiction perfectly; local advocates who want provisions the model text omits must break coalitional line, weakening the reason to have the model at all. The drafting process itself is a governance choke-point that becomes a target for capture — which organisations, which drafters, which academic reviewers hold the pen — and the strategy is exposed to the industry's countermove of offering a competing model bill as the "moderate compromise" that captures the same procedural advantage from the other direction.

## Ecology

**Feeds [coalition lobbying of binding regional regulation](strat-coalition-lobbying-of-binding-regional-regulation.md).** A coalition running a specific-jurisdiction lobbying campaign runs faster when it has a model text to lobby *for* rather than a policy demand to translate into bill language mid-fight.

**Feeds [municipal affirmative ban on a class of AI use](strat-municipal-affirmative-ban-on-a-class-of-ai-use.md)** and [AI-specific criminal-code amendments](strat-ai-specific-criminal-code-amendments.md) — both types of subnational legislation are natural adoption targets for a model text.

**Distinct from [mandatory algorithmic impact assessment requirement](strat-mandatory-algorithmic-impact-assessment-requirement.md)** as a *particular substantive policy demand*. The model-legislation strategy is orthogonal — it is a *how* rather than a *what*, and can carry an AIA requirement, a moratorium, a disclosure regime, or any other substantive demand as its content.
