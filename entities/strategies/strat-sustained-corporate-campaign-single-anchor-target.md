# Sustained corporate campaign against a single anchor AI target

A civil-society coalition selects one dominant AI vendor as its named anchor target and coordinates every available lever — litigation, shareholder pressure, consumer boycott, worker organizing, regulatory complaint, media exposure, executive accountability — against that single company across a multi-year horizon, until the company either concedes the coalition's substantive demands or breaks. The strategy is defined by *concentration of pressure on one target over years*, not by the choice of lever. Precedent lineages include Corporate Accountability's decades-long campaign against Nestlé over infant formula, Rainforest Action Network's forest-financing campaigns against JPMorgan Chase and other banks, and the sustained multi-lever pressure that produced Nike's contract-labor reforms in the 1990s.

An actor chooses this strategy because AI-industry power is concentrated in a handful of vertically-integrated firms whose decisions bind the field; converting industry-wide diffuse demands into company-specific enforceable ones is what no single-lever campaign against them can do. The anchor-target focus creates a media narrative every subsequent lever hooks into ("the company that…"), converts each partial win into evidence for the next escalation, and produces defection incentives for other AI vendors who prefer not to be the next target. It builds coalitional infrastructure — a shared research desk, litigation coordination, communications spine — that survives any single campaign.

It trades breadth for depth, and horizon for immediacy. Everything else the movement fights becomes secondary during the campaign's life, and the coalition can spend years on structural change at one company while a dozen others deploy the same practice unopposed. The target's countermoves — internal reforms designed to peel off coalition members, litigation against organizers, cultivation of alternative civil-society voices — are optimised against exactly this shape of pressure, and require a strategic discipline that most issue-coalitions lack. A campaign that runs down without a definitive win becomes evidence for the industry that the target could not be moved.

## Ecology

**Distinct from [personalised executive accountability campaign](strat-personalized-executive-accountability-campaign.md).** That strategy names individuals (a named CEO, a named investor); this strategy names a corporation and pursues every lever against it, treating the individual-accountability tactic as one of several inside a larger campaign.

**Encompasses [organised consumer boycott of AI products](strat-organized-consumer-boycott-of-ai-products.md)** and any of the shareholder / worker / regulatory levers when they are coordinated against the same anchor target across time. A boss campaign is not one lever — it is the discipline of running many levers on one target as one campaign.

**Feeds and is fed by [cross-movement coalition bridging](strat-cross-movement-coalition-bridging.md).** A boss campaign against a firm operating across multiple movement domains (climate exposure through data-centre compute, labor exposure through content-moderation contracts, discrimination exposure through decision-automation) is often the concrete organising site where cross-movement coalitions are built or dissolve.
