# Pro-Human AI Declaration

The **Pro-Human AI Declaration** is a cross-ideological civil society statement finalized in January 2026, asserting that artificial intelligence must serve humanity rather than replace it. Its 33 principles are organized under five pillars: Keeping Humans in Charge; Avoiding Concentration of Power; Protecting the Human Experience; Human Agency and Liberty; and Responsibility and Accountability for AI Companies. Specific demands include mandatory "bot-or-not" disclosure labeling, criminal liability for AI executives responsible for egregious AI harm, and a moratorium on deploying superintelligent architectures until scientific consensus and democratic approval are achieved.

The coalition was assembled by the Future of Life Institute with the deliberate exclusion of tech-industry representatives. Signatories span the ideological spectrum: AI researchers [Geoffrey Hinton](../persons/person-geoffrey-hinton.md) and Yoshua Bengio, conservative commentators Steve Bannon and Glenn Beck, former Obama National Security Advisor Susan Rice, consumer advocates Ralph Nader and [Meredith Whittaker](../persons/person-meredith-whittaker.md), the AFL-CIO Tech Institute, [SAG-AFTRA](../organizations/org-sag-aftra.md), the [American Federation of Teachers](../organizations/org-american-federation-of-teachers.md), [Public Citizen](../organizations/org-public-citizen.md), and more than 40 organizations in total. Polling [showed 83% of Americans](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/-human-ai-declaration-brings-together-unlikely-group-calling-trustwort-rcna261594) agreeing with the declaration's leading principle, "Humanity must remain in control of AI."

The declaration anchored the **Protect What's Human** campaign, [launched February 9, 2026](https://futureoflife.org/press-release/fli-launches-multimillion-dollar-nationwide-ai-regulation-campaign/) with up to $8 million in advertising and outreach across Iowa, Kentucky, Maine, Michigan, and North Carolina — targeting constituents outside the tech-policy centers to demonstrate that support for AI regulation is bipartisan and broad.
