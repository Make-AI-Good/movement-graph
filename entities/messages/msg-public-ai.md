# Public AI

**Public AI** is the civil society framing that AI systems and their underlying infrastructure — compute, models, data, and governance — should be owned, financed, and governed as public goods, provisioned like libraries, electricity, or roads rather than concentrated in a small number of private corporations. The framing proposes a structural alternative to the dominant model: not regulation layered on top of private AI monopolies, but publicly-owned and democratically-governed AI systems built alongside private ones.

## Intellectual origins

The framing's intellectual roots predate its organizational crystallization. Common Wealth UK published "Take Back the Future: Democratising Power over AI" in 2022, making the democratic-ownership case against concentrated AI infrastructure. The [AI Now Institute](../organizations/org-ai-now-institute.md) had made structural concentration at the Big Tech layer — compute, foundation models, distribution — the organizing theme of its policy research, arguing that a handful of companies were becoming gatekeepers to critical AI capabilities. Open Future in Europe was developing public-option frameworks for digital commons as generalizable policy tools, and the Berkman Klein Center at Harvard was pursuing AI commons concepts in parallel. These threads converged in summer 2023 when Joshua Tan (Metagov), Nick Garcia (Public Knowledge), and SJ Klein (Concordance Project) [met at the Internet Archive's DWeb Camp](https://www.aspendigital.org/blog/two-years-of-public-ai/) in the California redwoods — the decentralized-web community's annual gathering — where each had been independently working on overlapping parts of what they would name Public AI. They reconvened at a gathering they called "AI Palace" to draft a foundational whitepaper and launched the [Public AI Network](../organizations/org-public-ai-network.md)'s Slack community that fall with early support from the Rockefeller Foundation.

## Coalition formation and landmark publications

By late 2025, the [Public AI Network](../organizations/org-public-ai-network.md) had grown to over 350 members from more than 100 organizations worldwide, including Metagov, Aspen Digital, Open Future, [Mozilla Foundation](../organizations/org-mozilla-foundation.md), the Rockefeller Foundation, the Internet Archive, Public Knowledge, Code for Science and Society, Chatham House, and the Berkman Klein Center. The coalition's [first major public convening](https://www.aspendigital.org/blog/two-years-of-public-ai/) came in August 2024 — an all-day event at the Library of Congress co-hosted by over 60 organizations with 79 attendees. September 2024 produced the movement's two landmark publications. The [Public AI Network](../organizations/org-public-ai-network.md) released its foundational whitepaper establishing the conceptual framework. The [Mozilla Foundation](../organizations/org-mozilla-foundation.md) published [*Public AI: Making AI Work For Everyone, By Everyone*](https://www.mozillafoundation.org/en/research/library/public-ai/) (Nik Marda, Jasmine Sun, Mark Surman), which framed Public AI as "a robust ecosystem of initiatives that promote public goods, public orientation and public use throughout every step of AI development and deployment" — arguing that an AI ecosystem driven exclusively by market incentives would perpetually under-resource critical public-interest applications while over-investing in narrow profitable ones. The Mozilla report was released on September 30, 2024 alongside a Washington DC workshop co-organized with the Economic Security Project and Vanderbilt Policy Accelerator, which published companion papers: a national security case for AI infrastructure diversification, and a proposal to use the Defense Production Act to acquire semiconductors for public cloud infrastructure. In early 2025, the coalition convened a Public AI House gathering in Paris, timed around the AI Action Summit, carrying the framing into EU policy discussions; in 2025 Joshua Tan, Brandon Jackson, Robin Berjon, and economist Diane Coyle published an "Airbus for AI" proposal advocating for a European public AI institution modelled on cross-national aerospace cooperation.

## Policy materialization: CalCompute

The first major legislative enactment of the Public AI framing came in California. Governor Newsom [signed Senate Bill 53](https://www.californiacompute.org/history/) on September 29, 2025, establishing CalCompute — codified at Government Code Section 11546.8 — as a public cloud computing cluster within the California Government Operations Agency. CalCompute's mandate is equitable, secure, and sustainable AI computing access for universities, researchers, and public-interest organizations; its governance structure is a 14-member consortium with appointed seats for labor and public-interest representatives. The structural problem CalCompute names is the same one the framing has centered from the start: Amazon, Google, and Microsoft collectively control two-thirds of global cloud computing capacity, creating a structural barrier that prices out universities, public-interest researchers, and startups from meaningful AI development. A consortium framework report to the California Legislature is due January 1, 2027. CalCompute represents the first institutionalized attempt in the United States to translate the "AI as public infrastructure" argument from coalition advocacy into durable public-law form.
