# Stop AI Preemption

**Stop AI Preemption** is the civil society framing opposing federal action — legislative, executive, or regulatory — that would strip states of their authority to regulate artificial intelligence, either through a moratorium on state AI law enforcement, through preemptive federal legislation claiming the regulatory field, or through executive challenge to existing state laws. The framing pits a broad coalition of civil rights, consumer protection, and labor organizations against industry groups and the Trump administration, which argue that a patchwork of state AI laws creates unworkable compliance burdens and should give way to a minimally burdensome national standard.

## Origin: the Big Beautiful Bill fight

The framing's origin battle was the "One Big Beautiful Bill Act" of May–July 2025. On May 22, 2025, the House of Representatives [passed H.R. 1](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/07/alerts-practices-aiml-federal-ai-moratorium-dies-on-the-vine) with a provision imposing a 10-year federal moratorium on enforcement of any state or local legislation governing AI systems, AI models, or automated decision systems — a provision that would have preempted more than 1,000 pending state AI bills and invalidated existing laws in California, Colorado, New York, Illinois, and Utah. Forty-eight states and Puerto Rico had introduced AI regulation legislation in 2025 alone.

The civil society response organized in two tracks. On May 19, 2025, the Center for Democracy and Technology assembled a [coalition of 140+ civil rights and consumer protection organizations](https://cdt.org/insights/cdt-opposes-federal-preemption-of-state-ai-laws/) to sign a letter to Congress framing the provision as blocking enforcement of "common-sense laws developed in close dialogue with technologists and impacted communities" without providing any federal substitute. On June 4, 2025, [Americans for Responsible Innovation](../organizations/org-americans-for-responsible-innovation.md), Accountable Tech, and [Public Citizen](../organizations/org-public-citizen.md) [launched DefendAISafeguards.org](https://ari.us/coalition-petition-effort-opposes-state-ai-law-preemption/), a grassroots petition platform that gathered more than 25,000 signatures in its first two weeks — drawing on polling showing 81% of voters oppose decade-long restrictions on state AI laws and that concerns about insufficient federal AI regulation outnumber overregulation concerns by 3:1.

On June 12, 2025, the Leadership Conference on Civil and Human Rights led [more than 50 organizations](https://civilrights.org/resource/leadership-conference-letter-50-signatures-senate-opposing-ban-state-local-ai-laws/) — including the [ACLU](../organizations/org-aclu.md), NAACP, [EFF](../organizations/org-electronic-frontier-foundation.md), [Color of Change](../organizations/org-color-of-change.md), and [Public Citizen](../organizations/org-public-citizen.md) — in a Senate letter asking Congress to "strip from H.R. 1 the 10-year ban on the enforcement of state and local law related to artificial intelligence." The letter named concrete state laws at risk: facial recognition accuracy standards, political advertising disclosure requirements. On July 1, 2025, the Senate [voted 99–1](https://time.com/7299044/senators-reject-10-year-ban-on-state-level-ai-regulation-in-blow-to-big-tech/) — bipartisan amendment by Senators Blackburn, Cantwell, Markey, and Collins; only Senator Tillis dissented — to strip the moratorium. President Trump signed the bill on July 4, 2025, without the preemption provision.

## The ongoing federal push: NDAA, executive order, and the 2026 framework

The BBB defeat did not end the preemption effort. In [November 2025](https://www.americanprogress.org/article/moratoriums-and-federal-preemption-of-state-artificial-intelligence-laws-pose-serious-risks/), House Republican leaders considered the National Defense Authorization Act (NDAA) as a second legislative vehicle. On November 17, 2025, the [Congressional Progressive Caucus announced its official position](https://progressives.house.gov/2025/11/congressional-progressive-caucus-announces-official-position-opposing-preemption-of-state-ai-regulations-in-annual-pentagon-policy-bill) opposing any NDAA preemption language: "state laws are the only recourse American consumers have for protecting themselves and their data from Big Tech." The NDAA preemption attempt did not advance.

On December 11, 2025, President Trump signed [Executive Order 14365](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/12/eliminating-state-law-obstruction-of-national-artificial-intelligence-policy/), directing the Attorney General to establish an AI Litigation Task Force to challenge state AI laws that conflicted with the administration's national policy framework. The order named Colorado's algorithmic discrimination law as a target and identified conditions for federal legal action: state laws requiring AI models to alter their outputs, compelling disclosure alleged to violate First Amendment rights, or creating interstate commerce impediments. On March 20, 2026, the White House [published a National AI Legislative Framework](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/a-fresh-take/white-house-publishes-ai-legislative-framework-to-preempt-state-ai-regulation-102mnul) — legislative recommendations implementing the executive order's preemption architecture through statute, channeling federal oversight through existing sector-specific agencies and industry-led standards.

By mid-2026, the Stop AI Preemption coalition had been active for more than a year across three legislative vehicles (BBB, NDAA, the 2026 framework), one executive order, and a federal litigation task force, with the core advocacy infrastructure — [Americans for Responsible Innovation](../organizations/org-americans-for-responsible-innovation.md), [Public Citizen](../organizations/org-public-citizen.md), [Indivisible](../organizations/org-indivisible.md) chapters, civil rights organizations, and state-level attorneys general — holding together across each new escalation.

## Coalition character and core proposition

The framing's structural argument is: state AI laws represent the only durable enforcement mechanism currently available to consumers and communities — federal AI legislation has not passed, state attorneys general are the active enforcement actors, and preemption without a federal substitute creates a regulatory vacuum that primarily benefits the industry that lobbied for it. The 99–1 Senate vote validated the argument's cross-partisan reach: Heritage Foundation affiliates and the Rape, Abuse, and Incest National Network appeared in the same coalition record as [EFF](../organizations/org-electronic-frontier-foundation.md), [Color of Change](../organizations/org-color-of-change.md), and labor unions. The message's distinctive feature is its negative unity: constituencies with different stakes in AI governance — digital rights, racial justice, consumer protection, environmental, labor — converged on a single proposition that federal AI policy, whatever form it eventually takes, should not be used to remove the state-level safeguards that exist now.
