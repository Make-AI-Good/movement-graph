# Heising-Simons Foundation

The Heising-Simons Foundation is a family foundation [established in 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heising-Simons_Foundation) — originally as the Lemma Foundation — by Elizabeth (Liz) Simons and Mark Heising, based in Los Altos and San Francisco, California. Liz Simons, a former bilingual educator and [daughter of the late mathematician and hedge-fund manager Jim Simons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heising-Simons_Foundation), serves as board chair; Mark Heising, founder and managing director of Medley Partners, is board president. The two [signed the Giving Pledge in 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heising-Simons_Foundation). The foundation holds [approximately $872 million in assets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heising-Simons_Foundation) and made [total disbursements of $1.456 billion between 2007 and 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heising-Simons_Foundation), with annual grantmaking running at roughly $150 million — $152.1 million across 712 grants in 2023.

Heising-Simons organises its grantmaking across [five programme areas](https://www.hsfoundation.org/about/): Science, Education, Climate and Clean Energy, Human Rights, and the CEO Fund: Technology and Society. The first four are long-horizon institutional programmes: Science concentrates on astronomy, cosmology, fundamental physics, and climate-change science; Education on early childhood; Climate and Clean Energy on decarbonisation; Human Rights on global freedom-of-expression and civil-liberties work. The CEO Fund is a discretionary line the foundation makes available to its president to address a specific issue of their choosing — and in 2023 the foundation's president chose Technology and Society as that focus, pointing the CEO Fund explicitly at the promise and perils of AI.

## CEO Fund: Technology and Society

The [Technology and Society initiative](https://www.hsfoundation.org/ceo-fund-technology-and-society/) is framed to "harness technology's promise and mitigate its risks", with an equity premise: those most affected by technology's harms and inequities should have their perspectives and needs centred in the design and deployment of emerging technologies. This positions Heising-Simons's AI grantmaking at the algorithmic-accountability and digital-rights end of the field rather than the safety-research or technical-governance end — though the two overlap in its grantee mix.

The initiative's early grantees include two figures [TIME placed in its inaugural 100 Most Influential in Artificial Intelligence](https://www.hsfoundation.org/news-stories/technology-and-society-grantees-included-in-time-magazines-100-most-influential-people-in-artificial-intelligence/): the [Algorithmic Justice League](../organizations/org-algorithmic-justice-league.md) — founded by Joy Buolamwini, working on public awareness of AI impacts, algorithmic-bias accountability, and amplifying voices of communities harmed by AI systems — and the [DAIR Institute](../organizations/org-dair-institute.md), founded by Timnit Gebru, which conducts community-rooted research challenging AI hype and centring workers in the Global South whose labour underwrites AI systems. [WITNESS](../organizations/org-witness.md) received a $300,000 grant to build technological capacity for grassroots human-rights defenders — activists, journalists, and lawyers who use video and digital evidence in accountability work. [The Markup](../organizations/org-the-markup.md) received [$400,000 for general operations](https://www.insidephilanthropy.com/home/2024-2-27-one-year-in-heising-simons-president-is-zeroing-in-on-the-perils-and-promise-of-ai) to support its investigative journalism challenging technology in the public interest. [EPIC](../organizations/org-epic.md) received [$300,000 over three years](https://epic.org/press-release-epic-partners-with-the-heising-simons-foundation-to-promote-a-human-rights-first-approach-to-emerging-technology/) for its AI and Human Rights and Consumer Privacy teams to advance privacy rights in the AI ecosystem through advocacy, research, and litigation.

The CEO Fund also directed [$400,000 to the Democracy Frontlines Fund](https://www.insidephilanthropy.com/home/2024-2-27-one-year-in-heising-simons-president-is-zeroing-in-on-the-perils-and-promise-of-ai), a collaborative supporting Black-led organisations and social movements — a signal that the Technology and Society grantmaking strategy runs through civil-society infrastructure, not pure research, and frames technology harm as structurally racialised.

## Public Interest AI initiative

In November 2023, at the UK AI Safety Summit in London, Heising-Simons joined nine other philanthropies to [announce a new initiative](https://www.fordfoundation.org/news-and-stories/news-and-press/news/philanthropies-launch-new-initiative-to-ensure-ai-advances-the-public-interest/) to ensure AI advances the public interest — a collective commitment of more than $200 million, announced alongside United States Vice President Kamala Harris as part of the policy agenda flanking the summit. The co-founders are the [Ford Foundation](fund-ford-foundation.md), the [MacArthur Foundation](fund-macarthur-foundation.md), the [Kapor Foundation](fund-kapor-foundation.md), the [Mozilla Foundation](fund-mozilla-foundation.md), [Omidyar Network](fund-omidyar-network.md), the [Open Society Foundations](fund-open-society-foundations.md), the David and Lucile Packard Foundation, the Democracy Fund, and the Wallace Global Fund.

The initiative's five stated funding priorities are: protecting democracy and the rights and freedoms of all people, including defending free and fair elections while combating disinformation; developing inclusive, rights-respecting AI governance frameworks; guarding against harmful impacts to historically marginalised communities; promoting AI safety and responsible AI use; and ensuring AI's economic benefits flow broadly. The coalition agreed to coordinate grantmaking and to convene a cross-sector group to take stock of progress.

## Position in the funder landscape

Heising-Simons occupies a distinct position among foundations funding the AI-good movement: it is a mid-sized US family foundation — asset-scale comparable to Kapor and the Democracy Fund, smaller than the [Ford Foundation](fund-ford-foundation.md), [MacArthur Foundation](fund-macarthur-foundation.md), or [Hewlett Foundation](fund-hewlett-foundation.md) — that has moved fast and explicitly into AI harm mitigation via a single flexible vehicle shaped by presidential discretion. Its grantee mix leans toward algorithmic accountability and civil-rights-adjacent organisations (AJL, DAIR, EPIC), investigative journalism (The Markup), and grassroots-rights-technology infrastructure (WITNESS) — reflecting the equity-forward framing of the Technology and Society initiative. The CEO Fund structure gives it flexibility to back early-stage and community-centred work that more institutionalised programme vehicles sometimes struggle to reach; its co-founding of the ten-philanthropy Public Interest AI coalition places it in the coordinated philanthropic infrastructure through which several of the corpus's other major funders also work.
