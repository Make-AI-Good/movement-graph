# Andrew W. Mellon Foundation

The Andrew W. Mellon Foundation is a private [New York City](https://www.mellon.org/)–based foundation [established on 30 June 1969](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_W._Mellon_Foundation) through the consolidation of two earlier charitable endowments — the Avalon Foundation and the Old Dominion Foundation — created by Paul Mellon and Ailsa Mellon Bruce, children of the financier and former US Treasury Secretary Andrew W. Mellon. [The foundation began with $273 million in assets](https://www.mellon.org/financials), and today holds an endowment of [approximately $7.8 billion](https://www.mellon.org/financials) — making it the largest private funder of arts and humanities in the United States. Elizabeth Alexander, the poet, scholar, and former Yale University professor who read her poem at Barack Obama's first inauguration, [has led the foundation as president since 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_W._Mellon_Foundation). In 2024, Mellon [awarded approximately $555 million across roughly 699 grants](https://www.mellon.org/financials), for a total of [more than $9.1 billion in cumulative grantmaking since 1969](https://www.mellon.org/financials).

Mellon's grantmaking is organized into five program areas — Arts and Culture, Higher Learning, Humanities in Place, Public Knowledge, and Presidential Initiatives — all [grounded in the mission to "build just communities enriched by meaning and guided by critical thinking, where ideas and imagination can thrive"](https://www.mellon.org/). The foundation's primary constituencies are colleges and universities, museums and performing arts organizations, libraries and archives, and artists and humanists — the non-technical communities whose engagement with democratic culture and meaning-making forms Mellon's distinctive posture in the philanthropic ecosystem. Since June 2020, Mellon has explicitly oriented its grantmaking toward social justice.

## AI-good engagement

Mellon's entry into AI-good philanthropy is rooted in its humanities-and-equity identity rather than in technology or policy infrastructure. The foundation's position: humanistic knowledge — the capacity to examine how AI affects democratic life, social inequality, and human meaning — is precisely what is missing from AI governance, and that absence has consequences for the communities Mellon has served for five decades.

**Humanity AI co-founding.** On [14 October 2025](https://www.mellon.org/news/humanity-ai-commits-500m-build-people-centered-future-for-ai), Mellon joined nine other foundations as a co-founding member of [Humanity AI](fund-humanity-ai.md), a $500 million five-year philanthropic collaborative designed to ensure AI serves the public good and that communities beyond technology hubs co-determine how AI is built, governed, and used. The ten co-founders — alongside Mellon: the Doris Duke Foundation, [Ford Foundation](fund-ford-foundation.md), [Kapor Foundation](fund-kapor-foundation.md), [Lumina Foundation](fund-lumina-foundation.md), [MacArthur Foundation](fund-macarthur-foundation.md) and [Omidyar Network](fund-omidyar-network.md) ([co-chairs](https://www.macfound.org/press/press-releases/humanity-ai-commits-500-million-to-build-a-people-centered-future-for-ai)), [Mozilla Foundation](fund-mozilla-foundation.md), the David and Lucile Packard Foundation, and the Siegel Family Endowment — commit funding across five program domains: Democracy, Education, Humanities and Culture, Labor and Economy, and Security. Mellon's co-founder contribution is most distinctly positioned in the **Humanities and Culture domain**, where artists, cultural workers, and humanists are the intended non-AI audiences being engaged in shaping AI governance through cultural production and humanistic inquiry.

**Humanity AI inaugural grants.** The collaborative's initial [$18 million in pooled grants](https://www.mellon.org/news/humanity-ai-announces-more-than-18-million-in-new-grants) included $8 million to nine inaugural grantees — [AI Now Institute](../organizations/org-ai-now-institute.md), Center for Democracy and Technology, Council on Foreign Relations LEAD AI, DAIR Institute, Partnership on AI, TechEquity, Kinfolk Tech, Pulitzer Center, and Student Defense, each receiving $500,000 — for work spanning [AI accountability, labor rights, democratic institutions, journalism, and education](https://www.mellon.org/news/humanity-ai-announces-more-than-18-million-in-new-grants). A separate [$3 million AI Civics Initiative](https://www.mellon.org/news/humanity-ai-announces-more-than-18-million-in-new-grants) led by [Data & Society](../organizations/org-data-and-society.md) with the Digital Public Library of America as anchor partner [explicitly brings communities together in libraries to ensure grassroots participation in directing AI's creation and deployment](https://www.mellon.org/news/humanity-ai-announces-more-than-18-million-in-new-grants) — a library-anchored community participation model that sits squarely in Mellon's home domain. A further $10 million open call is forthcoming.

**'Unruly Intelligences' grantmaking.** Independently of Humanity AI, Mellon has incorporated AI into its Higher Learning Program through an ['Unruly Intelligences' focus area](https://www.mellon.org/article/higher-learning-open-call-for-concepts-2026) for the 2026 grant cycle — offering $250,000–$500,000 over four years to humanities scholars at accredited US liberal arts institutions. The strand [positions humanities scholars to examine how generative, predictive, and agentic AI intersects with democratic processes, human subjectivities, and social inequalities](https://www.mellon.org/article/higher-learning-open-call-for-concepts-2026), and asks them to make [practical, informed recommendations about how to regulate and/or adopt AI in learning, work, and public life](https://www.mellon.org/article/higher-learning-open-call-for-concepts-2026). Projects may involve community collaboration and must produce pathways to informing campus and wider policies. This is institutional-capacity grantmaking — recipients are universities — but the outputs target AI regulation and the non-specialist public's relationship with AI directly.
