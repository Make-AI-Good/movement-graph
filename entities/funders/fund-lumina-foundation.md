# Lumina Foundation

Lumina Foundation is a private [Indianapolis, Indiana](https://www.luminafoundation.org/)–based foundation established in [August 2000](https://www.luminafoundation.org/about/lumina-foundation-faq/how-was-lumina-foundation-founded/) as a conversion foundation — formed when USA Group, an Indianapolis-based nonprofit student-loan servicer, [sold most of its operating assets to Sallie Mae](https://www.luminafoundation.org/about/lumina-foundation-faq/how-was-lumina-foundation-founded/) and directed the [$770 million in proceeds into a new independent foundation](https://www.luminafoundation.org/about/lumina-foundation-faq/how-was-lumina-foundation-founded/). Renamed [Lumina Foundation for Education in February 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumina_Foundation) and now operating as Lumina Foundation, it holds approximately $1.5 billion in assets. [Martha Lamkin served as first president (2000–2007)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumina_Foundation); [Jamie Merisotis has led the foundation since 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumina_Foundation). Lumina has [no ties to the student loan industry](https://www.luminafoundation.org/about/lumina-foundation-faq/how-was-lumina-foundation-founded/) — it received Sallie Mae stock in the conversion and promptly sold it, operating as a purely independent grantmaking foundation since its founding.

Lumina's mission is structured around a single national ambition: [by 2040, 75 percent of adults in the US labor force will hold college degrees or other credentials of value leading to economic prosperity](https://www.luminafoundation.org/). The foundation works through grants to community colleges, public universities, minority-serving institutions, and workforce-training programs, with particular emphasis on [first-generation students, students of color, and low-income and working adults](https://www.luminafoundation.org/news-and-views/building-talent-for-an-ai-driven-future/) — the populations whose economic trajectories are most exposed to labor-market disruption. Lumina is a US-domestic funder; its grantmaking is concentrated in the United States and its primary audiences are American students, workers, and education institutions.

## AI-good engagement

Lumina's entry into the AI-good field flows directly from its core workforce mission. [AI will reshape the demand for skills, the pathways to economic security, and the meaning of "credentials with value"](https://www.luminafoundation.org/news-and-views/humanity-ai-putting-people-at-the-center-of-the-ai-revolution/) — which is why Lumina frames AI governance as inseparable from its education mandate. The foundation positions itself on the side of the non-technologist communities most affected by AI-driven labor transformation: [AI could hollow out the middle class or help rebuild it, depending on how it is deployed](https://www.luminafoundation.org/news-and-views/humanity-ai-putting-people-at-the-center-of-the-ai-revolution/), and Lumina's role is to build the workforce-and-credential infrastructure that shapes which outcome prevails. This framing — AI governance as an education and equity question, not a technology question — is what distinguishes Lumina's AI posture from that of digital-rights or algorithmic-accountability funders in the corpus.

**Humanity AI co-founding.** On [14 October 2025](https://www.macfound.org/press/press-releases/humanity-ai-commits-500-million-to-build-a-people-centered-future-for-ai), Lumina joined nine other foundations as a co-founding member of [Humanity AI](fund-humanity-ai.md), a ten-foundation $500 million five-year collaborative designed to ensure AI serves the public good and that communities beyond technology hubs co-determine how AI is built, governed, and used. The coalition — co-chaired by the [MacArthur Foundation](fund-macarthur-foundation.md) and [Omidyar Network](fund-omidyar-network.md) and managed by Rockefeller Philanthropy Advisors as fiscal sponsor — operates across five programmatic domains: Democracy, Education, Humanities and Culture, Labor and Economy, and Security. Lumina's Education and Labor and Economy domains map directly onto its existing workforce-credential infrastructure; the Humanity AI co-founding is the foundation's single largest AI-aligned commitment and its formal entry into coordinated AI civil-society philanthropy.

**AI literacy grantmaking.** Beyond the coalition vehicle, Lumina funds direct AI-literacy work with non-technical communities. A [$200,000 "Building Bridges of Knowledge" grant to CUNY](https://www.cuny.edu/news/cuny-receives-200000-from-lumina-foundation-to-create-teaching-tools-on-ethical-use-of-ai/) supported 30 faculty members across five academic disciplines — education, STEM, social sciences, health, and humanities — in [developing hands-on AI-ethics instructional materials during summer 2024](https://www.cuny.edu/news/cuny-receives-200000-from-lumina-foundation-to-create-teaching-tools-on-ethical-use-of-ai/). Materials were tested in fall courses and released as open educational resources through OpenEd CUNY. The approach [allows learners across non-technical disciplines to develop AI literacy through critical engagement — examining biases in classroom chatbots, distinguishing evidence-based from AI-generated false medical narratives — without requiring specialized technical backgrounds](https://www.cuny.edu/news/cuny-receives-200000-from-lumina-foundation-to-create-teaching-tools-on-ethical-use-of-ai/). The CUNY grant is a worked example of the engagement pattern the corpus tracks: students and educators in ordinary disciplines building critical AI literacy rather than technical training.

**Workforce strategy.** Through [FutureReady States](https://www.luminafoundation.org/news-and-views/building-talent-for-an-ai-driven-future/), Lumina supports state-level initiatives that build accessible credential pathways for working adults navigating AI-driven labor-market change. The foundation's ["human work" framing](https://www.luminafoundation.org/news-and-views/building-talent-for-an-ai-driven-future/) holds that AI's displacement of routine tasks raises the value of distinctly human capabilities — creativity, ethical judgment, compassion, complex problem-solving — and that credential systems recognizing those capabilities are the structural response to AI-driven displacement. [Jamie Merisotis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumina_Foundation) addressed AI's challenge to higher education at the Economic Club of Indiana in February 2026, framing the stakes as whether AI-era talent systems will extend economic opportunity broadly or concentrate it further.

## Position in the corpus funder slice

Against the corpus's funder admission test (2026-06-05 edge case: intersection of AI throughline AND small-d / grassroots engagement), Lumina passes both axes. The AI throughline is explicit: Humanity AI co-founding is a nine-figure commitment to AI civil-society philanthropy, and the CUNY grant is direct AI-literacy investment in non-technical educational communities. The grassroots engagement axis runs through Lumina's founding architecture: first-generation students, working adults, students of color, and low-income learners — the non-AI publics most exposed to AI's labor-market effects — are the foundation's primary constituency, not a programme add-on. The corpus's 2026-05-08 engagement-layer test frames the question as whether non-AI publics are being engaged in work to shape AI; Lumina's workforce-and-credential infrastructure, now explicitly reoriented around AI preparedness, is the engagement mechanism.

Within the funder slice, Lumina occupies a distinctive slot: the first large US education foundation whose AI-good work runs through the workforce-and-credential layer rather than through digital-rights, algorithmic-accountability, or journalism tracks. The [Ford Foundation](fund-ford-foundation.md), [MacArthur Foundation](fund-macarthur-foundation.md), and [Omidyar Network](fund-omidyar-network.md) all share the Humanity AI coalition but carry broader AI-good portfolios; Lumina's is narrower and more specifically anchored in education and workforce credentialing as the structural hinge between AI's deployment and its effects on working Americans. Its admission also represents a new entry-point category for the corpus funder slice: the education-sector major foundation joining AI civil-society philanthropy via the workforce-disruption threat, rather than via digital-rights or civic-governance tracks.
