# The Rockefeller Foundation

The Rockefeller Foundation is one of the oldest and largest private philanthropic foundations in the United States, [founded in 1913](https://www.rockefellerfoundation.org/about-us/) by John D. Rockefeller Sr. and incorporated by an act of the New York legislature. Headquartered in New York City, with additional offices in Washington D.C., Nairobi, Bangkok, Bogotá, and the Bellagio Center in northern Italy, the Foundation works globally across Africa, Asia, Latin America, the Caribbean, and the United States on challenges in health, food systems, energy, and economic opportunity. Its [endowment stood at $6.3 billion in 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Foundation) with annual expenses of approximately $568 million. The current president is Dr. Rajiv J. Shah, who took the role in March 2017 and is leading the Foundation's five-year, $1 billion commitment to advancing economic opportunity and averting the worst impacts of climate change.

Within the AI-good landscape, the Foundation operates across four linked program tracks. [AI for Development](https://www.rockefellerfoundation.org/initiatives/ai-for-development/) applies AI to the Foundation's core program areas — food security, health systems, energy access, and climate adaptation — with an equity focus on underserved populations including rural farming communities across the Global South. The [AI Readiness Project](https://www.rockefellerfoundation.org/news/rockefeller-foundation-and-center-for-civic-futures-launch-ai-readiness-project-to-build-state-capacity-for-responsible-ai/), launched jointly with the Center for Civic Futures, has invested more than $18 million in helping state, territorial, and Tribal Nation governments adopt AI responsibly, convening Chief AI Officers and senior officials across all 50 states in working groups focused on agentic AI, workforce impacts, and evaluation and monitoring, with a Government AI Knowledge Hub to follow in 2026. Alongside these institutional tracks, the Foundation has made targeted grants directly to civil society organizations working to counter algorithmic bias: a [$300,000 grant to Black in AI](https://www.rockefellerfoundation.org/news/the-rockefeller-foundation-announces-nearly-500k-to-combat-ai-bias-and-discrimination/) to expand representation of Black researchers in AI development and support underrepresented communities in re-evaluating training data for bias, and a grant of nearly $200,000 to the [Distributed AI Research Institute](../organizations/org-dair-institute.md), founded by Timnit Gebru, to document AI's harms on marginalized communities and center people and communities in AI research from inception. The Foundation is also named as an institutional funder of the [Algorithmic Justice League](../organizations/org-algorithmic-justice-league.md), [Joy Buolamwini](../persons/person-joy-buolamwini.md)'s digital advocacy organization; alongside Ford, MacArthur, Mozilla, and the Sloan Foundation, Rockefeller [contributed to at least $1.9 million in combined institutional support](https://www.influencewatch.org/non-profit/algorithmic-justice-league/) for AJL's work amplifying communities most affected by AI bias.

## Good Jobs for America

In [April 2026](https://www.rockefellerfoundation.org/news/rockefeller-foundation-launches-100m-good-jobs-for-america-strategy/) the Foundation announced a three-year, $100 million "Good Jobs for America" strategy framed explicitly around the disruption of U.S. labor markets by AI. The strategy aims to connect workers in the country's approximately 250 most economically distressed communities to stable employment in sectors with strong job-growth potential — healthcare and the care economy, energy transition, food systems, and AI-enabled industries — targeting the creation of roughly 1.6 million additional jobs and benefiting 10 to 20 million people. The rollout begins with 20 to 30 pilot communities in the first year, with scaling to the full 250 in the back half of the strategy. The Foundation's approach emphasizes localized implementation — testing real-world solutions in specific communities, removing barriers such as childcare and credentialing, adapting proven models to local conditions, and embedding successful approaches into public policy and employer practices rather than maintaining permanent philanthropic dependency.

## Relationship to the AI-good movement

The Rockefeller Foundation's AI portfolio occupies a distinctive position among major US philanthropies. The direct grants to DAIR and Black in AI, and the AJL relationship, place it among the small set of large foundations that have directly resourced civil society organizations doing hands-on work to expose algorithmic bias and document AI harms on marginalized communities. The Good Jobs for America strategy is unusual among major foundation AI interventions in locating the site of action in distressed communities themselves — with workers facing AI-driven labor-market disruption as the primary constituency — rather than in the think tanks, standards bodies, or regulatory processes that most AI philanthropy funds. While the AI Readiness Project's government-capacity shape is more institutional, the Foundation's overall AI portfolio consistently returns to communities most affected by AI's disruption of labor and social systems as the relevant unit of concern.
