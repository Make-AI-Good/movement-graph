# Pivotal Ventures

Pivotal Ventures is the investment and philanthropic arm of Pivotal, an [umbrella organization founded in 2015](https://capitalresearch.org/article/melinda-french-gates-and-her-pivotal-philanthropies/) by Melinda French Gates to accelerate social progress for women and young people in the United States and globally. The structure reflects deliberate operational flexibility: Pivotal Ventures LLC is the for-profit investment vehicle; alongside it operate the Pivotal Initiatives Fund (a 501(c)(4) nonprofit for advocacy work) and [five 501(c)(3) private foundations](https://capitalresearch.org/article/melinda-french-gates-and-her-pivotal-philanthropies/) for philanthropic grantmaking. In practice the whole ecosystem — grantmaking, investments, advocacy, and partnerships — operates under the single "Pivotal" brand, with French Gates as Director and Brooke Anderson as President.

Pivotal's programmatic portfolio spans Women's Power and reproductive rights; Women and Tech Innovation; Youth Mental Health; Women in the Workplace; and Caregiving and paid leave. AI has emerged as a [distinct cross-cutting priority](https://www.reworked.co/employee-experience/how-melinda-french-gates-150m-investment-is-shaping-ai-and-workforce-equity/): the organization aims to "build the pipeline of women leaders shaping AI and ensure companies deploying AI are maximizing its benefits and minimizing its harms" — framing AI as both an equity concern (who shapes the technology) and an accountability concern (civil society's role in governing AI harms).

## Women and Tech Innovation — the AI subprogram

Pivotal's Women and Tech Innovation focus area includes a dedicated "Unlocking AI's Potential" subprogram, part of a [$150 million commitment](https://experiencepoweredby.northeastern.edu/grant-from-pivotal-ventures-allows-northeasterns-cic-to-expand-ai-and-computing-education-nationally/) to remove barriers women face in the workplace. Approximately [$50 million of that commitment is AI-earmarked](https://www.reworked.co/employee-experience/how-melinda-french-gates-150m-investment-is-shaping-ai-and-workforce-equity/).

Named AI-focused grantees include **Break Through Tech AI** — the national expansion of Cornell Tech's WiTNY initiative — which works to increase women's representation by expanding access to AI education and mentorship across US universities, backed by a [$50 million GET Cities investment](https://tech.cornell.edu/news/witny-initiative-announces-national-expansion-to-recruit-more-women-in-tech-formally-becomes-break-through-tech/) as part of the Gender Equality in Tech Cities initiative. Northeastern University's [Center for Inclusive Computing](https://experiencepoweredby.northeastern.edu/grant-from-pivotal-ventures-allows-northeasterns-cic-to-expand-ai-and-computing-education-nationally/) received a second Pivotal grant (building on initial backing from the CIC's 2019 launch) to develop AI tracks within undergraduate computing degrees and create pathways for non-computing majors to pursue master's degrees in AI. The CIC's 97-institution network covered [23.3 percent of all US undergraduate computing graduates in 2023](https://experiencepoweredby.northeastern.edu/grant-from-pivotal-ventures-allows-northeasterns-cic-to-expand-ai-and-computing-education-nationally/) and has driven 136 percent growth in Black/African American women and 116 percent growth in Hispanic women majoring in computing since 2019 — grounding the pipeline work in racial and gender equity, not just numerical targets.

## WIN Challenge — the workplace-AI layer

In September 2025, [Pivotal co-launched the Workplace Innovation Now (WIN) Challenge](https://www.aspendigital.org/press-release/announcing-win-challenge-grant-competition/) with the Aspen Institute's Aspen Digital program — a $60 million nationwide grant competition seeking scalable solutions to workplace barriers disproportionately affecting women. The challenge divides $20 million equally across three pillars: Culture & Practices, Narrative, and AI. The AI pillar specifically targets how AI can be deployed to "build a workplace that supports everyone," addressing equity in how AI systems are introduced into workplaces. [Individual grants range from $2.5 million to $5 million](https://www.aspendigital.org/press-release/announcing-win-challenge-grant-competition/), supporting up to eight awardees per pillar. Eligibility is [open to 501(c)(3) nonprofits, nonprofit coalitions, and other grant-eligible entities](https://www.aspendigital.org/press-release/announcing-win-challenge-grant-competition/), foregrounding civil society organizations over corporate actors. Awards were expected in fall 2026.

## Position in the corpus funder slice

The AI throughline is explicit and programmatically structured: approximately $50 million of the $150M commitment is AI-earmarked; a dedicated "Unlocking AI's Potential" subprogram exists within Women and Tech Innovation; and the WIN Challenge's AI pillar channels $20 million specifically toward AI equity in workplaces.

The WIN Challenge is more direct: its AI pillar explicitly targets 501(c)(3) nonprofits working with workers who "have not typically seen themselves as having a role in shaping AI," and its eligibility structure routes funds specifically to civil society rather than companies. The operative question — are non-insiders being engaged in determining how AI is built and deployed? — is answered affirmatively by both programs.
