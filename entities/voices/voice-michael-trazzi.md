# Michaël Trazzi

Michaël Trazzi is the French documentary filmmaker, former AI safety researcher, and direct-action organizer who became the most visible street-level organizer of the AI moratorium movement in the United States in 2025–2026 (see [Person entry](../persons/person-michael-trazzi.md)). Operating through two overlapping platforms — [The Inside View](https://theinsideview.ai/about.html), a media company producing AI-safety documentaries and interview content, and [Stop the AI Race](../organizations/org-stop-the-ai-race.md), a grassroots organizing operation — Trazzi developed and deployed a distinctive voice that bridges the insider AI-safety research community's concerns with the rhetoric and tactics of street-level democratic protest. He is tracked here as a Voice because his public output — a conditional-pause coordination framing the movement has adopted, a documentary trilogy documenting the AI-pause movement's own emergence, a hunger strike that generated front-page coverage across three continents and elicited an on-the-record response from a major AI CEO, and two San Francisco marches that grew from 200 to 400 participants in four months — constitutes a mode of movement communication that no other figure in the corpus currently anchors.

## The Inside View: documentary evidence for the AI-pause case

[The Inside View](https://theinsideview.ai/about.html) is a media company Trazzi founded to produce documentary content about AI safety, accumulating an [archive of over 40 interviews](https://theinsideview.ai/about.html) with AI safety researchers, alignment researchers, and movement organizers. Reaching [50,000+ followers with 7M+ views across YouTube, TikTok, and Twitter](https://theinsideview.ai/about.html), The Inside View functions as both a documentary record of the AI-pause discourse and a public-outreach channel making research-community voices accessible to general audiences — including interviews with Owain Evans on situational awareness and Evan Hubinger on training deceptive language models.

The three feature documentaries mark the movement's major inflection points:

- ***2024: The Year of Artificial General Intelligence*** (2024) — Trazzi's first documentary short, covering the AI capability discourse of the year and establishing the timeline-imminence frame that motivates the conditional-pause demand.
- ***SB-1047: The Battle for the Future of AI*** (2025) — A [short documentary drawing on over 20 interviews with politicians, activists, and researchers](https://theinsideview.ai/about.html) covering California's AI safety bill SB-1047 and the movement that formed around it — the first major US state attempt to impose safety requirements on frontier AI developers.
- ***The Hunger Strike to Stop the AI Race*** (2026) — A documentary of Trazzi's own September 2025 action outside Google DeepMind's London office, framing the event as a founding movement moment rather than a biographical record; the film is the primary narrative artifact of Stop the AI Race's origin.

## The conditional-pause demand: a coordination framing

Trazzi's [core strategic contribution](https://aifront-page.com/stop-the-ai-race-protest-michael-trazzi-interview/) to the AI-pause movement is a specific reframing of the *form* the demand should take: rather than calling on individual labs to pause unilaterally — which they argue is competitively suicidal — the demand is that every major AI CEO publicly commit to pausing frontier model development *if every other major company credibly does the same*. The conditional structure turns the competitive-pressure rationale against itself, seeking a mutual-commitment mechanism rather than unilateral restraint.

He operationalizes this through a [three-stage strategic framework](https://aifront-page.com/stop-the-ai-race-protest-michael-trazzi-interview/): securing company-level CEO commitments; then achieving US-China geopolitical agreement; then reaching an internationally coordinated pause. The demand is deliberately minimal — "just say you'll pause if everyone pauses" — making it difficult for a CEO to publicly refuse without confirming that competitive pressure, not safety concern, is the actual constraint.

Trazzi has been publicly critical when major AI labs have retreated from conditional-pause positions. After Anthropic dropped its Responsible Scaling Policy commitments in February 2026, he [explicitly named Dario Amodei](https://aifront-page.com/stop-the-ai-race-protest-michael-trazzi-interview/), stating that Amodei "could be more public about wanting to pause if everyone else pauses" — documenting the gap between stated safety concern and conditional-commitment refusal as the movement's central critique of the frontier labs.

## The hunger strike and the DeepMind confrontation

On [5 September 2025](https://www.dazeddigital.com/life-culture/article/68614/1/meet-protesters-hunger-strike-prevent-ai-catastrophe-google-deepmind-london), Trazzi began a hunger strike outside Google DeepMind's London office — the demand addressed directly to CEO Demis Hassabis: publicly commit to halting frontier model development if all other major AI companies worldwide do the same. The action lasted approximately one week, ending after [Trazzi fainted and withdrew on medical advice](https://www.dazeddigital.com/life-culture/article/68614/1/meet-protesters-hunger-strike-prevent-ai-catastrophe-google-deepmind-london). [Denys Sheremet](https://www.dazeddigital.com/life-culture/article/68614/1/meet-protesters-hunger-strike-prevent-ai-catastrophe-google-deepmind-london), co-founder of Together Against AI, traveled from Amsterdam to join the strike and continued after Trazzi withdrew.

The action generated the most concentrated mainstream media coverage any single individual's AI-pause activism had received to that date: [The Telegraph, The Verge, Sky News, and Le Monde](https://theinsideview.ai/about.html) covered the London action, and the parallel Guido Reichstadter strike outside Anthropic's San Francisco headquarters and the subsequent Stop the AI Race marches drew reporting from [the New York Times, Washington Post, and The Atlantic](https://pauseai.info/stop-the-ai-race-march). The direct confrontation produced its intended result: Hassabis [addressed the conditional-pause question on the record](https://michaeltrazzi.com/). The exchange became the founding event documented in Trazzi's third documentary.

## Stop the AI Race: organizing at scale

Following the London strike, Trazzi built [Stop the AI Race](../organizations/org-stop-the-ai-race.md) into the central coordinator of US AI moratorium street activism through two major San Francisco marches.

The [March 21, 2026 march](https://decrypt.co/362129/protesters-rally-openai-anthropic-xai-offices) drew approximately 200 participants from Anthropic's offices to OpenAI's to xAI's, organized with a coalition including the Machine Intelligence Research Institute, [PauseAI](../organizations/org-pauseai.md), [Stop AI](../organizations/org-stop-ai.md), QuitGPT, and Evitable. [Speakers included Holly Elmore](https://pauseai.info/stop-the-ai-race-march) (then PauseAI US Executive Director), Dr. David Krueger (University of Montreal AI professor and Evitable founder), Nate Soares (MIRI President), and Will Fithian (UC Berkeley professor).

The [July 11, 2026 march](https://www.trendingtopics.eu/stop-the-ai-race-hundreds-march-against-openai-anthropic-and-google-deepmind-in-san-francisco/) drew approximately [400 participants](https://www.trendingtopics.eu/stop-the-ai-race-hundreds-march-against-openai-anthropic-and-google-deepmind-in-san-francisco/) across a [13-organization coalition](https://www.trendingtopics.eu/stop-the-ai-race-hundreds-march-against-openai-anthropic-and-google-deepmind-in-san-francisco/) that expanded beyond the AI-safety organizational network to include the National Union of Healthcare Workers and Food and Water Watch. The march ran from OpenAI's Mission Bay headquarters to Anthropic's SoMa offices to Google DeepMind's Rincon Park location, with rallies at each stop; it was [described as the largest AI protest in American history](https://www.dailycal.org/news/largest-ai-protest-in-american-history-sees-hundreds-march-on-downtown-san-francisco/article_8253b059-e793-4de4-a012-cc20ddc5ad69.html) to that date.

## Why this is a Voice entry

A Voice entry is created here, rather than additional structure on the [Person entry](../persons/person-michael-trazzi.md), because Trazzi's public-facing output is itself the object the corpus needs to track: the conditional-pause framing (not merely his personal position but a specific coordination structure the movement has adopted and that other organizations in the corpus now deploy); the documentary trilogy constituting the AI-pause movement's primary self-documentation artifacts; and the hunger strike and march series as direct-action performances that generated and amplified mainstream press coverage at a scale no other AI moratorium organizer in the US achieved in 2025–2026. Where the AI-pause movement's other corpus voices — [Andrea Miotti](voice-andrea-miotti.md) and [ControlAI](../organizations/org-controlai.md) in the parliamentary-organizing track, [Daniel Kokotajlo](voice-daniel-kokotajlo.md) in the forecasting-and-scenario track — operate primarily through policy channels and published research, Trazzi's register is street-level democratic protest combined with self-documentation through documentary film: the movement's own image of itself marching and striking, produced by the movement for the movement.
