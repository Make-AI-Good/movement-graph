# Luc Triangle

Luc Triangle is General Secretary of the [International Trade Union Confederation](../organizations/org-ituc.md) (ITUC), [representing nearly 200 million workers](https://www.ioe-emp.org/news/details/ioe-ituc-joint-statement-on-the-just-transition-work-programme-at-cop29) through 346 affiliated organisations in 171 countries and territories. He is tracked here as a Voice because his public output since taking the ITUC leadership in 2023 has established an identifiable personal voice in global AI governance deliberations — at the World Economic Forum, the Paris AI Action Summit, and the ILO's binding standard-setting process on platform work. While the [ITUC's own entry](../organizations/org-ituc.md) tracks the confederation as an institution, this Voice entry tracks Triangle himself as the personified articulator of the labor movement's AI governance demands: the individual whose named statements, summit appearances, and campaigning language have established the frame that AI governance is a labor rights issue, not merely a technology policy question. Affiliations and career history are in the linked [Person entry](../persons/person-luc-triangle.md).

His register is distinct from the research-accountability voices in this corpus. [Timnit Gebru](../persons/person-timnit-gebru.md) anchors the algorithmic-harms and structural-discrimination register through DAIR's research program; [Joy Buolamwini](../persons/person-joy-buolamwini.md) the computational fairness register through Gender Shades and the Algorithmic Justice League; [Alondra Nelson](../persons/person-alondra-nelson.md) the policy-architecture-as-civil-rights register through the AI Bill of Rights and the AI Democracy Projects. Triangle anchors the *organized-labor-as-AI-governance-actor* register: the voice claiming that workers — not just citizens, consumers, or civil society organizations — have the standing to demand rights inside AI-governed workplaces, and that their unions have the institutional infrastructure to win those rights through collective bargaining and international standard-setting rather than through litigation or advocacy alone.

## Voice anchor

This Voice closes the **global-labor AI-governance** gap in the corpus. The corpus has the ITUC as an institution and its campaign wins (the ILO platform work convention, the ITUC report on AI and trade unions) but not the individual who voices the labor movement's AI governance demands in high-level forums. Triangle's named statements have been the most-quoted expression of organized labor's position on AI regulation, and his presence at WEF, the Paris AI Action Summit, and ILO tripartite negotiations makes him the labor movement's face in the AI governance architecture. Without this entry, the corpus's record of who speaks for labor in AI governance deliberations is incomplete.

## Signature contributions

**"Too often artificial intelligence is being deployed not as a tool for progress but as a weapon against workers."** Triangle's [International Workers' Memorial Day campaign](https://www.ituc-csi.org/International-Workers-Memorial-Day-2025) (28 April 2025) used this frame to expose the concrete harms of algorithmic management: content moderators dismissed after organizing against AI-managed workloads; nurses facing AI-controlled shift apps creating dangerous conditions; workers subjected to constant surveillance, unrealistic productivity targets, and automated disciplinary decisions without an appeal path. The campaign demanded that deployment of new technologies respect workplace norms, that workers have a right to be consulted and included, and that governments act against algorithmic systems deployed without worker consent. The Workers' Memorial Day statement crystallized a campaigning frame that circulates widely in labor movement communications: AI as a management weapon is the adversarial deployment mode the movement must contest.

**ITUC AI governance mandate and report launch.** In October 2025, Triangle [launched the ITUC's report](https://www.ituc-csi.org/Artificial-Intelligence-Workers-unions-must-shape-deployment-and-regulation) "Artificial Intelligence: What are the implications for trade unions?" with the statement: "The rapid development and deployment of AI must be accompanied by sufficient regulation, with workers included in this process." The report brought together research on AI's effects on job quality, algorithmic management, social protection, and the environment, with a central finding that AI is accelerating work intensification and fragmentation without accountability. Triangle's launch statement set the political frame for the ITUC's regulatory demands: worker inclusion is not a feature to be added to AI governance but a condition of legitimacy.

**World Economic Forum and intergovernmental engagement.** At the 2025 World Economic Forum in Davos, Triangle appeared in the "Who Benefits from Augmentation?" panel as the labor movement's representative, arguing that workers need to be part of the design process of AI and that AI must be regulated — characterized [in coverage](https://tradeunionscurator.substack.com/p/why-is-the-ituc-secretary-general) as the most tech-cautious voice in the session. His Davos agenda encompassed advocating for universal social protections for informal and gig economy workers, pushing corporate accountability through supply chain transparency, and advancing ethical AI deployment. At the Paris AI Action Summit in February 2025, the ITUC under his leadership issued a statement that ["trade union rights are key to technological transformation"](https://www.scoop.co.nz/stories/WO2502/S00146/ai-action-summit-trade-union-rights-are-key-to-technological-transformation.htm), demanding that AI governance frameworks at every level include enforceable worker participation rights rather than voluntary social dialogue commitments.

**ILO platform work standard-setting.** In the ILO's tripartite structure — governments, employers, and workers' representatives — Triangle coordinates the workers' group through ITUC affiliates, bringing national bargaining experiences into global standard-setting. The ITUC's campaign for binding ILO standards on platform work [succeeded on 3 July 2025](https://www.ituc-csi.org/global-win-for-workers-ilo-agrees), when the International Labour Conference adopted a resolution committing to a Convention and Recommendation on platform work; [ILO Convention 193](https://www.industriall-union.org/workers-rights-advance-2026-international-labour-conference/), adopted 12 June 2026, became the first legally binding international treaty explicitly entitling platform workers to transparency about automated systems affecting their working conditions — a statutory constraint on algorithmic management Triangle had campaigned for as the movement's most concrete AI governance win.

**The algorithmic management critique as organizing frame.** Triangle's public output consistently returns to a specific diagnosis: that algorithmic management by digital platforms and employers treats workers as inputs in a profit-driven system, stripping them of consultation rights and human review. This frame, articulated in his [ILO interview](https://www.ilo.org/resource/news/2025-what-are-challenges-and-opportunities-trade-union-movement), positions AI governance not as a technology ethics question but as a labor rights question — the question of who controls the systems that govern workers' schedules, pay, evaluations, and dismissals. The practical implication is that collective bargaining, not legislation alone, is the primary accountability mechanism: AI deployed in a unionized workplace can be regulated by contract, whereas legislation depends on enforcement. This theory of change — that worker-organized AI governance is more durable than regulatory AI governance alone — is Triangle's distinctive contribution to the movement's strategic vocabulary.

## Why this is a Voice entry

A Voice entry is created here, rather than additional structure on the [Person entry](../persons/person-luc-triangle.md), because Triangle's public output since taking the ITUC leadership has established an identifiable personal voice in global AI governance deliberations distinct from the ITUC as an institution. The corpus tracks Voices when a person's public output is itself worth tracking as a movement resource: Triangle's named statements have been the labor movement's most-cited expression of AI governance demands at WEF, the Paris Summit, and ILO. His frame — "AI deployed not as a tool for progress but as a weapon against workers" — and his campaign for binding international standards on algorithmic management have given the global labor movement a public AI governance vocabulary that would otherwise be distributed across many unnamed institutional statements.
