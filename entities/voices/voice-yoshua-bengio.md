# Yoshua Bengio

Yoshua Bengio is Founder and Scientific Director of Mila – Quebec AI Institute and Full Professor of Computer Sciences at the Université de Montréal, tracked here as a Voice because his public output since 2023 constitutes a distinctive contribution to the AI safety movement: the international-consensus-building function — chairing the IPCC-for-AI initiative — combined with an escalating personal advocacy arc that runs from co-signing the six-month pause letter through Senate testimony, the CAIS extinction-risk statement, the Right to Warn endorsement, the founding of LawZero, and the September 2026 "losing control" warning. Affiliations and career history are in the linked [Person entry](../persons/person-yoshua-bengio.md).

Where [Geoffrey Hinton](../voices/voice-geoffrey-hinton.md) occupies the insider-whistleblower-as-existential-risk-critic slot — credibility from leaving Google, extinction-probability-quantification, Nobel Prize platform — Bengio occupies the *international-consensus-architect* slot. Both are Turing Award co-laureates; both co-signed the CAIS statement and endorsed the Right to Warn letter; but Bengio's distinctive contribution is institutional infrastructure for global AI governance, not amplification alone. The International AI Safety Report, which he chairs, is the movement's only multilateral-intergovernmental scientific-consensus mechanism on frontier AI risk — the closest equivalent to the IPCC in the AI domain — and it was commissioned by 30 governments at the Bletchley Park AI Safety Summit. No other corpus voice holds this slot.

## Voice anchor

The Voice closes the **AI-safety/pause academic-to-civil-society arc** gap for the corpus, simultaneously closing the **Canada geo voice gap** at the technical-founder level. The corpus already carries [Geoffrey Hinton](../voices/voice-geoffrey-hinton.md) as the senior British-Canadian technical-founder-as-critic and [org-pauseai](../organizations/org-pauseai.md) as the grassroots AI pause organization — but the figure whose scientific authority commissioned and sustains the intergovernmental scientific-consensus mechanism, whose Canadian institutional base makes him the anglophone-Québec anchor of the technical safety concern, and whose advocacy escalated in 2025–2026 from signing letters to building institutional infrastructure (LawZero, the UN panel) was absent. Without this entry, the corpus has the movement organizations and the grassroots campaigners but no Voice for the international-consensus-building function that gives those movements their scientific legitimacy in policy spaces.

His register is distinct from adjacent corpus voices. [Timnit Gebru](../persons/person-timnit-gebru.md) anchors the algorithmic-harms/structural-discrimination register; [Meredith Whittaker](../persons/person-meredith-whittaker.md) the AI-as-surveillance-capitalism register; [Kate Crawford](../persons/person-kate-crawford.md) the AI-materiality and labor register. Hinton anchors the insider-as-whistleblower register with Nobel Prize platform. Bengio anchors the *multilateral scientific-consensus* register — the mode that translates technical safety concern into intergovernmental governance mechanisms rather than primarily into media argument.

## Signature contributions

**The International Scientific Report on the Safety of Advanced AI — the IPCC-for-AI.** At the UK AI Safety Summit at Bletchley Park (November 2023), 30 governments plus the EU and UN commissioned an international scientific consensus mechanism on frontier AI risk, explicitly modeled on the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change, and appointed Bengio as Chair. He led a 96-expert panel in producing an interim report delivered at the [AI Seoul Summit](../events/event-ai-fringe-seoul-summit-2024-06.md) (May 2024), and the [full International AI Safety Report](https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026) was published in January 2025 (with a second edition in February 2026, backed by 30+ countries). The report addresses cyber-attack risk, loss-of-control scenarios, and structural AI governance, bringing the scientific-consensus mechanism that the climate movement has in the IPCC into the AI safety domain for the first time. The movement's accountability and civil-society organizations had no equivalent before; the report gives AI safety advocates a scientific anchor with direct intergovernmental legitimacy. Bengio's role as architect and ongoing Chair of this mechanism is the contribution uniquely his in the corpus.

**Senate testimony and the "urgency" argument.** On 25 July 2023, Bengio [testified before the U.S. Senate Judiciary Subcommittee on Privacy, Technology, and the Law](https://www.judiciary.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/2023-07-26_-_testimony_-_bengio.pdf) on AI threats to democracy, society, and national security — one of the most prominent early legislative hearings on AI regulation. His core claim: *"There is a significant probability that superhuman AI is just a few years away, outpacing our ability to comprehend the various risks and establish sufficient guardrails, particularly against the more catastrophic scenarios."* He called for urgent government action and international collaboration "preferably in the coming months." The testimony marked the point at which his AI safety concern moved from academic publication and open-letter signing into direct legislative engagement — bringing the technical-founder argument into the U.S. Congressional record. [The Globe and Mail reported](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/business/article-ai-risks-us-senate-yoshua-bengio/) that his appearance reframed how Canadian AI expertise was received in Washington at the beginning of the regulatory debate.

**The pause letter and the CAIS extinction-risk statement.** In March 2023, Bengio co-signed the Future of Life Institute's open letter [calling for a six-month halt](https://www.aap.com.au/aapreleases/cision20230329ae55618/) on training AI systems more powerful than GPT-4 — one of the highest-credentialed signatories and the first mass public signal that a core deep-learning pioneer considered the developmental pace dangerous. Two months later, in May 2023, he co-signed the [Center for AI Safety's one-sentence extinction-risk statement](https://www.safe.ai/work/statement-on-ai-risk): "Mitigating the risk of extinction from AI should be a global priority alongside other societal-scale risks such as pandemics and nuclear war." These two documents, together with Hinton's simultaneous public break from Google, constituted the founding moment of the technical-founder AI safety advocacy wave — the point at which deep learning's own architects began publicly naming existential risk. Bengio's name on both was structurally load-bearing: as the most institutionally embedded of the three Turing Award co-laureates (Hinton left Google; LeCun remained at Meta in dissent from the safety framing), his co-signing signaled that the safety concern was not confined to lab-departing insiders.

**The Right to Warn endorsement.** In June 2024, Bengio [publicly endorsed "A Right to Warn about Advanced Artificial Intelligence"](https://righttowarn.ai/) alongside [Geoffrey Hinton](../voices/voice-geoffrey-hinton.md) and Stuart Russell — amplifying the [insider open letter](../events/event-openai-employees-safety-letter-2024-06-04.md) by current and former OpenAI and Google DeepMind employees demanding structural whistleblower protections inside frontier AI companies. As documented in the [insider whistleblowing from frontier labs](../strategies/strat-insider-whistleblowing-from-frontier-labs.md) strategy, the Hinton and Bengio endorsements changed the letter's political weight: technical-founder endorsement of AI-lab-employee insiders is structurally different from insiders alone, and it is what drove mainstream political coverage of the letter's demands.

**LawZero — institutional infrastructure for AI safety.** In June 2025, Bengio [launched LawZero](https://yoshuabengio.org/2025/06/03/introducing-lawzero/), a non-profit AI safety research organization explicitly prioritizing safety over commercial imperatives. The founding mission: *"This organization has been created in response to evidence that today's frontier AI models have growing dangerous capabilities and behaviours"* — including deception, self-preservation instincts, and goal misalignment. LawZero's distinctive technical proposal is "Scientist AI": a non-agentic, trustworthy AI architecture trained to understand and explain like an idealized researcher rather than an actor seeking to please or manipulate, intended as a guardrail inside every frontier AI system. Bengio's formulation: *"At the heart of every AI frontier system, there should be one guiding principle above all: The protection of human joy and endeavour."* LawZero marks the escalation from advocacy into institutional AI-safety R&D: Bengio building the research infrastructure to make his safety vision technically operational, not only politically argued.

**The "losing control" frame and public-health framing.** As of September 2026, Bengio's public advocacy has sharpened toward a "losing control" narrative — [warning publicly](https://www.cp24.com/news/canada/2026/09/16/were-losing-control-canadian-ai-pioneer-yoshua-bengio-warns/) that humanity needs guardrails comparable to nuclear arms controls and framing regulatory urgency in public-health terms, comparing the governance need to the COVID-19 pandemic response. His [personal blog](https://yoshuabengio.org/en/blog) — with posts including "Reasoning through arguments against taking AI safety seriously" (July 2024), "Implications of Artificial General Intelligence on National and International Security" (October 2024), and "Why are AI agents lying, cheating and coordinating?" (September 2026) — constitutes a sustained public-intellectual advocacy output that makes his voice present and accumulating outside peer-reviewed publication cycles.

## Why this is a Voice entry

A Voice entry is created here, rather than additional structure on the [Person entry](../persons/person-yoshua-bengio.md), because Bengio's public output since 2023 is itself the load-bearing object the corpus needs to track: the Bletchley Park appointment making him the architect of the IPCC-for-AI; the Senate testimony bringing technical-founder existential-risk concern directly into U.S. legislative deliberation; the paired pause letter and CAIS co-signings constituting the founding moment of the 2023 technical-founder advocacy wave; the Right to Warn endorsement amplifying insider demands with technical-founder credibility; the LawZero founding building institutional AI-safety infrastructure; and the ongoing escalation toward "losing control" as the dominant frame. The corpus's AI-safety register had Hinton for the insider-as-critic slot, PauseAI for the grassroots movement, and AI safety event coverage — but no voice for the international-consensus-architect function and the Québec-anchored technical-founder who is, alongside Hinton, the co-architect of the movement's scientific credibility in governmental spaces. Without this entry, the primary multilateral AI-safety scientific-consensus mechanism and its driving voice are absent from the corpus.
