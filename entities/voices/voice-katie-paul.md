# Katie Paul

Katie Paul is Director of the [Tech Transparency Project](../organizations/org-tech-transparency-project.md) and the investigative researcher who leads TTP's AI-industry political-capture work, tracked here as a Voice because her investigative output on AI political money and AI harms is the corpus's anchor for this accountability-journalism function: a body of original FOIA-driven research documenting how AI-industry principals (Eric Schmidt, Palantir) have used government advisory positions and philanthropic structures to steer AI policy and procurement toward private financial interests, alongside a running investigation of concrete AI harms platforms systematically fail to prevent. Affiliations and career history are in the linked [Person entry](../persons/person-katie-paul.md).

The Voice entry closes the **AI-industry political-capture exposure** gap for the corpus (V4 G7.10). TTP is in the corpus as the primary watchdog organization; Paul is the named individual whose investigative authorship and public profile — media appearances, recognition record, congressional citations — make her the movement's most visible investigative voice on AI political money. Without this entry, the corpus has the org but no individual Voice anchoring the accountability-journalism function on AI political capture.

## Voice anchor

Paul's path to tech accountability is distinctive: as an anthropologist researching the Middle East post-Arab Spring, she observed Facebook enabling a large-scale black market for looted antiquities — traffickers in conflict zones photographing and selling cultural artifacts through Facebook groups. She co-founded the Antiquities Trafficking and Heritage Anthropology Research (ATHAR) Project, using open-source social media research to document and report the trade to law enforcement. The ethnographic methodology she developed — treating platforms as field sites for criminal networks — carried directly into TTP's approach, which relies on FOIA litigation, tax filings, SEC records, and open-source research rather than leaked documents or insider access. The result is a distinct register in accountability journalism: reproducible, document-anchored investigation.

She joined TTP in 2020 as Director, extending the FOIA-driven methodology that had anchored TTP's original Google revolving-door work into a sustained investigation of AI-industry political capture and concrete AI harms — making the AI conflict-of-interest question the center of TTP's AI portfolio.

## AI political-capture investigations

The anchor of Paul's AI work is TTP's investigation series documenting how AI-industry principals leverage government positions, defense advisory roles, and philanthropic structures to steer AI policy toward private financial interests — the corpus's only systematic accountability-journalism documentation of this pattern.

**The Eric Schmidt / NSCAI series.** TTP's [April 2024 investigation](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/eric-schmidt-cozies-up-to-chinas-ai-industry-while) used FOIA-obtained emails, tax filings, and SEC records to document Schmidt's $17 million investment in Gaoling Feeder Ltd — a fund managed by Hillhouse Capital with extensive holdings in Chinese AI companies developing surveillance and facial-recognition technology — while Schmidt simultaneously chaired the National Security Commission on Artificial Intelligence and publicly warned Congress of China's AI threat. The FOIA record showed Schmidt Futures staff asking NSCAI government employees to arrange personal meetings with Chinese AI industry figures during a November 2019 Schmidt trip, raising potential violations of Special Government Employee ethics rules prohibiting use of official time for private purposes.

A companion investigation, ["Eric Schmidt's Expanding Influence Apparatus"](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/eric-schmidts-expanding-influence-apparatus) (December 2022), documented Schmidt's placement of at least 24 fellows in key federal positions — spanning the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy, the FTC, OMB, GSA, and the Departments of Defense, Energy, and Commerce — via the Federation of American Scientists Day One Project and the Intergovernmental Personnel Act Mobility Program. The positions tracked Schmidt's private AI investment portfolio, creating conditions for conflicts of interest across agencies controlling AI research funding, procurement, and regulatory oversight.

**Palantir's influence operation.** ["Inside Palantir's Expanding Influence Operation"](https://www.techtransparencyproject.org/articles/inside-palantirs-expanding-influence-operation) traced the revolving door between Palantir and the Pentagon's Chief Digital and Artificial Intelligence Office — the unit controlling military AI procurement — documenting how former government officials placed at Palantir reinforce a self-serving narrative that the China AI threat requires increased Pentagon purchases from private AI contractors. Palantir's lobbying spending grew from $1 million in 2016 to approximately $5.8 million in 2024, while the company established the Palantir Foundation for Defense Policy and International Affairs in 2023 to fund research and conferences aligned with its commercial AI-procurement interests.

## AI harms investigations

Beyond political-capture work, Paul has led TTP's investigation of AI harms — areas where platform self-governance has failed and TTP's research has driven regulatory and legislative action.

**Nudify app investigation.** A 2025–2026 investigation identified 38 apps in the Apple App Store and Google Play that enable the creation of AI-generated non-consensual nude images — with approximately 483 million combined downloads and $122 million in revenue. Paul told CNBC that the apps "were definitely designed for non-consensual sexualization of people." Senator Jon Ossoff cited TTP's research in pressing Apple and Google on deepfake nudification at a congressional hearing; multiple states launched their own scrutiny of the apps' availability on major platforms.

**AI-generated CSAM in platform advertising.** In 2026, TTP documented Meta running hundreds of paid ads containing AI-generated child sexual abuse material, generating coverage in the BBC, Wired, Bloomberg, and the New York Times, and prompting India's National Commission for Protection of Child Rights to summon Meta executives. The investigation produced a cease-and-desist letter from the San Francisco City Attorney.

## Recognition and reach

TTP has produced 207+ published reports generating more than 1,224 instances of media coverage in the New York Times, Washington Post, Wall Street Journal, and Bloomberg News. Paul's recognition record spans: Fast Company Most Creative People in Business (2021), Newsweek America's Greatest Disruptors (2021), InStyle Badass Women (2022), and Washingtonian DC Tech Titans in both 2025 and 2026 — consecutive-year recognition in the latter category. Platform policy changes at Meta and YouTube have been documented as downstream outcomes of TTP research. Congressional uptake runs through Senate Judiciary, Intelligence, and Commerce committees; Senator Elizabeth Warren cited TTP research in questions to the Department of Defense about former Google CEO conflicts of interest on defense advisory boards; TTP's FOIA archive of 43,000+ pages of government-industry correspondence is the evidentiary resource underlying much of this legislative engagement.
