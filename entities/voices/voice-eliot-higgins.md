# Eliot Higgins

Eliot Higgins is tracked here as a Voice — in addition to the [Bellingcat](../organizations/org-bellingcat.md) organization entry — because his personal public output articulates the democratic philosophy that frames Bellingcat's investigations: open-source intelligence as a tool any citizen can use for accountability, not the exclusive province of state agencies or legacy newsrooms. The [org entry](../organizations/org-bellingcat.md) captures Bellingcat's institutional investigations; this Voice entry carries the "intelligence agency for the people" intellectual framework — the argument that OSINT methodology, extended to AI-generated synthetic media, is civil society's own instrument for holding accountable the anonymous operators of deepfake platforms and the governments that rely on AI-generated disinformation. Affiliation history and biography are in the linked [Person entry](../persons/person-eliot-higgins.md).

## "Intelligence agency for the people"

[*We Are Bellingcat: An Intelligence Agency for the People*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliot_Higgins) (Bloomsbury, 2021) is Higgins's foundational text. The book documents Bellingcat's investigations — the MH17 Buk identification, the Salisbury GRU agents, the Navalny FSB surveillance team — not primarily as conflict-verification case studies but as demonstrations that open-source methods available to any citizen can surpass the investigative capacity of professional intelligence services. The "intelligence agency for the people" framing is the book's organizing proposition: that satellite imagery, corporate registries, financial records, and social media archives in the public domain can be assembled by anyone to hold governments and corporations accountable, without institutional access. This is precisely the methodology Bellingcat later applied to the AI domain — tracing anonymous deepfake-platform operators through data-breach credential correlation, cryptocurrency tracking, and corporate-registry tracing, the same combination used to identify Russian intelligence officers in the Salisbury case.

The book and its methodology have been taken up in journalism training and civil society capacity-building contexts. Bellingcat's training workshops extend the approach to journalists, lawyers, human rights investigators, and civic-tech workers; [Utrecht University hosts a joint investigative hub](https://piji.com.au/blog/bellingcat-open-source-journalism/) where students conduct OSINT investigations with Bellingcat support. In September 2024, Bellingcat published the [Online Investigations Toolkit](https://www.bellingcat.com/resources/2024/09/24/bellingcat-online-investigations-toolkit/) — a free, collaboratively maintained directory of open-source investigation tools — explicitly positioned as shared infrastructure for the investigative journalism and human-rights communities globally.

## Deepfakes as AI accountability

Since the rise of generative AI, Higgins's public commentary — through interviews, his social media presence ([@EliotHiggins](https://twitter.com/EliotHiggins)), and Bellingcat publications — has consistently framed AI-generated disinformation and deepfake abuse as democratic accountability challenges rather than distant technological risks: how do citizens, journalists, and institutions verify the authenticity of evidence in an environment where any video, photograph, or document can be computationally fabricated? His answer — deploy the same OSINT verification methodology that works on state surveillance — is the continuity between Bellingcat's conflict-investigation origins and its AI-harm investigation strand.

Bellingcat's deepfake-abuse investigations — documented in the [org entry](../organizations/org-bellingcat.md) — are the institutional expression of this framing. The January 2025 Faking It investigation linked MrDeepFakes to AI companies whose applications were subsequently removed from Google and Apple app stores. The May 2025 investigation identified a Canadian pharmacist as a MrDeepFakes administrator and led to the platform's shutdown; [the investigation coincided with Congress passing the Take It Down Act](https://www.bellingcat.com/news/2025/05/07/canadian-pharmacist-linked-to-worlds-most-notorious-deepfake-porn-site/), which criminalized non-consensual deepfake pornography distribution. The December 2025 investigation exposed a Hungarian national as the operator of RefacePorn and DeepfakePorn, both of which became inaccessible following Bellingcat's inquiry. Higgins's public advocacy has contributed to the policy discourse on synthetic-media governance that these investigations helped produce.
