# Julia Angwin

Julia Angwin is the investigative journalist who, over twenty years spanning The Wall Street Journal, [ProPublica](../organizations/org-propublica.md), and [The Markup](../organizations/org-the-markup.md), built algorithmic accountability from an occasional investigative approach into a recognized journalistic discipline with documented policy outcomes — federal enforcement actions, Fair Housing Act litigation, and a shift in how civil society and academic researchers engage with AI systems (see [Person entry](../persons/person-julia-angwin.md)). She is tracked here as a Voice because her sustained public-facing output — the Machine Bias investigations establishing the empirical AI-audit template; *Dragnet Nation* as the mainstream privacy-surveillance reckoning at the pivotal post-Snowden moment; and Proof News as the current-register expansion of the accountability model — collectively engages non-AI publics with evidence that algorithmic systems operate as instruments of discrimination and require the same investigative scrutiny as financial fraud or corporate malfeasance.

## Voice anchor

Angwin anchors the **accountability-journalism investigative register** — the corpus's voice slot for the individual whose investigative journalism created the field's foundational methods and whose documented outcomes make algorithmic-accountability investigations a recognized form of civil-rights enforcement trigger.

The existing corpus voices in the algorithmic-accountability register — [Kate Crawford](../persons/person-kate-crawford.md) (academic research, *Atlas of AI*, AI Now Institute), [Joy Buolamwini](../persons/person-joy-buolamwini.md) (facial-recognition bias, *Gender Shades*, Algorithmic Justice League), [Timnit Gebru](../persons/person-timnit-gebru.md) (LLM critique, DAIR), [Trevor Paglen](../persons/person-trevor-paglen.md) (surveillance-photography and participatory dataset critique) — work primarily through academic publication, coalition organizing, and museum-scale art installations. None occupies the register Angwin anchors: the **newsroom investigator whose datasets and methodology releases create the public evidentiary record that civil rights agencies and litigators subsequently act on**.

Angwin's entry fills a specific gap: the corpus had no Voice carrying the **empirical-audit-to-enforcement pipeline** shape — an investigator who builds the dataset, publishes the methodology, watches federal agencies cite the investigation in enforcement announcements, and then founds successive institutions to repeat the pattern.

## The Wall Street Journal and the privacy investigation template (2000–2013)

At the Wall Street Journal, Angwin [led a privacy investigative team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Angwin) — [2010 Gerald Loeb Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Angwin) for privacy reporting; Pulitzer finalist 2011 for Explanatory Reporting — and was part of the [2003 Pulitzer Prize–winning team for corporate corruption coverage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Angwin). The privacy investigations established her core method: obtain proprietary datasets (tracking cookies, data-broker profiles, ad-targeting parameters), analyze them empirically, publish the data and methodology alongside the story, and translate technical findings for general-news audiences. This method, developed at the Journal, became the template she applied to algorithmic systems at ProPublica and The Markup.

*Dragnet Nation: A Quest for Privacy, Security, and Freedom in a World of Relentless Surveillance* (Times Books, 2014) emerged directly from this investigative period. Published months after the Snowden revelations, it became a New York Times bestseller and was shortlisted for the Financial Times Best Business Book of the Year. Its general-audience framing — Angwin as a journalist trying, and mostly failing, to protect her own digital privacy, with detailed chapters on data brokers, surveillance infrastructure, and opt-out barriers — positioned it as the mainstream entry point to a debate that had previously existed mainly in civil-liberties and technical communities. It engaged non-AI, non-specialist publics with the argument that mass surveillance is not an abstraction but a commercial-industrial system operating against individual users.

## Machine Bias at ProPublica (2014–2018) — the field-founding investigations

[ProPublica](../organizations/org-propublica.md)'s [Machine Bias series](https://www.propublica.org/series/machine-bias) (launched 2015), which Angwin led with Jeff Larson, Surya Mattu, Lauren Kirchner, and Terry Parris Jr., established the template for empirical AI-bias investigation in journalism. Two investigations from the series carried the most consequential movement outcomes.

**The COMPAS analysis (2016).** The May 2016 investigation obtained more than 7,000 individual risk scores from Northpointe in Broward County, Florida, and documented that COMPAS — a recidivism prediction algorithm used in criminal sentencing across the United States — mislabeled Black defendants as future criminals at nearly twice the rate of white defendants. ProPublica [released the full dataset and analysis code publicly on GitHub](https://github.com/propublica/compas-analysis). The investigation's publication triggered years of academic debate on algorithmic fairness — dozens of peer-reviewed papers, an entire sub-field — and the Wisconsin Supreme Court ruling that risk scores [cannot be determinative in sentencing decisions](https://www.propublica.org/article/making-algorithms-accountable). The Pulitzer Board named Angwin, Larson, Mattu, Kirchner, and Parris Jr. [finalists for the 2017 Pulitzer Prize in Explanatory Reporting](https://www.pulitzer.org/finalists/julia-angwin-jeff-larson-surya-mattu-lauren-kirchner-and-terry-parris-jr-propublica).

**Facebook housing-ads discrimination (2016–2018).** The Machine Bias team documented that Facebook's ad delivery algorithms allowed housing advertisers to exclude users by race, religion, national origin, and disability — categories protected under the Fair Housing Act. Follow-up investigations found Facebook had not eliminated the capability after its public promises. In 2019, the Department of Housing and Urban Development filed a Fair Housing Act complaint against Facebook; in June 2022 the Department of Justice reached a settlement with Meta requiring an overhaul of its ad targeting system and court oversight — the most consequential regulatory outcome traced to a single AI-harm investigation in U.S. journalism history. Angwin won the [2018 Gerald Loeb Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Angwin) for this work.

## Co-founding The Markup (2018–2023) — the institutional template

In September 2018, Angwin co-founded [The Markup](../organizations/org-the-markup.md) with Jeff Larson and Sue Gardner, with $20 million in anchor funding from Craig Newmark. The institutional design translated her investigative method into organizational policy: every investigation published with its full methodology, dataset, and analysis code so that any reader could verify or refute the conclusions. The Citizen Browser — a paid-panel browser extension producing statistically valid samples of algorithmic feeds — and Blacklight — a public website-privacy inspector — were both extensions of the "make the algorithm visible as a public instrument" principle. Angwin [departed The Markup in February 2023](https://talkingbiznews.com/media-news/the-markup-founder-angwin-departing-tech-news-site/). The Markup joined CalMatters in April 2024.

## Proof News and the expanded model (2024–present)

In [March 2024, Angwin founded Proof News](https://www.niemanlab.org/2024/03/proof-news-is-julia-angwins-attempt-to-bring-the-scientific-method-to-investigative-journalism/), a nonprofit journalism studio described as "an attempt to bring the scientific method to investigative journalism." Proof News extends the empirical-accountability methodology beyond technology to climate change and corporate accountability — applying the same dataset-build, methodology-publish, and outcome-track discipline to new domains. In 2026, she was named founding director of a new independent media initiative at the Shorenstein Center on Media, Politics and Public Policy at Harvard Kennedy School.

*On Courage: How to be a Dissident in an Age of Fear* (HarperCollins, 2026) extends the public-engagement register into the political and ethical dimensions of investigative work.

## Why this is a Voice entry

A Voice entry is warranted because Angwin's public-facing output is itself load-bearing for the corpus: the Machine Bias investigations are the foundational empirical documents the algorithmic-accountability movement acts on; *Dragnet Nation* is the general-audience surveillance book at the pivotal moment when mass digital surveillance entered mainstream political debate; Proof News is the current-register institution extending the methodology. Her output engages non-AI, non-specialist publics — *Dragnet Nation* readers, mainstream-news audiences for the COMPAS and Facebook investigations, Proof News readers — with evidence-based arguments that algorithmic systems require public accountability. The movement connection is not aspirational but documented: federal enforcement actions cited her investigations; civil rights litigators drew on her datasets; academic researchers built a sub-field on her published COMPAS analysis. The corpus's accountability-journalism voices had no entry anchoring the empirical-audit-to-enforcement pipeline that defines Angwin's practice.
