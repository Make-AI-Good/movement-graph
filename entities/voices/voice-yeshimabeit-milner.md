# Yeshimabeit Milner

Yeshimabeit Milner is the founder and CEO of [Data for Black Lives](../organizations/org-data-for-black-lives.md), which she co-founded with Lucas Mason-Brown in November 2017 to use data science as a tool for racial justice and to contest algorithmic systems that harm Black communities. She is tracked here as a Voice because her public output — the term "Data Weapon," the "Abolish Big Data" pamphlet, a Demos paper on data capitalism and algorithmic racism, and a body of keynotes across academic and movement venues — constitutes the practitioner-intellectual argument for applying the full abolitionist tradition to algorithmic systems: not incremental improvement, but dismantlement and the creation of alternatives centered on community self-determination. Affiliation history and career detail are in the linked [Person entry](../persons/person-yeshimabeit-milner.md).

## Voice anchor

The Voice closes the **US data-justice abolition** slot. The corpus carried [Data for Black Lives](../organizations/org-data-for-black-lives.md) as the organizational anchor for this slot — a movement of over 20,000 scientists, activists, and organizers using data science to contest algorithmic power — but had no Voice carrying the founder's intellectual framings, which circulate independently of D4BL and have shaped how the broader data-justice movement understands both the problem and the alternative. Two aspects of the slot are most clearly missing without a Milner Voice entry: the theoretical argument that data capitalism is structurally rooted in chattel slavery economics (not merely failing to live up to its own technical standards), and the abolitionist demand for community self-determination as the positive alternative rather than incremental reform of surveillance infrastructure.

The register is distinct from adjacent corpus voices. Where [Joy Buolamwini](../voices/voice-joy-buolamwini.md) operates through technical auditing and artistic advocacy, and [Safiya Umoja Noble](../voices/voice-safiya-noble.md) through academic analysis of algorithmic oppression, Milner's voice is primarily organizer-intellectual: she anchors technical argument in abolitionist movement theory, campaigns directly against specific surveillance systems, and appears at AI-fairness academic conferences to challenge the field's governing framework from an organizing rather than research position. The combination of technical engagement, organizing infrastructure, and abolitionist intellectual tradition is the distinctive configuration.

## Signature framings

**"Data Weapon" and the naming function.** The term "Data Weapon," coined by Milner, names surveillance technologies — gang databases, predictive risk scores, facial recognition deployed at protests, social media monitoring of organizers — not as flawed tools requiring accuracy improvements but as deliberate instruments of racial control. The [#NoMoreDataWeapons campaign](https://d4bl.org/campaigns/2-no-more-data-weapons), launched in 2021, built this naming function into specific policy demands: ending investment in data weapons, prohibiting new ones, and removing the cover of legitimacy from existing ones. The naming is itself a strategy — calling these systems weapons rather than tools insists that the operative question is not "does facial recognition work accurately?" but "why is any level of accuracy acceptable for a weapon deployed against our communities?"

**Abolition as the alternative.** The ["Abolish Big Data" pamphlet](https://d4bl.org/campaigns/47-abolish-big-data) (first distributed at the second Data for Black Lives conference, January 2019; co-released with the [Data & Society Research Institute](../organizations/org-data-and-society.md); [presented at the United Nations in Geneva](https://d4bl.org/campaigns/47-abolish-big-data) in March 2019) frames Big Data not as a collection of technologies but as "an ideological regime" demanding redistribution rather than regulation. The abolitionist frame, drawn from the prison abolition tradition, holds that reform of surveillance infrastructure leaves its coercive function intact: a less inaccurate facial recognition system is still a weapon. The positive demand is redistribution of data power to affected communities and the creation of data systems governed by those they affect — an argument that D4BL operationalizes through The Switchboard (a community data-sharing platform) and the Movement Scientists Program.

**Data capitalism rooted in chattel slavery.** "Data Capitalism and Algorithmic Racism," [co-authored with Amy Traub and published by Demos in May 2021](https://www.demos.org/research/data-capitalism-and-algorithmic-racism), is Milner's most systematic structural argument. The paper defines data capitalism as "an economic model built on the extraction and commodification of data" and argues that algorithmic racism is "a feature, not a bug" — tracing its lineage through chattel slavery and white supremacy to show that contemporary surveillance and algorithmic sorting continue historical patterns of racial extraction and control. The paper names "extreme informational asymmetry" as the structural condition: citizens are surveilled by systems whose algorithms are secret and unaccountable, concentrating power in precisely the way slavery concentrated the extraction of value. The argument repositions AI fairness debates: the technical accuracy problem is not a deviation from a basically sound system but a particular form of a structurally racist economic model that fair algorithms alone cannot fix.

**"No Humans Involved."** In her [September 2022 speech](https://d4bl.org/dispatch), Milner introduced her most philosophically distinctive framing: "The battle for the soul of AI is not about preventing tech from becoming human but about preventing AI from being used to define who is human and who is not." The title "No Humans Involved" references the designation historically used by the Los Angeles Police Department — code NHI — when categorizing crimes whose victims were unhoused people or people of color, treating those victims as outside the bounds of legal personhood. Milner's argument is that AI systems function in the same way: predictive policing algorithms, benefits-denial systems, facial recognition deployed against protesters, and automated hiring screens do not merely fail to see Black and Brown people accurately — they systematically define their subjects as outside the class of persons whose interests warrant protection. This frames the AI danger not as a distant risk of misaligned superintelligence but as a present practice of algorithmic dehumanization.

**"Apartheid Is an Algorithm."** Milner's [October 2023 keynote](https://d4bl.org/dispatch) at the Milky Way Tech Summit (Milwaukee) extended the structural argument: algorithmic systems do not produce incidental racial disparities but construct and maintain racial ordering through technical means — a function analogous to apartheid's administrative architecture of racial classification and differential entitlement. The framing positions AI governance not as a civil-rights extension problem (applying existing anti-discrimination frameworks to new technologies) but as a structural question about whether algorithmic administration is a permissible form of racial domination.

## Public channels

Milner's public output flows through four channels.

**Foundational texts and papers.** "Abolish Big Data" (2019) and ["Data Capitalism and Algorithmic Racism"](https://www.demos.org/research/data-capitalism-and-algorithmic-racism) (Demos, 2021, with Amy Traub) are the two anchor documents through which Milner's intellectual framework circulates in academic, philanthropic, and movement contexts. "Abolish Big Data" is the practitioner-facing manifesto; the Demos paper is the structural-economic diagnosis that undergirds it. Both are widely assigned in data-ethics and racial-justice curricula and cited in the data-justice literature beyond D4BL's own network.

**Academic and computing-research keynotes.** Milner's [FAccT 2021 keynote](https://d4bl.org/videos/56-yeshimabeit-milner-f-acc-t-2021-keynote) at the premier algorithmic accountability academic conference called on AI-fairness researchers to move beyond "fairness, accountability and transparency" and demand "justice, power and self-determination." Her appearance as closing keynote at [UbiComp/ISWC 2024](https://www.ubicomp.org/ubicomp-iswc-2024/closing-keynote-october-12-yeshimabeit-yeshi-milner/) (Cancun, October 2024) marks her continuing presence in the computing-systems research community as its most prominent abolitionist interlocutor.

**Policy engagement.** In [August 2023, Milner delivered remarks at the White House Roundtable on Protecting Americans from Harmful Data Broker Practices](https://d4bl.org/dispatch), calling for the abolition of FICO credit scores — extending the data-weapons analysis to financial infrastructure where FICO scores embed historical redlining into algorithmic credit allocation. The engagement marked D4BL's reach into federal financial-policy arenas alongside its ongoing surveillance and criminal-justice campaigns.

**Direct engagement with AI existential-risk discourse.** Milner has publicly debated AI existential-risk scholars about whether AI constitutes an existential threat, representing the community-harm-now position: the AI dangers that are urgent and present are not hypothetical extinction risks but operational algorithmic systems that right now deny people housing, benefits, and freedom based on racialized predictions. This positions her voice as the substantive organizing-tradition alternative to long-termist AI safety in the movement ecology.

## Why this is a Voice entry

A Voice entry is created here, rather than additional structure on the [Person entry](../persons/person-yeshimabeit-milner.md), because Milner's named public output is independently load-bearing for the corpus. The term "Data Weapon" functions as movement vocabulary beyond any single D4BL campaign. "Abolish Big Data" is the foundational document of the data-justice abolition tradition and is assigned and cited across data-ethics and racial-justice curricula well outside the D4BL network. "Data Capitalism and Algorithmic Racism" (Demos, 2021) was co-published with a major US think tank as a policy document and constitutes the structural economic diagnosis undergirding the field's abolitionist turn. The "No Humans Involved" framing — its inversion of standard AI safety concern, its NHI reference — is the most intellectually distinctive single contribution in her body of work, circulating in academic and movement contexts as a frame for naming algorithmic dehumanization. The corpus carried [Data for Black Lives](../organizations/org-data-for-black-lives.md) as the organizational anchor for the US data-justice abolition slot; this entry closes the asymmetry by carrying the Voice whose intellectual framings constitute the theory of change behind D4BL's campaigns and whose speeches at academic and policy venues have made the abolitionist data-justice argument legible to audiences outside the racial-justice movement.
