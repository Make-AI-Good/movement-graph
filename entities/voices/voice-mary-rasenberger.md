# Mary Rasenberger

Mary Rasenberger is the CEO and leading public spokesperson of [The Authors Guild](../organizations/org-authors-guild.md) — the United States' oldest and largest professional organisation for published writers — and the individual whose public output has most consistently framed the creator-rights sector's position on AI training-data harms for the U.S. Congress, the intellectual-property law community, and the general literary public. She is tracked here as a Voice because that output is itself the load-bearing register the corpus needs to map: her [July 2023 written testimony before the Senate Judiciary IP Subcommittee](https://authorsguild.org/app/uploads/2023/07/AG-Senate-IP-Subcommittee-Testimony-Part-II-AI-Hearing.pdf) proposing collective licensing as the legislative framework for AI training-data compensation; her byline in [*The New York Review of Books*](https://www.nybooks.com/contributors/mary-rasenberger/); her named-speaker engagements at civic literary forums including [The Salisbury Forum](https://www.salisburyforum.org/events/mary-rasenberger-ceo-the-authors-guild) and the [Brooklyn Public Library](https://www.bklynlibrary.org/calendar/ai-copyright-literary-central-library-dweck-20240228); the "identity theft on a grand scale" and ["the artist is a visionary; AI is not"](https://www.managingip.com/article/2a5cz0ug7vhetbdkm13wg/authors-guild-ceo-the-artist-is-a-visionary-ai-is-not) framings that have entered creator-rights advocacy shorthand; and the [ABA IP Section Council](https://authorsguild.org/about/team/staff-directory/mary-rasenberger/), [Copyright Society USA Executive Committee](https://copyrightsociety.org/bio/mary-rasenberger/) advisory, and ALI Restatement of Law, Copyright advisory roles that position her arguments inside the professional community that advises Congress and the courts on IP doctrine. She is recognised as [PW Notables 2025](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/awards-and-prizes/article/99290-pw-noteables-2025-mary-rasenberger.html) and [Managing IP's 50 Most Influential People in IP 2024](https://www.managingip.com/article/2e49x9dxup8ekoyg57p4w/special-projects/50-most-influential-people-in-ip/50-most-influential-people-in-ip-2024-mary-rasenberger) (see [Person entry](../persons/person-mary-rasenberger.md)).

The Voice anchors two corpus registers previously unfilled.

- **The creator-rights CEO-advocate voice anchor.** The corpus has entries for the Guild's class-action litigation, open-letter campaigns, and federal-policy engagement — all of which are named-CEO-led — but no Voice entry tracking the public-output register through which those institutional positions are argued and explained to non-specialist audiences. The CEO-as-advocate shape — where the spokesperson's public framings are themselves the movement object, not merely institutional press statements — is what this Voice maps. The Guild's [Organisation entry](../organizations/org-authors-guild.md) documents the operational tracks; this Voice carries the public argumentation register.
- **The collective-licensing legislative-proposal anchor.** Rasenberger's July 2023 Senate testimony introduced the specific form — a collective management organisation (CMO) licensing training-data rights on behalf of authors at negotiated market rates — that has circulated in the U.S. AI-and-copyright legislative conversation as the creator class's named proposed alternative to applying fair use doctrine at scale. No other in-corpus entity holds this framing; the Voice is its appropriate home.

## Public output and venues

Rasenberger's public-facing work runs through four named channels.

- **Congressional testimony and government record.** The primary formal output is her [written testimony before the Senate Judiciary Committee Intellectual Property Subcommittee](https://authorsguild.org/app/uploads/2023/07/AG-Senate-IP-Subcommittee-Testimony-Part-II-AI-Hearing.pdf), submitted for Part II of the committee's AI hearing in July 2023. The testimony argues that the fair use doctrine is structurally ill-suited for the mass AI training-data use case — where the sheer scale of ingestion defeats per-work market-harm arguments — and proposes as the legislative alternative a voluntary collective-licensing system whereby a CMO would license training-data rights on behalf of registered rights-holders at market rates and distribute royalties to participating authors. Rasenberger is also the named Authors Guild lead on the U.S. Copyright Office's [ex parte record for the 10 May 2024 Authors Guild meeting](https://www.copyright.gov/policy/artificial-intelligence/ex-parte-communications/letters/Authors-Guild-May-10-2024.pdf), where she argues for training-data attribution rights and an AI-output labelling regime.
- **Published writing.** Rasenberger is a named contributor to [*The New York Review of Books*](https://www.nybooks.com/contributors/mary-rasenberger/) — the NYRB byline is the publication register through which her copyright and authors'-rights arguments reach the general literary readership that the Guild's professional base overlaps with. The Authors Guild also publishes her as the named author of [copyright Q&A and AI best-practices resources](https://authorsguild.org/resource/copyright-qa-with-the-authors-guild-ceo/) aimed at the Guild's 17,000+ member authors, which form the member-facing arm of the same public-output register.
- **Civic and literary-forum speaker register.** Rasenberger holds a confirmed named-speaker record at civic literary forums: [The Salisbury Forum](https://www.salisburyforum.org/events/mary-rasenberger-ceo-the-authors-guild) hosted her as featured speaker for "The Future of Books in the Age of AI", and the [Brooklyn Public Library](https://www.bklynlibrary.org/calendar/ai-copyright-literary-central-library-dweck-20240228) hosted her on the "AI & Copyright: Literary Arts in the Age of Machines" panel in February 2024 — both confirmed engagements carrying the CEO-advocate register to the public literary audience. The [Authors Guild's own event programming](https://authorsguild.org/event/ai-and-copyright-literary-arts-in-the-age-of-machines/) runs in parallel, positioning her as the Guild's named intellectual presence at the intersection of AI, copyright, and literary culture.
- **Professional-association advisory register.** Rasenberger's public-voice standing is grounded in three named advisory roles that position her arguments inside the authoritative IP-law professional community: she [sits on the council of the American Bar Association's Intellectual Property Section](https://authorsguild.org/about/team/staff-directory/mary-rasenberger/), serves as [advisor to the Executive Committee of the Copyright Society of the USA](https://copyrightsociety.org/bio/mary-rasenberger/), and advises the [American Law Institute's Restatement of Law, Copyright](https://authorsguild.org/about/team/staff-directory/mary-rasenberger/). She is additionally the founder of Copyright Awareness Week and a member of the advisory board of the Created by Humans licensing platform.

## Signature framings

Three formulations recur across Rasenberger's public output and have circulated beyond the Guild's own channels into the broader creator-rights conversation.

- **"Identity theft on a grand scale."** In the [Authors Guild's 20 September 2023 announcement](https://authorsguild.org/news/ag-and-authors-file-class-action-suit-against-openai/) of the class-action filing against OpenAI, Rasenberger characterised AI training on fiction authors' work without permission as "identity theft on a grand scale" — a framing that positions the training-data claim as a violation of authorial voice and identity rather than an abstract copyright-infringement question. The phrase has circulated in press coverage of the lawsuit and in the Guild's subsequent advocacy.
- **"The artist is a visionary; AI is not."** In a [Managing IP interview](https://www.managingip.com/article/2a5cz0ug7vhetbdkm13wg/authors-guild-ceo-the-artist-is-a-visionary-ai-is-not), Rasenberger articulated the human-vs.-AI creative distinction through which the Guild's position distinguishes authors' work from AI-generated output — the formulation that anchors her public argument that AI output is recognisably derivative and that the substitution threat to authors' livelihoods is therefore real and legally actionable. This is the public-register version of her collective-licensing claim: if AI systems recombine rather than originate, the creators whose work they recombine have a principled claim for compensation.
- **"The future of journalism, literature, and the arts depends on policies that adequately incentivize human creators to continue working."** Stated in the Guild's 2023 Congressional-advocacy engagement, this formulation frames the policy ask not as protection for a narrow professional class but as a systemic condition for sustaining the broader cultural-information ecosystem — the argument that moves the creator-rights claim from an IP-specialists' debate to a public-interest proposition.

## Organisational vehicle

Rasenberger's public output runs through one named organisational vehicle: [The Authors Guild](../organizations/org-authors-guild.md), of which she has been Chief Executive Officer since November 2014. The Guild supplies the platform for all four registers — the Congressional testimony and Copyright Office engagement, the NYRB and member-facing writing, the civic-forum speaker record, and the professional-association advisory positioning — and the legal and policy team (Chief Legal Officer Kevin Amer, Director of Advocacy Umair Kazi) that backs the substantive arguments she deploys publicly. Her individual public framings and the Guild's institutional litigation and advocacy tracks are not separate objects: the Voice is the public-register face of the same institutional position the Organisation entry records from the operational side.
