# Ed Newton-Rex

Ed Newton-Rex is a British composer, technologist, and founder of [Fairly Trained](../organizations/org-fairly-trained.md) — the movement's primary consumer-facing AI certification instrument for creator consent — and the individual whose public output most consistently frames the creator-consent-certification position for technical, music-industry, and general policy audiences. He is tracked here as a Voice because that output is itself the load-bearing register the corpus needs to map: the [founding rationale and consent-vs-fair-use framing](https://ed.newtonrex.com/blog/launching-fairly-trained) that created the category of movement-run AI certification; white papers on AI training-data licensing and commercial research exemptions; opinion pieces in The Guardian, The Times, and The Hollywood Reporter that carry the consent argument outside creator-class specialist circles; the 50,000+ creator open letter and the 1,000+-musician silent album protest [organized in February 2025](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2025/2/25/music-stars-release-silent-album-in-protest-against-uk-ai-copyright-plans) that stand among the largest creator-class public interventions on AI training practices; and [TIME100 AI 2025 recognition](https://time.com/collections/time100-ai-2025/7305844/ed-newton-rex/) placing his work among the year's most consequential AI governance interventions. The [Person entry](../persons/person-ed-newton-rex.md) carries affiliation and biographical structure.

The Voice anchors the corpus's creator-consent certification slot previously unfilled: no in-corpus entity has held the movement's consent-certification framing as a named public-output register. Fairly Trained's [Organisation entry](../organizations/org-fairly-trained.md) documents the certification program's operational structure; this Voice carries the public argumentation and organizing register through which the consent-or-no-consent divide is named, argued, and mobilized.

## Public output register

Newton-Rex's public-facing work runs through four named channels.

- **Policy writing and white papers.** His two principal technical documents — *The Unworkability of a Commercial Research Exception for AI Training* and *The generative AI data licensing market*, [both available through his personal website](https://ed.newtonrex.com/) — argue the economic and structural case for why consent-based licensing is feasible and why commercial-research fair-use exceptions would impose uncompensated costs on creators at scale. The linked initiative [AI Training Statement](https://aitrainingstatement.org/) provides a standardized, opt-in public declaration mechanism for AI developers and researchers to disclose their training-data consent posture.

- **Opinion writing.** Newton-Rex has published opinion pieces in The Guardian, The Times, and The Hollywood Reporter, [listed through his personal website](https://ed.newtonrex.com/), carrying the consent argument to general and entertainment-industry readerships beyond the AI specialist press. His [first-person Music Business Worldwide resignation piece](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/why-just-resigned-from-my-job-generative-ai/) — published at the moment of his departure from Stability AI — established the insider-leaving-on-principle register that his subsequent public writing inherits: he argues from demonstrated willingness to sacrifice professional position, not from the outside.

- **Mobilization campaigns.** Newton-Rex organized two major creator-class public interventions. The [50,000+ signature open letter](https://time.com/collections/time100-ai-2025/7305844/ed-newton-rex/) condemning unlicensed AI training gathered signatories including Thom Yorke, Julianne Moore, James Patterson, and Kevin Bacon — one of the largest coordinated creator-class statements on AI training-data practices. In February 2025, Newton-Rex organized [*Is This What We Want?*](https://www.aljazeera.com/amp/news/2025/2/25/music-stars-release-silent-album-in-protest-against-uk-ai-copyright-plans) — a silent album featuring more than 1,000 musicians, including Kate Bush, Annie Lennox, and Cat Stevens, in response to proposed UK copyright law changes that would have permitted training AI models on any lawfully-accessed material without consent. The twelve silent tracks were recorded across UK studios and empty spaces and combine to carry an embedded message: "The British government must not legalise music theft to benefit AI companies." The protest contributed to sustained public and parliamentary pressure on the UK government's AI copyright consultation.

- **Technical and founding framing.** Newton-Rex's [founding blog post for Fairly Trained](https://ed.newtonrex.com/blog/launching-fairly-trained) established the consent-vs-fair-use binary as the organizing frame for the certification mark: AI training practices either obtain consent from data providers or they do not, and Fairly Trained's certification makes that divide legible at the point of product adoption. This framing — developed over his tenure leading Stability AI's audio team under licensed-data conditions and cemented by his public resignation from that company — is the conceptual architecture the certification mark rests on.

## Signature framings

Three formulations from Newton-Rex's public output have circulated beyond his own channels into creator-rights discourse.

- **"Companies worth billions of dollars are, without permission, training generative AI models on creators' works."** The central consent-equity framing from his [Music Business Worldwide resignation piece](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/why-just-resigned-from-my-job-generative-ai/) — it positions the AI-industry training-data practice as a power-asymmetry problem rather than a legal-doctrine question, placing the grievance at the scale and resource asymmetry rather than the specific technical act.

- **"I can only support generative AI that doesn't exploit creators by training models — which may replace them — on their work without permission."** From the same [resignation piece](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/why-just-resigned-from-my-job-generative-ai/) — the insider-as-principled-leaver construct that grounds Newton-Rex's public positioning. The formulation distinguishes support for AI technology from support for non-consensual AI training practices, making the consent demand a condition of engagement rather than opposition to the technology.

- **"I couldn't live with myself if I stole everyone else's work to compete with them."** [Quoted in TIME100 AI 2025](https://time.com/collections/time100-ai-2025/7305844/ed-newton-rex/) — the personal-stakes form of the consent argument, framing the consent standard as an ethical minimum he applied to his own conduct before requiring it of others. This construction anchors the insider-leaving-on-principle register in moral rather than legal terms.

## Organizational vehicles

Newton-Rex's public output runs through two named organizational instruments. [Fairly Trained](../organizations/org-fairly-trained.md) is his primary vehicle — it supplies the certification mark, the coalition relationships (including [The Authors Guild](../organizations/org-authors-guild.md) and [SAG-AFTRA](../organizations/org-sag-aftra.md)), and the institutional legitimacy for his consent-certification arguments. [AI Training Statement](https://aitrainingstatement.org/) is a second instrument: a standardized public-declaration mechanism through which AI developers and researchers can disclose their consent posture on training data, operating as a directory complement to Fairly Trained's binary certification.

## Why this is a Voice entry

A Voice entry is created here, rather than additional structure on the [Person entry](../persons/person-ed-newton-rex.md), because Newton-Rex's public-facing output is itself worth tracking as a movement object. His resignation from Stability AI produced the first widely-circulated insider-leaving-on-principle statement on AI training-data consent, establishing a public-output register that his subsequent writing, white papers, and organizing campaigns have built on. The consent-vs-fair-use framing he articulated at that moment — and that Fairly Trained's certification mark operationalizes — is the movement's principal instrument for making the training-data consent divide legible at the point of AI product adoption. The voices slice previously carried no creator-consent certification anchor and no Fairly Trained voice anchor; this entry supplies both.
