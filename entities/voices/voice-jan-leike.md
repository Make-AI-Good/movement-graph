# Jan Leike

Jan Leike is a German AI alignment researcher and co-author of the RLHF methodology foundational to modern large language model training. He is tracked here as a Voice because his May 2024 resignation from OpenAI's Superalignment team — accompanied by a public X thread that constitutes among the most direct insider criticisms of a frontier AI lab's safety culture yet made by a senior technical leader — functions as a movement act: primary-source evidence, from someone inside the institution, that the mechanism AI safety advocates had long identified (commercial pressure overriding safety process) was documented to have occurred. See the linked [Person entry](../persons/person-jan-leike.md) for affiliations and career history.

## Voice anchor

The specific function Leike occupies in this corpus is the insider testimony slot: credibility that comes not from independent advocacy but from having led safety work at the world's most prominent AI lab and publicly describing what that work encountered. His X thread on 17 May 2024 carried a direct statement that external advocates had made for years: that commercial product-launch pressure systematically deprioritized safety work. The statement was worth more coming from Leike than from any outside critic because of his technical role — he had co-led InstructGPT and the Superalignment initiative, had built safety capability inside OpenAI from 2021, and had seen from inside what the tradeoffs looked like when capability and safety competed for resources.

Two elements of the thread shaped the movement's public record. First, the direct factual claim: ["safety culture and processes have taken a backseat to shiny products"](https://x.com/janleike/status/1791498184671605209) — a primary-source statement of how resource allocation worked internally, distinct in kind from an external observer's inference. Second, the moral framing addressed to OpenAI employees: "Learn to feel the AGI. Act with the gravitas appropriate for what you're building." The phrase placed the failure as a failure of seriousness about real stakes, not a management disagreement.

The resignation came two days after co-lead Ilya Sutskever's own departure, compounding the signal that the Superalignment team had lost internal viability. OpenAI subsequently [dissolved it](https://fortune.com/2024/05/17/openai-researcher-resigns-safety/). The departure is directly documented in the corpus's [insider whistleblowing from frontier labs](../strategies/strat-insider-whistleblowing-from-frontier-labs.md) strategy entry as a precipitating event behind OpenAI's reversal of its nondisparagement agreements and the [Right to Warn open letter of 4 June 2024](../events/event-openai-employees-safety-letter-2024-06-04.md).

## Technical credibility and RLHF lineage

Leike's insider testimony carries distinctive weight because he is a foundational contributor to the safety-relevant methodology his critique turns on. The 2017 paper "Deep Reinforcement Learning from Human Preferences" (co-authored with Paul Christiano, Dario Amodei, and others at DeepMind) established the empirical groundwork for reinforcement learning from human feedback — the mechanism through which current LLMs are trained to be helpful, harmless, and honest. He led the InstructGPT project at OpenAI in 2022, which brought RLHF to large-scale language models and is the direct technical ancestor of GPT-3.5 and GPT-4. He is therefore not an outsider critiquing AI safety culture: he is someone who built the training methodology that defines the current generation of frontier models, and who then observed from inside how the organisation deploying those models treated safety as a priority.

## Ongoing public output

Leike's public voice continues through two channels after the resignation.

His **Aligned Substack** — "Musings on the Alignment Problem" — carries ongoing technical writing about AI alignment research: approaches to the supervision problem, directions in scalable oversight, and comparisons of alignment strategies. The register is practitioner-facing rather than mass-audience advocacy, shaped toward technically literate readers tracking alignment research direction.

His **current role at Anthropic** as lead of the Alignment Science team gives his ongoing research public significance. His research agenda — scalable oversight, weak-to-strong generalization, and automated alignment research — represents the technical programme that follows from the critique of OpenAI. The implicit public argument the move makes is that meaningful safety-oriented alignment research is possible under the right institutional conditions, and that those conditions require deliberate cultivation of a different kind than he documented at OpenAI.
