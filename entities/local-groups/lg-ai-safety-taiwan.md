# NTU AI Safety

NTU AI Safety (NTUAIS — National Taiwan University AI Safety) is a Taipei-based, student-led AI-safety community group anchored at National Taiwan University whose central activity is a suite of reading-group, workshop, and fellowship programmes designed to build AI-safety and AI-alignment talent in Taiwan and across Asia. [Founded in 2024](https://ntuais.org/team) by Sin-tshong (Shin) Tsu and running with a roster of ten-plus organisers and four faculty/field-builder mentors by 2026, [the group's stated mission](https://ntuais.org/about) is to "help more people in Taiwan learn about AI safety, raise awareness across industry, and discover potential talent" — drawing on Taiwan's particular position in the global AI supply chain (hardware manufacturing, semiconductor design) to make the case for Taiwan-anchored safety contributions. NTUAIS names AICoE (Taiwan AI Center of Excellence), the Taiwan AI Research Association (TARA), and Pathfinder (Kairos Project) as [partner organisations](https://ntuais.org/).

## Founding and structure

[NTUAIS traces its activity](https://ntuais.org/events/) to January 2024, when its first reading group cohort launched as a ten-week introductory programme — the earliest documented group event. Co-founder [Sin-tshong (Shin) Tsu](../persons/person-shin-tsu.md) established the group in 2024; by 2026 it had grown to 50+ members running three concurrent programme tracks simultaneously.

The mentor roster is unusually well-connected for a student-led group. **Tzu-Kit Chan** (Chief of Staff at Atlas Computing; former Executive Director of Stanford AI Alignment / SAIA) [advises 35+ university AI safety groups](https://ntuais.org/team) internationally and is the youngest advisor to Malaysia's National AI Office — providing NTUAIS with a direct connection to the global university-group coordination network Atlas helps run. **Alvin Lau** (Data Team Lead at Travo AI; organiser of EA Taiwan; founder of the Taiwan AIS Group) links the group to the Effective Altruism Taiwan community and to a parallel community-building initiative in Taipei. **Po-Yi Lu** (PhD student at NTU GINM; visiting scholar at Texas A&M; founder of the Taiwan AI Safety Group researching preference alignment and generative models) is an NTU-internal research anchor for the group's alignment track. **Shang-Tse Chen** (Associate Professor at NTU CSIE; head of the [NTU AI Security Lab](https://ntuaislab.github.io/)) connects the group to NTU's formal research infrastructure in adversarial machine learning, security, and fairness in ML models.

Current organisers (2026) include **Leonardo Foo Haw-Yang** (NTU GICE MS student; audio/speech and mechanistic-interpretability research), **Chia-Tai Chang** (NTU CSIE BS; TSMC research; Fall 2026 Pathfinder fellow), **Tzu-Tsen (Christine) Hsieh** (NTU EE MA graduate; AI safety field-building focus), and **David** (independent AI governance researcher specialising in US-China-Taiwan dynamics; GovAI 2026 Summer Fellow; Cambridge MPhil and Fulbright alumnus) — a team combining NTU graduate technical depth with policy expertise specific to Taiwan's geopolitical situation in AI governance.

## Programmes

NTUAIS runs three concurrent reading-group tracks in a typical semester, [documented on its events page](https://ntuais.org/events/):

**Fundamental Reading Group** (semester-long, September–December) — guided discussions covering alignment foundations, safer training methods, evaluations, interpretability, and AI control, drawing from the BlueDot Impact curriculum framework. Designed as the entry track for participants with no prior AI-safety background.

**Policy Reading Group** (weekly sessions, September–December) — covers AI governance, state power, compute governance, the US-China-Taiwan dynamic, institutional design, and international cooperation. The policy track reflects the group's distinctive Taiwan-facing angle: among the documented discussion topics are Taiwan-specific considerations that few other local AI-safety groups address directly.

**Technical Paper Reading Group** (15-week weekly sessions, September–December) — advanced track covering threat models, reward hacking, scheming, mechanistic interpretability, and AI control; designed for participants with ML or technical backgrounds seeking research-depth engagement with the safety literature.

Beyond the semester programme, NTUAIS has run standalone technical workshops: an **ARENA 3.0 Workshop** (August 2, 2025 — two-day hands-on session covering Transformers, Mechanistic Interpretability, RLHF, and Model Evaluation, adapted from the internationally-used ARENA curriculum) and an NTU AI Safety Workshop 2026 carrying forward the same format. The group also ran a [Technical AI Safety Course](https://ntuais.org/events/) in the Spring 2026 semester (March–June) following the BlueDot Impact framework.

## Taiwan's positioning

[NTUAIS's About page](https://ntuais.org/about) frames Taiwan's relevance to AI safety explicitly: the group's vision is to leverage "Taiwan's hardware industry and AI supply chain expertise to strengthen safety efforts domestically and contribute to global AI safety progress" — a claim rooted in Taiwan's dominant position in global semiconductor manufacturing. The Policy Reading Group's curriculum directly operationalises this: topics include compute governance, the US-China-Taiwan geopolitical dynamic, and international cooperation in AI governance — areas where Taiwan's semiconductor supply-chain role makes locally-anchored analysis distinctively valuable. Organiser David's GovAI 2026 Summer Fellowship focus on US-China-Taiwan dynamics embeds this angle in active research work running alongside the community-building programmes.

The broader Taiwan AI-safety ecosystem that NTUAIS operates inside includes the **Advanced AI Safety Forum** — an annual event [co-organised by the NTU IoX Center and Taiwan AICoE](https://iox.ntu.edu.tw/en/event/b4AZI46XGFwueXy6) convening government, industry, and academic stakeholders on AI risk and governance. A 2026 edition ran on August 28, 2026 at the GIS NTU Convention Center with international speakers including Daniel Remler (Senior Fellow at the Center for a New American Security) alongside financial-sector CTOs and CISOs from Taiwan's major banks and insurers; a preceding 2025 edition ran on December 7, 2025 at the Howard Civil Service International House in Taipei. NTUAIS is a named partner of Taiwan AICoE and operates alongside this institutional layer as the community-building and talent-pipeline counterpart.

## Place in the movement

NTU AI Safety is the corpus's first entry for a Taiwan AI-safety community group and the third East Asia entry in the insider-community pattern — groups whose central activity is reading-group, workshop, and expert-talk programming aimed at building the local practitioner pipeline rather than public-facing campaign or protest work — joining [AI Safety 東京](lg-ai-safety-tokyo.md) in Japan and the [Singapore AI Safety Hub](lg-singapore-ai-safety-hub.md) in Southeast Asia. Where AI Safety 東京 runs a monthly benkyoukai supplemented by an annual research conference (TAIS), NTUAIS runs three concurrent multi-track semester programmes at higher session frequency, a format shaped by its university infrastructure and graduate-student composition. NTUAIS connects to the global EA-adjacent / BlueDot-anchored university group network through its BlueDot curriculum adoption and through mentor Tzu-Kit Chan's Atlas Computing role advising 35+ such groups internationally.

Taiwan's specific contribution to the AI-safety community-group layer is anchored in the semiconductor supply chain — a thread running through the group's stated vision, the Policy Reading Group curriculum, and the GovAI-affiliated organiser's active research focus. NTUAIS is one of the few local-group entries in the corpus where the group's geographic position is itself a substantive research topic, not only a demographic fact.
